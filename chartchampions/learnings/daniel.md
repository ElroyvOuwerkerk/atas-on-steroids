# Daniel's method, from the transcript

A synthesis of Daniel's "crypto to stocks" approach, built from the one captured lesson. Every claim
links to a timestamp. Quotation marks mark his own words; everything else is paraphrase.

Source: `../transcripts/contender-sessions/making-the-move-to-stocks.md` (46:49).

Attribution: Elroy confirmed on 2026-11-16 that this is Daniel. The "Daniel" greeting in the Q&A is
Daniel reading aloud a question addressed to him, not a greeting to someone else. He is a crypto-roots
trader who covers moving into stocks for long-term, high-conviction investing. Only this single lesson
is captured, so this is a one-source read, not a pattern across sessions.

## 1. The idea: pivot from crypto into stocks

Learn stocks so you can pivot when crypto goes choppy, allocate long-term from trading profits, and
diversify into assets with stronger fundamentals (making-the-move-to-stocks [00:00], [02:20]). This is
an investing-leaning approach layered on a crypto-trading background, not an intraday method.

## 2. Tokenized stocks versus traditional brokers

- Tokenized stocks on crypto exchanges trade against stablecoins like altcoins, with no ownership of
  the underlying. They trade 24/7 with no market closure, which suits day and swing trading
  (making-the-move-to-stocks [03:00], [04:30]).
- Traditional brokers give actual share ownership and dividend eligibility and are required for
  long-term investing (making-the-move-to-stocks [04:30]).
- A mixed approach serves both goals. KYC friction is the main barrier when moving from crypto
  (making-the-move-to-stocks [03:00], [09:30]).

## 3. The three barriers, and how he handles them

The three things a crypto trader must adjust to (making-the-move-to-stocks [09:30]):

- Gaps. A long with a 2.5 percent stop can become a 25 percent loss if a weekend gap jumps straight
  through the stop. 24/7 tokenized markets remove this gap-through risk for active traders
  (making-the-move-to-stocks [06:20]). A gap can be measured wick-high to candle-low or wick-high to
  candle-high; confluence decides which boundary to use (making-the-move-to-stocks [12:20]).
- Market closures that distort higher-timeframe levels. His rule for marking levels across a gap: for
  support use the lowest of the closing/opening prices across the gap, for resistance use the highest,
  on dailies, weeklies and monthlies alike (making-the-move-to-stocks [14:10]).
- Fundamentals matter more than in crypto (making-the-move-to-stocks [09:30], [16:30]).

## 4. Selection, entry, exit

- Long only. Avoiding shorting stocks is a long-standing personal rule; he takes only longs aligned
  with strong fundamentals and investment conviction (making-the-move-to-stocks [10:40]).
- Fundamental selection: companies with widely used, essential products, household-name mega-cap tech
  with dominant products and widespread daily usage. Avoid speculative or obscure picks
  (making-the-move-to-stocks [16:30], [39:00]).
- Entry: preset laddered limit orders at well-identified higher-timeframe support zones, which removes
  emotional second-guessing when price arrives and lets him add if price drops further
  (making-the-move-to-stocks [20:00]).
- Tools carry over from crypto: higher-timeframe levels, Fibonacci, fixed-range volume profile, speed
  fans, anchored VWAPs, harmonics, pitchforks, Elliott waves (making-the-move-to-stocks [21:30]).
- Exit: conservative profit-taking, closing roughly 50 percent near major resistances, then re-adding
  on significant pullbacks to keep or grow the position, blending trading with long-term accumulation
  (making-the-move-to-stocks [22:40]). Taking profit near all-time highs is not automatic; it waits for
  a clear multi-signal setup (making-the-move-to-stocks [37:00]).

## 5. The delta-neutral funding-rate strategy

Buy shares on a traditional broker and simultaneously short the equivalent tokenized version on a
crypto exchange. Collect funding payments every eight hours while staying price-neutral. The primary
risk is exchange failure, not price movement, and elevated funding rates on tokenized stocks can
generate meaningful yield (making-the-move-to-stocks [23:30]). The same farming on altcoins is riskier
because of low liquidity and token collapse; it is safer on major assets such as BTC, ETH and SOL
(making-the-move-to-stocks [34:30]).

## 6. Risk stance: no hard stops on conviction holdings

- On high-conviction holdings he gives the position "space to breathe" and avoids a hard stop loss, so
  he is not shaken out during chop (Google and NVIDIA examples) (making-the-move-to-stocks [27:40]). A
  NVIDIA wick down through support stopped out hard-stop traders just before a reversal to new highs
  (making-the-move-to-stocks [29:20]).
- He separates high-conviction holdings (no hard stops) from momentum or cycle plays such as SanDisk,
  where he does use hard stops because of higher valuation risk (making-the-move-to-stocks [30:30]).
- Severe fundamental-driven drops are treated as discounted buying opportunities rather than exit
  signals, as long as conviction in the business is intact (making-the-move-to-stocks [31:30]). On the
  AI-bubble question he weighs downside against the opportunity cost of cash, and notes that a
  long-term accumulator's entry basis gives a cushion (making-the-move-to-stocks [41:00]).

## 7. How this relates to Severin, and the caution

Daniel's investing stance overlaps with Severin's separate-investing-book framework in `severin.md`
section 9 (buy-the-dip on quality mega-caps, laddered limit orders at HTF support, conservative
profit-taking, treating drawdowns as opportunity). Both keep a long-term book apart from active
trading.

The one place they diverge sharply, worth flagging so the two are not conflated: Daniel runs
high-conviction holdings with no hard stop loss. Severin is "very meticulous with my stop loss"
(`severin.md` section 6) and trails by structure on his trades. The no-hard-stop approach is an
investing stance for mega-cap, high-conviction names, where the plan is to average down and the risk
is sized for that. It is not a trading rule, and it carries real ruin risk if the conviction is wrong
or the position is oversized. It should not be read as licence to drop stops on an active trade. This
also sits against Elroy's own standing rule (`../../docs/decisions.md`, 2026-11-16: the stop advances
on reclaim, no script moves it), which is a trade-management rule, not an investment-holding one.

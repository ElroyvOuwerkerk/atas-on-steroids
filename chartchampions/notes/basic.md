## 1. Getting Started — risk management — `how-to-get-started-day-trading`
- Becoming consistent takes **3-5 years**; chase consistency, not single big days. Never use
  Binance's 25/50/100% quick buttons.
- **Method 1 — the 1% rule:** know entry/stop/target first. Position size = **risk $ ÷ stop-loss
  decimal**. Example: $5,400 account → 1% = $54 risk; 4% stop → $54 ÷ 0.04 = **$1,350 position**; a 12%
  target = $162 (3:1).
- **Method 2 — % risk (large accounts only):** **never risk more than 5% per trade**, average ~1%.
- Master **one style** before expanding; simplicity beats complexity. Before adding/removing anything
  ask: *does this increase profitability or reduce risk?*

## 2. Support and Resistance — `support-and-resistance`
- Mark from **higher timeframes down** (monthly → daily). **Color code: monthly purple, weekly
  yellow, daily blue** (higher TF = stronger). Horizontals are more objective than trendlines.
- **Marking rule:** resistance = a green close followed by a red close; support = a red close followed
  by a green close. Stocks gap, so for resistance take the higher of close/open, for support the lower.
- **A level weakens with each test** (resting liquidity depletes → eventual breakout). Trendlines: 2
  touches to draw, 3rd = speculative, needs validation. **Alt+I** on TradingView inverts the chart to
  shed directional bias. Keep only nearby levels visible.

## 3. Support/Resistance Flips — `SR-flips`  ·  Vault: "Level Flips"
Broken support → resistance; broken resistance → support. **Look for the backtest to confirm**; a
backtested flip that is **lost = a Failed Auction setup**, and a failed flip → expect a large
countermove. Flips happen on fib 0.382, speed fans, pitchforks. Extended consolidation at resistance
before a breakout makes a flip-to-support more likely. Check order flow (volume + delta) on the break.

## 4. Daily Trading Routine — `Planning-routine` ★
Know the setup before it forms. **Plan both a bullish and a bearish scenario** each day; let the trade
come.
- **Context:** trend on 15m/1h (local) and 1D (higher); trending vs sideways. **Elliott wave count:**
  Wave 3 → be aggressive, long dips, compound, trail; Wave 4 triangle → ranges, tight profits, trade
  extremes only. CVD divergence shows what big traders do.
- **Mark daily:** CC fib (global ones are rare but huge — only twice on BTC in 2 yrs, both major
  tops; plus local), **4h 55 & 200 EMAs** (confluence only), daily/weekly/monthly POCs, key
  horizontals. **Volume info (maybe the most important):** previous-day OI, daily volume + delta,
  funding. Watch price + OI + volume all rising (confirmation) or diverging.
- **Timing:** wake **45-60 min before London open**, spend ~25 min marking levels + recording stats.
  Three pillars: plan the trade, be ready, understand it. Volume info beats buy/sell indicators
  (no edge when 100 people see the same signal).

## 6-7. Market Structure by Severin — `market-structure-by-severin-part-1` / `-part-2` ★  ·  Vault: "Market Structure"
The two guiding questions: **"Where is the market trying to go?"** (part 1) and **"Is it doing a good
job?"** (part 2).
- **Three structures:** uptrend (HH + HL), downtrend (LH + LL), range (narrow horizontal). Markets
  trend only **~20-30% of the time** — not knowing how to trade ranges is a main reason traders fail.
- **Confirm a trend with three pivots:** uptrend = HH → HL → **break of the previous high** (downtrend
  mirrors). Use **swing highs/lows** as reference points (Igor-T's **Fractal Indicator** on TV). ★ The
  swing high/low that started the impulse breaking the prior HH/LL **must stay intact** for the trend
  change to be valid. A swing that fails to make a new HH/LL = first sign of weakness.
- **Part 2 — "doing a good job" in 3 steps:** (1) define the attempted direction (structure); (2)
  evaluate **trade facilitation by volume** (high confirms, low = no conviction); (3) judge the
  **impact on the value area** — did buyers place value **clearly higher** (strong), **overlapping**
  (lesser conviction), or **contained inside** the prior value (**Inside Day**)? Value-area placement
  gives early warning of a trend shift. **Trade with the trend.**

## 8-9. Types of Traders — `types-of-traders` / `contender-session-types-of-traders-p2`  ·  Vault: "Types of Traders"
Four styles by timeframe: **scalp** (very low TF, needs order flow + most screen time), **day**
(30m-4h), **swing** (4h-daily/weekly, relaxed, for busy lives), **position/investing** (weekly+). No
right/wrong — pick by time available. Swing traders can still use scalp tactics for entries; learning
all styles helps. (CC's own start points: scalpers → the Severin scalping strategy; day traders → the
Day Trading strategy; swing → the swing-trading lesson.) Part 2 is a Q&A walkthrough (not transcribed).

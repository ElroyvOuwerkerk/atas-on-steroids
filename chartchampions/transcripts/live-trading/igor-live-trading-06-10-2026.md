# Igor Live Trading 2026-10-06

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session focuses on live futures trading with an emphasis on momentum-based long entries during a bullish market environment. We review key concepts such as value area analysis, VWAP interactions, opening range breakouts, delta reading, and the use of visual order flow bars to identify low-risk entries. The session also highlights the importance of disciplined session management through the "lemon shop" analogy, which reinforces defined trading hours and avoiding overtrading.

### Highlights

- [09:00] Introduction to the Lemon Shop analogy Ñ a framework for thinking about trading hours, fatigue, and decision quality. Limiting operating hours helps avoid poor decisions driven by overtrading.
- [13:00] Overview of options flow analysis Ñ identifying walls of open interest and volume that act as magnet or reversal levels for NQ and ES traders.
- [20:00] Core indicator stack reviewed: VWAPs, key levels, ORB, high/low markers, Coinbase premium, volume profiles, and sequence tools. Emphasis on a minimal, functional chart setup.
- [22:00] Multi-timeframe bullish structure analysis on NQ Ñ six consecutive bullish daily candles, higher highs/higher lows on weekly, and a bullish monthly open supporting a long bias.
- [25:00] Value area analysis from RTH Ñ identifying value area high, point of control, and value area low from the prior session to map support/resistance and magnet levels.
- [29:00] Discussion of gap trading context Ñ a large gap-up open creates a bullish setup, with gap-fill levels serving as potential support targets.
- [33:00] ORB (Opening Range Breakout) framework introduced Ñ using the first opening range high as a key level for breakout/retest trades.
- [42:00] Live example of waiting for VWAP retest Ñ observing how price interacts with VWAP at the open before committing to a trade.
- [49:00] Order flow analysis at the 500 level Ñ identifying absorption, accumulation, and the importance of acceptance above a key level before entering long.
- [53:00] Entry trigger example Ñ long entry taken on a one-minute consolidation break above a defined level with a tight stop just below structure.
- [59:00] Trade management lesson:
  - Scale out partials near defined targets
  - Move stop to breakeven once momentum confirms
  - Trail stop based on candle structure
- [1:04:00] Reading delta vs. price Ñ recognizing divergence between rising price and weak delta as a signal to take profits rather than hold for extended moves.
- [1:10:00] Discussion of patience and selectivity Ñ one quality trade per session can outperform multiple low-conviction entries. If no setup appears Monday, walk away.
- [1:12:00] Low pain threshold entries Ñ entering with the trend after confirmation reduces psychological drawdown and allows quick stop-to-breakeven management.
- [1:22:00] Reversal vs. momentum trading Ñ reversal trades require larger confirmation stops; momentum trades align with trend and allow tighter risk.
- [1:28:00] ORB target projections Ñ using the opening range as a measured move for defining T1, T2, and T3 targets throughout the session.
- [2:11:00] Observing stop runs above session highs Ñ understanding how clustered stops trigger cascading moves once key levels break.
- [2:19:00] Psychological pivot discussion Ñ moving from reversal trading against the trend to disciplined momentum trading with bias alignment can dramatically improve consistency.
- [2:28:00] Session close framework Ñ defining a clear end to the trading day prevents giving back profits and reinforces professional discipline.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, practicing with proper position sizing, and applying strict risk management when integrating any of these ideas into your own trading plan.

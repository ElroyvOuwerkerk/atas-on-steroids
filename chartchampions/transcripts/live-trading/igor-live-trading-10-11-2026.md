# Igor Live Trading 2026-11-10

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session reviews pre-market reaction to hot economic data, opening range analysis, and multi-timeframe level-based trade planning across NQ, ES, QQQ, SPY, BTC, and select equities. We examine how to combine volume profile levels (point of control, value area, VWAP, prior day levels) with session structure (A-session, B-session, IB), orb breakouts, and higher-timeframe moving averages (notably the weekly 200 SMA) to build patient, confluence-based setups while avoiding overtrading.

### Highlights

- [06:16] Reading post-data market reaction: after a hot data release, the market sold off sharply. We let price "chew" and avoid chasing immediately after large data-driven candles.
- [08:20] Multi-timeframe perspective: dropping down to lower timeframes to confirm expansion, then zooming to 30-minute to assess structure; a sharp low with no retest is usually revisited.
- [09:00] Anchored VWAP from session lows as a dynamic reference for post-data trade planning.
- [12:00] Opening below prior day value (noted at ~27,800) combined with an ugly 1-hour candle is a bearish contextual signal; waiting for a retest rather than selling the open.
- [13:40] Risk management at the open: being cautious after an early session profit to avoid giving it back; sizing down and waiting for high-quality setups.
- [15:00] Targeting overnight lows as logical draw on liquidity when rejecting value area and point of control.
- [18:00] P-shape profile transition: watching for a P-shape to transition into a lower B-shape, signalling continuation to the downside.
- [19:00] Waiting for rejection at prior day value before entering a short back toward overnight low; avoiding entries inside long candles.
- [22:00] Previous day value (RTH) vs full session: prioritising RTH levels because they reflect where the majority of institutional participants are watching.
- [27:30] One-minute opening range (ORB) applied to a 30-minute chart as an objective reference for breakout and failure trades.
- [28:30] Simplifying contract rollovers: use continuous charts, ignore back-adjusted levels, and simply track gaps as they form at rollover.
- [35:00] Discipline against overtrading: one quality trade per session is often enough; the best setups typically appear between B and C profile periods.
- [37:00] Reading order flow on breakouts: watching for aggressive participation (or lack thereof) to validate an ORB breakout.
- [42:00] Cross-market confluence: comparing ES and NQ behavior at previous day value low to assess whether a push is sustainable.
- [48:30] Patience inside A-session: waiting for structural shifts (lower high/lower low) before committing to a trade in the direction of context.
- [53:00] Position engagement reduces temptation to over-trade; knowing personal limits is a key edge.
- [59:00] Large wick on 4-hour indicates rejection but may still be within a chop zone Ñ use higher-timeframe location to grade the signal.
- [1:05:00] Relative strength/weakness between indices: if ES rolls over, NQ typically follows; set alerts below key swing lows to avoid missing moves.
- [1:16:30] Session CVD / Delta confirmation: strongly negative session delta during attempted rallies confirms distribution and weakness.
- [1:20:00] Trade management via trailing: moving stop above/below one-minute swing highs as price breaks structure to lock in risk.
- [1:23:00] Absorption and failed breakouts: buying above prior highs that fails to bring new higher prices signals exhaustion; watch for break of VWAP as confirmation.
- [2:34:00] Daily 200 SMA interaction on QQQ and the Bitcoin ETF (IBIT): closing below the 200 on the daily is a meaningful risk-off signal.
- [2:37:00] Investing framework: use a line chart template with 20/50/100/200 SMAs on the daily for cleaner trend reading on major names.
- [2:38:00] Position-sizing for mega caps: paying attention to names like NVIDIA (large index weight) and waiting for setup rather than buying near all-time highs.
- [2:41:00] Weekly 200 SMA retest pattern: break-below, retest and rejection is a repeating structure worth monitoring on major indices.
- [2:43:00] Sequence / countdown signals on daily: wait for a trend flip (bullish price flip above prior bar highs) before acting on a potential buy signal.
- [2:45:30] "Late vs. early" entries: there is always a next trade; entering after confirmation at a slightly worse price with a clearer structure is better than being early with an unclear setup.
- [2:49:00] Higher-timeframe context for BTC: watching for precision reactions at major daily/weekly levels and confirmation from Coinbase Premium.
- [2:50:00] Stacked resistance (wall of levels): IB, prior day value, A-session high, and naked point of control clustering creates a strong ceiling; breakouts above such clusters tend to run.
- [2:52:00] Multi-month distribution structure: extensive value built above current price plus down-sloping moving averages creates a difficult environment for sustained upside without reclaim.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully and practising them in a controlled environment with disciplined risk management before applying them to live markets.

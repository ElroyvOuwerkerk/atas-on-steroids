# Igor Live Trading 2026-11-17

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session covers live futures trading on ES and NQ, focusing on order flow analysis, volume profile, options positioning, and the use of lower timeframe tools to identify entries and manage risk. We explore absorption at key levels, reclaim-based entries, opening range targets, and the application of DeMark-style exhaustion counts (9/13 sequences) for identifying potential reversals. The discussion also includes platform considerations for order flow trading and how options data can serve as magnets or confluence for trade planning.

### Highlights

- [10:43] Introduction to the session with an open invitation for trading-related questions.
- [12:06] Discussion on using 5-minute and 15-minute timeframes for entries and why waiting for candle closes can cause missed moves. Order flow is presented as a faster alternative for invalidation and entry timing.
- [13:12] Walkthrough of an absorption-and-reclaim entry model:
  - Identify area of interest (e.g., 7,360 on ES).
  - Watch for orders being filled with price failing to drop further (absorption).
  - Wait for reclaim of a key level (e.g., 7,377).
  - Confirm with buyers stepping in on the delta.
  - Place stop below the swept low; target the range high for ~2R or better.
- [15:30] Explanation of broker/data feed preferences for order flow (Rithmic vs CQG), noting Rithmic's faster profile loading and deeper market depth for Quantower.
- [17:00] Overview of options modules on different platforms, highlighting how open interest and volume walls can act as magnets and directional confluence for take-profit placement.
- [21:40] Macro review of ES relative to the daily 200 SMA and prior lows, identifying key support confluence zones and the 7,000 retest potential.
- [22:50] Range analysis showing a deviation above high, deviation below low, back inside 50% break and retest structure Ñ a framework for anticipating further downside continuation.
- [24:00] Use of fib tools from low to high across sessions to map untested value and key levels (7,415, 7,450).
- [25:20] CVD (Cumulative Volume Delta) analysis of the prior session: price trimmed lower throughout the day while CVD rebuilt only in the final five minutes, illustrating divergence and session-end positioning.
- [26:00] Market profile value area discrepancy: large gap between the current POC and prior session POCs indicating the market is not in a hurry to return to value, favoring continued downside pressure.
- [37:13] Trade planning at key zone: choosing between taking a long at the low with stop below, or waiting for a reclaim confirmation Ñ a lesson in trade entry patience.
- [40:15] Observation of sweep of the lows and sweep of the highs on NVIDIA as a classic liquidity pattern preceding directional moves.
- [42:10] Reminder: never chase a trade; the first zone of resistance is usually the prior swing high.
- [43:33] Resting liquidity at 7,416 taken out after rejection Ñ illustrating how unfilled orders often get swept before directional continuation.
- [44:03] Post-sweep plan: wait for price to go back inside value, reclaim, retest from above, then look for value rotation higher.
- [1:03:12] Point of control acting as resistance in a chop zone; expectation of further push lower to generate rotation and break session lows.
- [1:05:14] Reading bid stacks on bookmap and delta divergences at potential bottoming zones.
- [1:16:45] Opening Range Breakout (ORB) target discussion: multiple failed attempts at the ORB low confirm it as a significant level for many participants; wait for confirmation rather than long blindly into a wall.
- [1:22:36] Lesson on not shorting into obvious support without confirmation Ñ wait for reclaim or rejection structure before entering.
- [1:23:06] Session structure review: high negative delta (~4K), price pressing session lows, and watching for value rotation at open.
- [2:47:33] Introduction of DeMark Sequential (9/13 exhaustion count) as a tool for identifying potential reversals:
  - 13 count requires printing two bars back below the reference bar.
  - Useful as an exhaustion signal, but should not be used blindly in strong one-timeframe trending conditions.
  - Can be combined with walls and value area context for higher-quality setups.
- [2:54:25] Interpreting a fresh "1" print followed by waiting for the "2" to confirm reversal structure.
- [3:01:37] Session wrap: emphasis on using the stream to practice and reinforce learned concepts, treating small consistent gains as paying for tuition and compounding skill.
- [3:02:41] Trade management distinction: on ES, let trades cook to targets and trail two bars back; on NQ, take quicker entries using 1-minute structure when ES is hitting key levels.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully and applying them with disciplined risk management, using them as a framework for study rather than as direct trade signals.

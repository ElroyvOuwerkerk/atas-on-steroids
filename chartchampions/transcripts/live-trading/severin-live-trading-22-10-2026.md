# Severin Live Trading 22-10-2026

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session covers trading plan preparation, demand/supply zone selection, trade management principles, capital efficiency and margin allocation across accounts, and the mindset required to patiently wait for predefined levels. We also review liquidity auction concepts, position sizing philosophy, and the long-term relationship between time, risk, and emotional attachment to P&L.

### Highlights

- [05:18] Opening market context: identifying the previous month value area low as a key level of interest and maintaining the same plan from earlier sessions.
- [07:02] Observing BTC price action and noting why scalp trades are intentionally avoided in favor of higher-quality setups.
- [08:08] Preview of upcoming concepts: pairing liquidity auctions with additional context tools and understanding demand and supply on failed auctions.
- [09:38] Evaluating demand zones: a demand zone is more reliable when it has been re-proven multiple times and sits closer to current price. Isolated zones far behind price action with no re-tests in between are weaker.
- [11:34] Statistical observation on candle sequences Ñ the probability of two consecutive same-color candles is higher than alternating candles. Useful only when paired with reversal setups, not as a standalone edge.
- [12:50] Trade management tips:
  - Use a floating stop loss initially on predefined levels, since price can tag the level, reclaim, and then react.
  - Allow price distance from entry before trailing to avoid being wicked out immediately.
  - Check whether you are repeatedly fading a strong trend, which naturally leads to being stopped out.
- [14:38] Single-stock context tools: 1-minute chart with previous month, previous week, previous day range and volume, VWAP, SVP, and the open.
- [19:05] Capital efficiency model for allocating trading capital:
  - Determine your maximum intraday position size and the minimum margin required.
  - Determine your maximum overnight position size and its margin requirement (significantly higher).
  - Add wiggle room for drawdowns.
  - Excess capital beyond these needs is moved to accounts collecting yield (e.g., ~2.7Ð2.9%), so idle margin is not wasted.
- [38:36] Liquidity auction entries: avoid targeting mini auctions on very low timeframes. Use realistic targets Ñ the full liquidity auction as a new high Ñ and allow room rather than fixating on the nearest small zone.
- [44:28] Key milestones in trader development:
  - Early stage: dropping to lower timeframes to build capital.
  - Mid stage: trading less frequently on higher timeframes once capital allows.
  - Mature stage: focusing on predefined levels, context, and selective entries.
  - The overarching factor is time Ñ skill compounds across years.
- [47:45] Detachment from P&L develops gradually. Early on, every trade feels significant; with accumulated results, each new trade becomes a small percentage of the overall account, which reduces emotional weight.
- [50:00] Starting with limited capital (e.g., $10K): begin on demo, prove the strategy, then trade one micro contract live before scaling. Avoid oversizing early.
- [51:30] Capital versus risk progression: beginners typically have small capital with higher risk; the goal over time is large capital with lower risk.
- [55:40] Market quality assessment: if the NY session shows no measurable difference from Asia/London in terms of movement, that is a strong reason to stand aside. Imagining the trade with a much larger position size is a useful filter Ñ if the setup wouldn't be acceptable at scale, it shouldn't be taken at small size either.
- [1:03:58] Position sizing and target expectations:
  - Larger size is reserved for predefined levels where a large move away from the level is expected.
  - Trade duration should always be dictated by what the market offers through trailing and reading context, not by position size.
- [1:05:40] Building playbooks and strategies: start by isolating individual statistics, then combine pieces into a bigger picture, and continually refine by removing or adding components.
- [1:11:36] Psychology Ñ acceptance as the only way through drawdowns. Losses must be accepted so that focus returns to execution.
- [1:12:30] Example of level prioritization: when two potential lows exist, prioritize the one with the higher probability of becoming a medium-timeframe low, even if it means missing a reaction at the shallower level.
- [1:17:30] Backtesting discipline: set strict rules, avoid long sessions that lead to confirmation bias, and re-evaluate every couple of hours to stay neutral.
- [1:21:00] Why fading a weak reaction into resistance is a low-quality setup Ñ longing into resistance after a weak bounce is "deserved to be stopped out."
- [1:22:30] Market behavior insight: expecting price to hold at an obvious liquidity level (such as an overnight low) when nearby liquidity is still untouched is generally unrealistic.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, backtesting them within your own framework, and applying them with disciplined risk management and patience for high-quality predefined setups.

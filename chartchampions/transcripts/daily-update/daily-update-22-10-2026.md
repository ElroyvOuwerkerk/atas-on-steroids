# Daily Update 22-10-2026

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session reviews current Bitcoin positioning with a focus on hedging an existing spot investment, managing risk through break-even stop placement, and planning around key market structure levels. ### Highlights

- [05:56] BTC short trade review: We revisit the short entry taken on the retest of the previous day's lows after a reversal from the yearly value area high. The entry logic favored the retest confirmation over an aggressive top-side SFP entry.
- [07:07] Key resistance context: The yearly value area high on BTC/USD is identified as the critical level. A rejection here supports continuation lower, while a reclaim would signal potential new highs.
- [07:51] Hedging a spot position: We walk through the rationale for hedging a profitable spot position with a short trade Ñ the goal is to protect unrealized profits, not to generate new profit. Multiple limit orders were used to fill the hedge position.
- [09:03] Stop-loss management on hedges: After the rejection, the hedge stop-loss is moved to break-even. The reasoning: if a reclaim invalidates the setup, there is no need to accept further loss on the hedge since the spot thesis remains valid.
- [09:45] Trade plan scenarios:
  - If price tests 77,000 with a strong sign of strength, close the hedge and preserve profits.
  - If price reclaims the previous demand zone, close the hedge for a smaller gain.
  - If a deeper drop occurs, consider closing the spot at a loss while keeping the short active to offset the position.
- [12:48] Scalp trade selectivity: A potential BTC long scalp exists based on structure, but given macro conditions (stock market weakness), the probability is near 50/50. We prefer to stand aside and wait for higher-probability setups.
- [15:22] Demand-to-supply framework: We revisit the liquidity auction concept Ñ a demand zone retest targeting supply, with high-liquidity-to-high-liquidity targets. This same framework applies across different assets.
- [16:38] ES futures outlook: ES is sitting at a naked daily value area low. Holding this level suggests a poor, choppy trading environment. A break lower would open up better opportunities at the SFP region and the previous month value area low.
- [19:01] "If one, then two" logic: Since the highs were taken, the preferred scenario is a sweep of the lows to complete the move.
- [20:07] Scalp trade criteria: Scalps are acceptable only when they align with a liquidity auction Ñ demand retest into previous supply with a clear high-liquidity target.
- [22:21] Reviewing missed sessions: It's valuable to analyze sessions you didn't trade Ñ identify what setups were present and whether they aligned with your framework.
- [23:30] Fading low-timeframe liquidity auctions: When price keeps producing demand retests that fail to break down with momentum, the structure favors fading these auctions in the direction of the dominant trend.
- [26:00] Single prints as scalp targets: On ES, only the upper single print area combined with the previous day value area low qualifies as a scalp short Ñ anything else is skipped.
- [28:21] NASDAQ structure: Losing the point of control opens the path toward the previous month value area low and the naked monthly value area low Ñ the preferred zones for building positions.
- [31:40] Realizing losses strategically: We discuss how, in some cases, it can make accounting sense to realize losses on a spot position and re-enter larger at a better price, rather than simply compounding at a lower level.
- [34:49] Reading the environment early:
  - Avoid taking trades in the first 10 minutes of the session.
  - If the first 10Ð15 minutes show tight, overlapping ranges with no follow-through, the environment is unlikely to produce clean momentum.
  - Recognize these conditions early and step away from the screen rather than forcing trades.
- [36:29] Patience over participation: Forcing trades in poor environments produces repeated stop-outs. The disciplined response is to wait for predefined levels to be reached before engaging.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, defining your own levels in advance, and applying strict risk management when considering hedging, scalping, or multi-timeframe position building.

# Daily Update BTC 22 10 2026

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session reviews the recent Bitcoin price action following a rejection from the CC Fibonacci zone, with emphasis on why timing the exact high was a low-probability setup compared with waiting for a confirmed failed auction. We examine inter-market correlations with the Nasdaq, gold, and oil, and discuss where valid long and short trade setups exist based on invalidation quality and confluence.

### Highlights

- [03:42] Review of the recent Bitcoin move: price pushed through the daily naked point of control, parallel channel, and value area high confluence, but did not produce a clean rejection from the CC Fibonacci zone.
- [04:37] Key lesson on hindsight bias: a profitable short does not equal a good trade. Shorting above the range high with low confluence and no sign of weakness is a low-probability setup.
- [06:10] Inter-market context Ñ Nasdaq struggling at the psychological 30,000 level provided downward pressure. A valid short on Nasdaq would only come after confirmation of a fake-out back below 30k.
- [07:46] Individual stocks (AI/memory sector) showed a clean point-of-control rejection, which served as an early warning of broader market resistance.
- [08:42] Gold weakness noted Ñ likely to make new lows despite fundamental factors that would normally support it. Bitcoin generally correlates with equities rather than acting as a hedge.
- [09:46] Failed auction setup explained:
  - Price fakes out above the range high, then returns below.
  - Entry: on reclaim back inside the range.
  - Stop loss: above the failed auction high.
  - Target: the opposite side of the range (very bearish target, stronger than a swing failure).
- [11:30] Context shift: before the failed auction confirms, there is no weakness; after confirmation, the context changes significantly and justifies a short with a defined invalidation.
- [13:30] Discussion on long setups at the lower end of the range Ñ do not front-run; wait for a reaction, a swing failure pattern, or a failed auction before entering.
- [14:50] Take-profit planning for a potential long near the range low: first target around the value area low (~81.2) or session VWAP, rather than the full opposite side, due to local weakness.
- [18:30] Analysis of current support near previous week point of control, value area low, and CC Fibonacci Ñ technically support exists, but the invalidation is poorly defined, making it a low-quality trade.
- [22:00] Core principle Ñ invalidation quality: if a clear invalidation cannot be identified, the trade should be skipped, even if confluence looks attractive.
- [23:50] Why swing failure patterns and failed auctions are preferred setups:
  - Clear, repeatable entry trigger (reclaim of the level).
  - Clear, non-negotiable invalidation (the wick high/low).
  - High win rate at key levels with defined risk.
- [26:50] Next potential long zones identified: a failed auction/swing failure near current lows, then the 77,000 area (weekly/monthly naked point of control) if the range low breaks.
- [28:30] No valid short setup remains nearby Ñ once a move drops quickly without building resistance above, chasing shorts into a bounce lacks confluence and invalidation.
- [30:30] Market outlook: higher probability of a bigger drop ahead based on failed auction, CC rejection, Nasdaq weakness, gold weakness, and strong oil (inverse correlation).
- [33:30] Bias vs. execution: regardless of directional bias, trades are taken only at legitimate levels with proper setups. A bearish bias does not prevent taking longs at valid support, and vice versa.
- [35:00] Summary of next actionable levels: longs favored around 79k or deeper at 77k on confirmation; no high-quality short available unless new highs are made.

We encourage traders to carefully review these concepts Ñ particularly the importance of defined invalidation, waiting for confirmation setups, and respecting inter-market context Ñ and to apply them with disciplined risk management.

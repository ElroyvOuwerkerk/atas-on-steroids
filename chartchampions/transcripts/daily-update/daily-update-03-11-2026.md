# Daily Update 03-11-2026

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session reviews multi-timeframe analysis across ES, NASDAQ, SPY, and QQQ using volume profile tools, with a focus on identifying the most significant structural levels, planning reversal trades at monthly support zones, and managing laddered equity investments. We examine how to define key invalidation levels, how to adapt position sizing for high-timeframe setups, and how to approach trades that favor high risk-to-reward over high win rate.

### Highlights

- [24:25] Defining the most significant chart levels: The key question to ask is “which level must be lost for an extremely high probability of a new high or new low?” As long as that level holds, the broader trend remains intact and dips can still be viewed as buying opportunities.
- [25:00] SPY context: A ~5% move is not ideal for spot investment sizing, but still an actionable level when it aligns with multiple confluences.
- [26:14] Monthly volume profile technicals on ES: Observing a monthly open outside previous month’s value area, followed by a retest of the previous month’s value area low, which then drove price into a new low — a clean technical sequence.
- [26:54] Weekly and monthly open confluence: A weekly open outside the previous week’s value area combined with a monthly open outside the prior month’s value area creates a highly technical bearish context.
- [27:06] Key ES region: The 7,500 zone is highlighted as a major psychological and structural level of interest for potential reversal trades.
- [29:19] Trade planning context on ES:
  - Daily open in the lower quartile and outside previous day’s value area supports downside continuation
  - Primary interest area for building a long position is near the monthly support zone around 7,500
  - First resistance identified at previous day value area low (~7,600)
- [30:00] Position sizing on high-timeframe levels: When trading a monthly-level reversal, expecting a clean one-minute reaction is unrealistic. A practical adaptation is to scale down to micros and build a laddered position rather than attempting a single precise entry.
- [32:17] NASDAQ monthly structure: Price down ~11% from all-time highs. Investment logic favors zones where potential upside to new highs is at least 12%+, making the risk-to-reward meaningful for spot allocation.
- [33:00] Identifying the last support before trend invalidation: The region tied to the impulsive July 30 move is the final structural support before a move below 27,200 becomes probable. This level also aligns with the 200 SMA, strengthening confluence.
- [34:18] Entry trigger process on NASDAQ:
  - Multiple failed low sweeps observed
  - Initial impulse and momentum retest were not strong enough for entry
  - Waited for a confirmed 5-minute impulse before executing
- [35:03] Reversal trade expectations: Reversal trades carry a high probability (~75%) of being stopped at break-even. The trade is accepted as part of a broader plan where the goal is either a very high R multiple or a break-even outcome.
- [36:00] Two forms of leverage in trading setups: Understanding which category a trade belongs to defines how it should be managed.
  - Setups built for an extremely high win rate
  - Setups built for an extremely high risk-to-reward
- [36:21] Managing unrealized profits: Unrealized profits must be viewed as not yet yours — simply numbers on a screen until realized. This mindset reduces emotional attachment during trade management.
- [39:00] Naked monthly and weekly value area levels: Review of untapped value area lows as reference points, and the importance of distinguishing between actionable scalp zones and true structural supports.
- [40:12] Weekly profile analysis on NASDAQ: Weekly open outside previous week’s value area, followed by a liquidity sweep into a new low — a technically consistent sequence.
- [42:05] Mapping resistance using single prints and point of control shifts: When resistance structure is unclear, confluence with weekly VAH and prior session point of control helps define reaction zones.
- [44:09] Key intraday level: Yesterday’s A-session point of control acts as pivotal structure — reclaiming it opens probability of taking out the previous day high, potentially setting up an SFP (Swing Failure Pattern) scenario.
- [45:00] Order book observations: Repeated large resting orders being pulled and relocated as price approaches — useful awareness when reading intraday liquidity behavior.
- [45:35] Equity investment framework: Laddered buy-the-dip approach applied across Amazon, Google, AMD, QQQ, and NVIDIA — adding more size at deeper discounts rather than committing fully at one price.
- [46:35] Trailing stop management: Using trailed stops to lock in profits on positions like Tesla and IBM, especially when a position reaches 25–30% gains, to protect against broader market pullbacks.
- [49:30] Profit realization under changing market conditions: Partial profit-taking is used to avoid watching large unrealized gains fade if the broader market enters a corrective phase.

We encourage reviewing these multi-timeframe planning concepts, confluence-based level selection, and the distinction between high win-rate and high risk-to-reward setups carefully, and applying them with disciplined risk management suited to your own trading plan.

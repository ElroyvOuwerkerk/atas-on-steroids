# Making the Move to Stocks

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session explores the concept of transitioning between crypto and stock trading, covering the differences between tokenized stocks on crypto exchanges versus traditional brokers, the risks of weekend gaps, how to adapt higher timeframe level analysis to stock charts, and a delta-neutral funding rate strategy. We also learn about fundamental selection criteria, a mid-term investment mindset for high-conviction stocks, and risk considerations when trading without hard stop losses.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the Crypto to Stocks concept Ñ rationale for pivoting into stocks during slow crypto periods, especially during extended sideways markets where opportunities are limited.
- [02:20] Why learning stocks is valuable even for dedicated crypto traders:
  - Allows pivoting when crypto markets are choppy
  - Supports long-term investment allocation from trading profits
  - Provides diversification into assets with stronger fundamentals
- [03:00] Barriers to entry when moving from crypto to stocks, including KYC concerns, and how tokenized stocks on crypto exchanges can serve as an accessible alternative Ñ traded against stablecoins similar to altcoins, but without ownership of the underlying asset.
- [04:30] Comparing tokenized stocks vs. traditional brokers:
  - Tokenized: 24/7 trading, no market closure, ideal for day/swing trading
  - Traditional brokers: Required for long-term investments, actual share ownership, and dividend eligibility
  - A mixed approach can serve both trading and investing goals
- [06:20] Gap risk example Ñ a long NVIDIA trade with a 2.5% stop loss can turn into a 25% loss if a weekend gap bypasses the stop. 24/7 tokenized markets eliminate this gap-through risk for active traders.
- [09:30] Three main barriers for crypto traders moving to stocks: understanding gaps, adjusting to market closures affecting higher timeframe levels, and the increased importance of fundamentals.
- [10:40] Discussion on why avoiding shorting stocks has been a long-standing personal rule Ñ focusing only on long trades aligned with strong fundamentals and investment conviction.
- [12:20] Reading gaps on the chart:
  - Gap can be measured from wick-high to candle-low, or wick-high to candle-high
  - Neither is strictly right or wrong Ñ confluence determines which boundary to use
- [14:10] Marking higher timeframe levels on stocks Ñ same theory as crypto but visually more confusing due to gaps:
  - For support: use the lowest of the closing/opening prices across the gap
  - For resistance: use the highest of the closing/opening prices across the gap
  - Applies to dailies, weeklies, and monthlies alike
- [16:30] Fundamental selection criteria Ñ focusing on companies with widely used, essential products (e.g. mega-cap tech), as these align with long-term bullish conviction and allow simultaneous trading and investing.
- [20:00] Entry strategy:
  - Use preset laddered limit orders at well-identified HTF support zones
  - Removes emotional second-guessing when price arrives at the level
  - Position sizing allows adding more if price drops further
- [21:30] Core trading tools translate directly from crypto to stocks: higher timeframe levels, Fibonacci, fixed range volume profile, speed fans, anchored VWAPs, harmonics, pitchforks, and Elliott waves.
- [22:40] Exit approach:
  - Conservative profit-taking (e.g. closing ~50% near major resistances)
  - Re-adding on significant pullbacks to maintain or grow position size
  - Blends trading with long-term investment accumulation
- [23:30] Delta-Neutral Funding Rate Strategy:
  - Buy shares on a traditional broker and simultaneously short the equivalent tokenized version on a crypto exchange
  - Collect funding payments every 8 hours while remaining price-neutral
  - Primary risk: exchange failure Ñ not price movement
  - Elevated funding rates on tokenized stocks can generate meaningful yield
- [27:40] Mid-term focus and "space to breathe" Ñ avoiding hard stop losses on high-conviction holdings to prevent being shaken out during choppy conditions, as illustrated with Google and NVIDIA examples.
- [29:20] NVIDIA case study showing a wick down through support that stopped out hard-stop traders just before a reversal to new highs Ñ reinforcing the rationale for wider risk tolerance on strongly bullish names.
- [30:30] Distinction between high-conviction holdings (no hard stops) and momentum/cycle plays like SanDisk (hard stops used due to higher valuation risk).
- [31:30] Q&A Ñ Geopolitical risk management: viewing severe fundamental-driven drops as discounted buying opportunities rather than exit signals, provided conviction in the underlying business remains intact.
- [34:30] Q&A Ñ Funding rate farming on altcoins: possible but carries extra risk from low liquidity and potential token collapse; safer on major assets like BTC, ETH, SOL.
- [37:00] Q&A Ñ Taking profit near all-time highs: not automatic; depends on technical confluence and probability of a strong pullback. Profit-taking is reserved for clear multi-signal setups.
- [39:00] Q&A Ñ How to find stocks: focus on familiar companies with dominant products and widespread daily usage. Avoid speculative or obscure picks; prioritize household-name leaders.
- [41:00] Q&A Ñ AI bubble concerns:
  - Earnings continue to beat expectations despite valuation concerns
  - Consider both downside risk and the opportunity cost of staying in cash
  - Entry price basis matters Ñ long-term accumulators have more cushion against drawdowns
- [44:30] Discussion on secondary AI beneficiaries (e.g. memory stocks) Ñ even heavily extended names can offer tradable setups during major 60%+ pullbacks to psychological support, where local value emerges.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, cross-referencing with the lessons on gaps and higher timeframe levels, and applying any approach discussed only with disciplined risk management suited to your own objectives and tolerance.

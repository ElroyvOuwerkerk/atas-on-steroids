# Context Mastery - Liquidity Auctions

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This lesson introduces the concept of liquidity auctions as a framework for reading market context, selecting predefined levels, filtering scalp trades, and managing open positions. We learn that markets tend to move from one meaningful liquidity region to the next, and that aligning trades with the active auction can improve selectivity, trade management, and overall decision quality. The lesson also covers position sizing differentiation between high-conviction predefined trades and lower-conviction scalp opportunities.

- [11:30] Core thesis of liquidity auctions: price moves from one meaningful liquidity region toward the next. These regions act as contextual targets and can function interchangeably.
- [13:20] Rules for a finished liquidity auction: Whichever region comes first.

  - SFP of high targets: previous supply or previous low

- [27:50] Combining successive liquidity auctions: once one auction completes, a new one often begins immediately. The market continuously transitions from one auction to the next.
- [29:30] Multi-auction walkthrough on a 30-minute chart, showing how to patiently wait when no clear auction is active.
- [31:30] Same framework applied to lower timeframes (5-minute, 1-minute) for scalp trading. Liquidity auctions work fractally.
- [33:30] Reinforcement: context outweighs individual confluences such as Fibonacci retracements when they conflict with the active auction direction.
- [36:40] Applying context to daily plans. A great daily plan is selective Ñ context provides the overall idea, confluence refines the exact level.
- [39:00] Real example (QQQ/NASDAQ): once a supply?demand flip targets a new high, levels between become invalid shorts, even if confluence looks attractive.
- [42:00] Key concept: missed trade vs. avoidable loss. Skipping a trade against context preserves capital; forcing it risks real loss despite one's own analysis warning against it.

- [1:00:00] Example of a bad scalp: retest trades that fade an active higher-timeframe auction. These are the trades to systematically avoid.
- [1:03:20] Position sizing differentiation: predefined, high-conviction trades receive larger size; scalp trades receive reduced size. Inspired by the principle of concentrating capital where conviction is highest.
- [1:06:30] Trade management shift: focus on reading context rather than watching P&L. Ask "how long can I stay in this trade while the market still justifies it?"

  - Trail the stop loss based on each new liquidity auction that forms
- [1:10:20] NASDAQ trade example: entry at a supply?demand flip, moving stop to breakeven when the local liquidity target is reached, then trailing based on new auctions.
- [1:12:30] Example showing successive auctions holding the entire position, letting profits compound rather than taking partial TPs that interrupt a strong contextual move.
- [1:15:30] Scalp trade example on previous-day value area low with a washed level Ñ only valid when a new auction establishes in the trade's direction.

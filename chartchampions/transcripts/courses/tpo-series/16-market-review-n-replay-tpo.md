# Market Review & Replay (TPO)

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session presents a market replay walkthrough of the RTH session using Quantower, focusing on how to apply market profile concepts, key reference levels, and 30-minute volume analysis to identify high-probability trade locations. We explore how to mark important levels before the open, interpret volume thresholds within TPO periods, and use the equilibrium (halfback) of prior sessions as low-risk entry zones for scalps targeting prior highs and lows.

### Highlights

- [07:36] Introduction to the market replay format using Quantower, where the RTH session is reviewed with live commentary to slow the pace and understand trade entries.
- [13:08] Explanation of ETH (Electronic Trading Hours) vs RTH (Real Trading Hours), and why focus shifts entirely to the RTH session once the open occurs.
- [13:46] Marking essential pre-market levels:
  - Overnight high and low
  - Previous day value area high, value area low, and point of control
  - Poor highs, excess highs/lows, and single prints
- [16:40] Context analysis: opening below yesterday's value suggests a likely rebalance back into value. Confluence of unfinished auction above (poor highs, point of control, equilibrium) points to upside references.
- [17:00] 30-minute volume thresholds on the ES: Low volume at an extreme often signals an exhausted auction.
  - Above 100 contracts = healthy volume
  - Below 50 = low volume
  - Below 25 = very low / drying auction
- [19:40] Best time to mark levels is pre-open (around 7-8 AM) so the London session's highs and lows are already available for reference.
- [26:08] Walkthrough of the opening auction: observing drying volume (2000 ? 660 ? 79 contracts) at the session low as a signal that the downside auction is exhausted, suggesting a long entry on return above the first TPO period.
- [27:20] Core rotation thesis: Once price accepts inside one side of yesterday's value (e.g., value area low), it typically rotates to the opposite side (value area high).
- [31:40] Using the IB equilibrium (midpoint of A period) as a dynamic retest zone. If price rips higher on strong volume, buying the EQ pullback offers a low-risk continuation entry.
- [36:50] Scalping confluence highs/lows:
  - Overnight high + yesterday's value area high = strong magnet
  - Preset limit orders a tick inside, with 12-20 tick stops
  - Target: the reference level itself, closing before it's breached
- [40:00] Volume on breakout = continuation signal. High volume punching through a reference (not low volume) indicates the move is not finished, favoring buying pullbacks toward EQ.
- [44:30] B-period analysis: Rapid rotation back to the A-period equilibrium (EQ), with low volume (~70 lots) at the session low confirming exhaustion before the reversal higher.
- [48:30] The halfback (EQ) of the previous 30-minute candle is one of the most reliable intraday pullback entries, consistently respected to the tick.
- [53:30] C-period setup: Breaking the A-period high confirms bullish one-time-framing. Entries at the 50% retracement of the prior period's range offer repeatable low-risk scalps targeting the prior high.
- [1:00:30] Tracking value migration intraday: drawing a box around developing value area to confirm whether value is building higher or lower than the prior day Ñ this guides directional bias.
- [1:04:30] Essential chart templates and levels to always display:
  - Daily/session open
  - Overnight EQ, high, low
  - Previous day high, low, EQ, settlement price
  - Point of control (only valid when the profile is not skinny)
- [1:05:00] Settlement price rule: Above settlement, lean long; below settlement, lean short Ñ always combined with value context.
- [1:06:30] Skinny profiles do not produce meaningful points of control. In those cases, reference the prior RTH POC instead.
- [1:19:00] Example of a liquidation run: breaking one-time-framing lower but with aggressive market orders and value still above Ñ identifying it as a stop hunt rather than a trend change.
- [1:24:00] Looking for measured move symmetries: using prior session range projections (negative extensions) as targets for the following sessions.
- [1:30:00] Power hour (M-period) analysis: EQ of the previous session acted as a precise long entry, with the measured move of the L-period providing the upside target.
- [1:33:00] Trading outside value requires caution Ñ long entries far above developed value carry asymmetric risk unless taken at a clear retracement reference.
- [1:36:00] Discussion on open interest limitations in ES/NQ (only released after session close) versus crypto, where real-time open interest offers additional context.
- [1:38:30] Combining volume profile (to identify finished auctions) with delta/DOM data (to see buy vs sell aggression at key levels) for higher-conviction entries.
- [1:40:00] Clarifying one-time framing breaks: require 2 ticks of confirmation, and if price quickly re-enters the range, treat the break as a stop hunt rather than a trend reversal.

We encourage reviewing these market profile concepts carefully and practicing them through replay sessions to build pattern recognition, always applying strict risk management and position sizing.

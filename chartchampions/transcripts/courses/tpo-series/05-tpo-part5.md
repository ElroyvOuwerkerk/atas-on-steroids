# TPO Part 5

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session focuses on single prints within TPO (Time Price Opportunity) profiles, explaining how they function as support and resistance levels similar to CME gaps. We learn how to interpret buying tails, selling tails, and poor highs/lows, and how to combine these observations with volume, open interest, and delta to recognize strength or weakness in price action. The content also demonstrates how these concepts align with swing failure patterns and initial balance pivots for higher-probability trade decisions.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to single prints as the TPO equivalent of a wick within the middle of price action, distinct from buying/selling tails at the extremes.
- [00:47] Trading single prints like CME gaps: the top acts as support if trading above, the bottom as resistance if trading below.
- [01:15] Rule of acceptance: once price accepts into a row of single prints, expect the row to fill before a potential bounce.
- [01:32] How single prints appear on the TPO template, with the number of prints (shown in brackets) indicating level strength Ñ ideally five or more for a strong level.
- [03:11] Summary of the three core rules:
  - Top of single prints = support if trading above
  - Bottom of single prints = resistance if trading below
  - Acceptance into the area = expect the row to fill
- [04:52] Real example: a row of single prints was filled via a quick wick down, followed by a strong move back up Ñ demonstrating the fill-then-bounce dynamic.
- [05:23] Definitions of buying tails and selling tails: formed at the lows/highs of the profile, representing larger participant rejection of price.
- [05:47] Concept of poor highs and poor lows: profiles without tails, suggesting larger traders did not defend the level, implying continuation is likely.
- [06:40] Example of a profile with a selling tail at the high and a poor low Ñ interpreted as bearish with expectation of lower prices or a swing failure forming a buying tail.
- [08:36] Follow-up example showing a buying tail and swing failure pattern of the prior day's low at the daily open initial balance, producing a bullish reversal setup.
- [10:00] Combining tools: previous day low backtest + CC Fibonacci retracement + buying tail at the initial balance for confluence-based long entries, with first take profit at the prior day's point of control.
- [10:40] Avoiding premature shorts when a poor high forms Ñ waiting instead for continuation higher or a swing failure that creates a selling tail.
- [12:05] Technical criteria: minimum of three blocks to form a row of single prints or tails, but five or more is preferred for stronger levels; context always matters.
- [12:49] Critical confirmation step: always check volume, open interest, and delta within buying and selling tails to confirm their significance.
- [13:17] Interpreting trapped participants:
  - Selling tail + rising open interest + positive delta = trapped longs (bearish)
  - Buying tail + rising open interest + negative delta = trapped shorts (bullish)
- [14:50] Tails without volume, delta, or open interest confirmation are weaker signals and generally not high-conviction setups unless at major pivots.
- [15:48] The core lesson: recognizing strength and weakness early leads to fewer but higher-quality trades, improving win rate over time.
- [16:46] Final summary Ñ look for acceptance or rejection at single print areas, identify poor highs/lows for continuation, and confirm tails with volume for reversal signals.

We encourage traders to carefully study these TPO concepts, practice recognizing these structures in live markets, and always apply disciplined risk management when integrating them into a trading plan.

# TPO Part 3

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session provides an in-depth review of the six core TPO (Time Price Opportunity) day types used in market profile analysis. We explore how the initial balance (IB) and prior day context can be used to anticipate whether the upcoming session will be range-bound or trending, allowing for more strategic trade selection across both crypto and stock markets.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to day types and how they help us prepare for either a range-bound or trending session using TPO charts.
- [01:00] Definition of the Initial Balance (IB): the first hour of the trading day, measured from UTC midnight in crypto markets, used as a key context tool.
- [01:27] Normal Day explained:
  - Characterized by a wide initial balance.
  - Price remains contained within the IB for the remainder of the session.
  - More common in stock markets than in crypto.
- [03:00] Practical example of a Normal Day showing repeated rejections at IB high and low, with the close occurring within the IB.
- [04:00] Normal Variation Day:
  - Smaller initial balance.
  - Range extension occurs within the first six hours (Eagle Session 1).
  - Often resembles a P-shape or b-shape profile.
- [06:00] Neutral Day:
  - Generally features a wider IB with value developing in the middle.
  - First attempts to break both the range high and low are rejected, ideally with buying tails and selling tails.
  - The following day is highly likely to remain range-bound Ñ strong continuity edge.
- [08:00] Example of a Neutral Day where the volume point of control and TPO point of control align in the middle, confirming balance.
- [09:00] Trading the day after a Neutral Day:
  - Look for swing failure patterns (SFPs) at the highs or lows of the prior neutral range.
  - Highest probability trades: longs at range lows, shorts at range highs.
- [10:30] Neutral Day Variation:
  - Similar to a Neutral Day, but price closes at the high or low of the session.
  - Signals potential momentum continuation Ñ more relevant to stock traders due to overnight gaps.
- [12:00] Trend Day:
  - Strong one-directional move accompanied by increases in volume, delta, and open interest.
  - Structure: rows of single prints separated by short consolidations.
  - Each previous higher low (or lower high) is not violated Ñ known as one-time framing.
- [13:20] Recognizing trend exhaustion: when increases in volume, delta, and open interest fade, the trend is likely weakening.
- [14:00] Key principle: avoid fading a strong trending market regardless of pre-identified support or resistance Ñ respect what current order flow is showing.
- [15:00] Double Distribution Day:
  - Two balanced areas connected by a single column of single prints.
  - Structure: balance ? impulsive move ? new balance.
  - Differs from a trend day because it has only one transition rather than multiple staged moves.
- [17:00] Summary of the six day types Ñ four range-bound (Normal, Normal Variation, Neutral, Neutral Variation) and two trending (Trend, Double Distribution).
- [17:30] Strategy framework:
  - In trending markets, trade with the direction of the trend.
  - In range-bound markets, short the highs and long the lows.
  - A range can transition into a trend when broken with increases in volume, open interest, and delta.
- [18:00] Final takeaway: understanding day types early gives a contextual edge, helping us select higher-probability trades and avoid being positioned on the wrong side of the market.

We encourage reviewing these day type concepts carefully and combining them with proper risk management and confirmation tools before applying them in live market conditions.

# TPO Part 11

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session introduces Eagle Sessions, a day-trading tool derived from Eagle Time Theory that is visually similar to TPO charts but applied differently. The focus is on using naked session points of control from the previous session as high-probability reference levels, with emphasis on the importance of confluence from tools such as VWAP, Fibonacci retracements, and swing failure patterns. We also learn how to merge sessions, identify double points of control, and recognize when a trending day may be ending.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to Eagle Sessions as a tool derived from Eagle Time Theory, best suited for day traders working on lower timeframes rather than monthly or swing timeframes.
- [00:58] Definition of a naked point of control Ñ a point of control that has not yet been retested. Each day contains four sessions, with each session producing its own value area high, value area low, and point of control.
- [01:39] Core strategy: trading naked session points of control from the previous session. These levels represent resting liquidity and often trap traders who are on the wrong side of recent high-volume activity.
- [02:11] Statistical note from New York session trading: when the naked session point of control from the previous session aligns with at least one additional strong confluence, historical performance has been strong. Confluence remains essential Ñ these levels are not traded in isolation.
- [02:35] Supporting tools used alongside Eagle Sessions:
  - Open interest
  - Order flow
  - Delta
- [03:17] Example of a high-confluence scalp setup combining: This triple confluence is highlighted as a preferred scalp entry.
  - Previous session naked point of control
  - VWAP intersecting the same level
  - Fibonacci retracement aligning at the same zone
- [04:05] Eagle Sessions on higher timeframes: although the preference is lower timeframes, naked session points of control left inside the current trading range are still important references, even when they do not appear on daily naked POC charts.
- [05:16] Comparison with TPO charts Ñ the Eagle Session view looks similar but TPO charts do not display naked session points of control, which is why Eagle Sessions are preferred for day trading.
- [05:53] Example: price reacts to a lower naked session point of control with a wick-through, retest, and continuation. A common pattern to observe on most trading days.
- [06:14] Observation that price often trades between two naked session points of control, with one acting as resistance and one as support until a breakout occurs.
- [07:37] Favorite setup: naked session POC + VWAP + swing failure pattern for high-probability entries.
- [07:58] Concept of the double session point of control: when the current naked session POC aligns with a prior (non-naked) session POC at nearly the same price, the level becomes significantly stronger. The more stacked points of control, the stronger the level.
- [09:17] Example showing how a single isolated session POC can be sliced through, while a double session POC successfully produces a rejection wick.
- [09:37] Merging Eagle Sessions: profiles can be merged when the points of control overlap closely and share similar value areas. Merging produces a stronger composite naked point of control level.
- [10:46] Using Eagle Sessions on trending days or short squeeze environments:
  - Each new session's point of control should rise and act as support for the following session.
  - When price loses the previous session's point of control, it is a signal that the trend may be over.
  - After trend loss, expect consolidation or a retracement lower.
- [11:47] Summary of key takeaways:
  - Eagle Sessions are primarily a day trading tool.
  - Main use is identifying resting liquidity through session points of control.
  - Visually similar to TPO charts but applied with a different methodology.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, practicing identification of session points of control, and applying them only alongside strong confluence with disciplined risk management.

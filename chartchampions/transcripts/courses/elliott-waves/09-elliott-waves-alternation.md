# Elliott Waves: Alternation

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This video provides an in-depth explanation of the concept of alternation in Elliott Wave theory, focusing on the utility, implementation, and tips and tricks for applying this principle. The key takeaways include understanding the four types of alternation, the importance of switching between log and normal scales to review different Fibonacci pools, and the need for practice to develop intuition in identifying the most sensible wave counts.

### Timestamps:

- [00:00:22] Introduction to the session on Elliott Wave alternation, covering utility, implementation, and tips and tricks.
- [00:00:27] The rule of alternation: Wave 2 and Wave 4 must be different in some way.
- [00:00:58] One wave in an impulse (Wave 1, 3, or 5) must extend.
- [01:08] Tip: Regularly switch between log and normal scales to review different Fibonacci pools for Elliott Wave counts.
- [01:32] The four types of alternation that must occur between Waves 2 and 4:
  - Time
  - Percentage retracement
  - Intricacy
  - Construction
- [03:07] Guidelines for wave extensions:
  - One wave should extend at least 161.8% of the next longest wave.
  - Generally, Wave 3 is the longest and extended wave.
  - Non-extended waves (usually Waves 1 and 5) are related by 61.8% or 100%.
- [03:48] Personal approach: For higher timeframe counts, subdivision is not important in every wave if the count fits the trading plan.
- [04:12] Explanation of missing waves, which can occur in X waves and may only be visible on lower timeframes.
- [06:40] The importance of regularly switching between log and normal scale views to review different Fibonacci pools.
- [07:49] Log scale is generally best suited for volatile or historic price action (1 year+), but both log and normal scales should be used to find the best confluence with other tools.
- [09:11] Tips and tricks:
  - If Wave 2 is a zigzag, look for Wave 4 to be sideways, flat, or a triangle (standard alternation).
  - Always check both log and normal scales for confluence.
  - Developing intuition for the most sensible wave counts comes with time and experience.

Practice is key to mastering Elliott Wave analysis and developing the intuition to identify the most sensible wave counts. By applying the principles of alternation and regularly reviewing different Fibonacci pools using both log and normal scales, traders can improve their skills and confidence in using this powerful analytical tool.

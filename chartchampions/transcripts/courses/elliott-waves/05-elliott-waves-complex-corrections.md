# Elliott Waves: Complex Corrections

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session explores complex corrections within Elliott Wave theory, focusing on double and triple combinations (WXY and WXYXZ structures), their internal subdivisions, and how they connect through X-waves. We learn how to identify correct versus incorrect wave counts, recognize starting and ending diagonals, and apply key rules such as channeling, alternation, and extension to avoid common labeling mistakes.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to complex corrections, typically observed during extended, drawn-out sideways price action on higher timeframes.
- [00:46] Labeling structure: WXY for double combinations and WXYXZ for triple combinations, where each segment is joined by an X-wave.
- [01:03] Definition of double combinations (correction ? X-wave ? correction) and triple combinations (correction ? X-wave ? correction ? X-wave ? final correction).
- [01:20] Double and triple zigzags channel well; each internal wave is a three-wave structure (3-3-3 or 3-3-3-3-3 overall).
- [01:46] Key rule: complex corrections cannot start with a triangle, but the final segment of a triple can be a triangle.
- [01:56] Possible variations covered:
  - Zigzag ? X ? Zigzag (double zigzag)
  - Zigzag ? X ? Flat
  - Flat ? X ? Triangle
  - Flat ? X ? Flat ? X ? Triangle
  - Zigzag ? X ? Zigzag ? X ? Triangle
- [02:51] Example of a double flat: ABC flat (3-3-5) joined by an X-wave zigzag, followed by another ABC flat, forming WXY.
- [03:59] Example of a triple combination (flat-X-flat-X-triangle), ending in an ABCDE triangle before the larger trend resumes.
- [05:02] Incorrect count examples Ñ recognizing when price action is mislabeled as an impulse:
  - No extension in any of waves 1, 3, or 5
  - Waves are too similar in time and price
  - No alternation between waves 2 and 4
  - Price channels too perfectly (a corrective trait)
- [06:11] Correct relabeling of the same structure as a double zigzag (zigzag-X-zigzag = WXY).
- [07:54] Reinforcement: always look for an extension in one impulse wave and alternation between corrective waves to validate an impulse count.
- [08:18] Ending diagonals: appear in wave 5 or wave C, subdivide as 3-3-3-3-3, with wave 4 overlapping wave 1; behave like a rising or falling wedge.
- [09:37] Ending diagonal characteristics:
  - Sometimes shows a throw-over of the trendline (bearish signal)
  - Wave 5 may not even reach the trendline
  - Wave 3 cannot be the shortest
  - Followed by a quick, sharp reversal
- [10:29] Starting diagonals: appear in wave 1 or wave A; internal structure can be 5-3-5-3-5 or more commonly 3-3-3-3-3, with same overlap and length rules.
- [11:09] Practical tip: if a zigzag channels perfectly, it is more likely an X-wave within a larger complex correction rather than a completed correction.
- [11:55] Important rule for X-waves following a zigzag: the X-wave should not exceed the start of the prior zigzag. A break above invalidates the corrective interpretation.
- [12:26] Progression illustrated: WXY double zigzag ? if it again channels perfectly, expect another X-wave leading to a WXYXZ, often ending in a triangle.
- [13:27] Reviewing how miscounting a complex correction as an impulse leads to incorrect directional expectations Ñ emphasizing extension, alternation, and channeling checks.
- [15:27] Summary principles:
  - Channeling is a hallmark of corrective price action
  - Complex corrections = two normal corrections joined by X-waves
  - X-waves themselves are three-wave corrective patterns
  - After a starting or ending diagonal, expect a quick and strong reversal
  - Diagonals provide clear invalidation levels for trade structuring

We are encouraged to review these wave structures carefully, validate counts with extension, alternation, and channeling checks, and apply them with disciplined risk management.

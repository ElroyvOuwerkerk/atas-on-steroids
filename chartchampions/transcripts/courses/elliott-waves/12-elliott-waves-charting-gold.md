# Elliott Waves: Charting Gold

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session provides a live Elliott Wave analysis applied to Gold futures (GC1!), demonstrating how to construct a long-term wave count across multiple decades of price history. We learn how to combine Elliott Wave theory with Fibonacci extensions, Fibonacci time cycles, trendlines, pitchforks, and speed fans to identify high-probability pivots and confluence zones for trade setups. The session emphasizes practical application of wave counting over strict adherence to every rule, highlighting how pivot selection via Elliott Waves can improve the accuracy of technical tools.

### Highlights

- [00:00:20] Introduction to live Elliott Wave charting on Gold Futures (GC1!), highlighting the importance of using log scale for higher timeframe analysis with extensive historical data.
- [02:00] Guidance on identifying the starting pivot of a wave count:
  - Early price action unrelated to the current impulse can be ignored.
  - Focus on where the first clear impulse begins to anchor the Super Cycle count.
- [03:30] Discussion on wave subdivision:
  - Subdivision is not strictly required to validate an impulse wave.
  - Common sense and continuous higher highs/lows can confirm impulsive structure even without clear subdivisions.
- [06:30] Analysis of Wave 2 structure using the alternation principle between waves 2 and 4, and recognition that similar time, price, and structure invalidates simple counts, requiring complex WXYXZ corrections.
- [09:30] Introduction of the 0-2 and 2-4 trendline technique:
  - Trendline breaks can signal the end of impulsive phases.
  - Breakdown of the 2-4 trendline often precedes extended corrective or bear phases.
- [13:30] Validation of a fifth wave failure on gold's early impulse, where price fails to break the wave 3 high, often leading to multi-year bear markets.
- [15:30] Breakdown of Wave 2 as a complex WXY correction with sub-structures:
  - Zigzag (impulse-correction-impulse)
  - Flat (3-3-5 structure)
  - Ending diagonal with falling wedge breakout
- [19:50] Verifying Wave 3 using Fibonacci extensions:
  - Wave 3 must be longer than Wave 1.
  - Combining Elliott Wave pivots with Fibonacci extensions helps pinpoint accurate highs.
- [22:00] Demonstration of Fibonacci time projections aligning with the 2.618 extension, showing confluence of price and time at the Wave 3 top.
- [24:40] Analysis of Wave 4 as a complex corrective structure:
  - Initial WXY formation
  - Zigzag and flat sub-structures
  - Terminal ending diagonal with trendline break
- [28:30] Alternative Wave 4 count triggered by the broken 2-4 trendline, illustrating flexibility in Elliott Wave interpretation and the importance of allowing multiple valid scenarios.
- [31:30] Bullish vs. bearish scenarios going forward:
  - Bullish count: Wave 3 beginning, targeting approximately 3000 using 1.618 extension and 1:1 Fibonacci time projection.
  - Bearish count: Current structure is still part of Wave 4, with a larger ABC correction unfolding.
  - Invalidation: Breaking below the current Wave 2 low invalidates the bullish count.
- [36:30] Practical trade construction using Elliott Wave pivots:
  - Pulling Fibonacci retracements from Elliott Wave pivots rather than obvious swing points.
  - Identifying confluence at the 0.5 Fibonacci retracement.
- [39:00] Use of Fibonacci time cycles for projecting potential turning points via W-X 1:1 projections.
- [40:30] Application of pitchforks on Elliott Wave pivots to find dynamic support/resistance zones.
- [41:30] Use of speed fans from the overall low and from Wave 4 pivot, identifying strong confluence at the 0.5 and 0.618 fan levels.
- [43:00] Key takeaway: Elliott Waves are most useful for selecting correct pivots, which can then be used with Fibonacci tools, harmonics, pitchforks, volume profiles, and naked point of controls to find high-probability trade setups.
- [45:00] Trade execution philosophy: at the point of entry, the wave count becomes secondary Ñ what matters is the entry, stop loss, and target derived from the confluence of technical tools.

We encourage viewers to carefully review these Elliott Wave techniques, practice identifying valid pivots, and apply the tools in combination with proper risk management before incorporating them into live trading decisions.

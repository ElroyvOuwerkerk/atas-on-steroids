# Elliott Waves: Fibonacci Time

## Portal Summary & Highlights

### Timestamps:

- [00:00] Introduction to the Fibonacci time session and legal trade disclaimer
- [00:30] Utility of Fibonacci time in predicting wave highs and lows - Can help provide more confidence in trade setups
- [00:50] Implementation of Fibonacci time extensions - Wave 1 to Wave 4 for predicting Wave 5 (1:1, 0.618, or 1.618) - Wave 1 to Wave 1, then Wave 4 for trend-based time projection (1:1, 0.618, or 1.618) - Wave 1 to Wave 1, then Wave 2 for predicting Wave 3 high (1.618, 2.618, or longer)
- [03:19] Zigzag corrections and Fibonacci time - Easiest to predict using 1:1 Fibonacci time and price extensions - If a zigzag channels perfectly, expect a Wave X before another correction - Wave X can take 0.618 of the time taken for the entire zigzag to form
- [07:05] Flat and Triangle corrections - Flats are harder to predict, but generally stick to 1:1 for Waves A and B, and Waves A and C (C can extend to 1.618) - Triangle time relationships: A to B (1.618), B to C (1:1), C to D (1:1), D to E (0.618)
- [08:45] Tips and tricks - Waves generally relate by 0.618, 1.00, or 1.618 (longer waves can go up to 3.618) - Use Fibonacci time on different degrees (timeframes) to find confluence - Do not use Fibonacci time alone; always look for other tool confluence
- [11:35] Wrap-up and reminder about Q&A hosted on chartchampions.com

By using Fibonacci time relationships in conjunction with Elliott Wave analysis and other technical tools, traders can gain valuable insights into potential turning points in the market. While not always exact, these time projections can help provide a range of dates to watch for important highs or lows, increasing confidence in trade setups. As with any technical analysis technique, it's essential to use Fibonacci time as part of a comprehensive trading plan and not rely on it as a standalone tool.

# Elliott Waves: Introduction Part 2

## Portal Summary & Highlights

[00:00:08]

- The video starts with an important legal trade disclaimer that viewers should understand and accept before proceeding.

[00:00:23]

- Log scale should be used on Elliott Wave counts that contain a lot of price action (e.g., one or more years) or extreme volatility (high percentage moves in a short period).
- This is covered in more detail in the separate log scale video, which should be watched before attempting to understand this concept.

[00:01:35]

- In Elliott Wave theory, Wave 3 is generally the longest and strongest wave of the overall five-wave impulse.
- However, Wave 3 doesn't have to be the longest or strongest, but it cannot be the shortest wave.

[00:01:55]

- Degree labels are used to differentiate between waves of different time frames or sizes.

[00:02:23]

- The video demonstrates the concepts using the Bitcoin chart on a weekly time frame, which meets the criteria for using log scale due to its long history and extreme volatility.
- On the log scale chart, a clear five-wave impulse can be seen, with waves 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 labeled.

[00:03:56]

- The presenter subdivides Wave 5 into smaller degree waves, labeled with a different color and degree notation (primary).
- It's important to note that not every impulse wave needs to subdivide for the count to be valid, as long as one of the three impulse waves subdivides.

[00:06:47]

- The overall Wave 1 is labeled as a super cycle wave, with a different color for clarity.
- Degree labels can be thought of as corresponding to different time frames (e.g., weekly, daily, monthly) to make the concept easier to understand.

[00:07:27]

- The presenter emphasizes the importance of using log scale and understanding the guidelines and rules surrounding Wave 3 in Elliott Wave theory.

[00:07:58]

- Tips and tricks for using Elliott Waves effectively include switching between log and normal scale, using degree labels to understand the time frame of the count, and verifying Fibonacci pools (to be covered in future videos).

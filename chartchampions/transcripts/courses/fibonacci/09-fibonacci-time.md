# Fibonacci Time

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session focuses on Fibonacci Time as an essential dimension of technical analysis, emphasizing how the x-axis (time) is often overlooked by traders despite being crucial for timing entries and exits. We explore how to use the Trend-Based Fibonacci Time tool to project likely reaction points, how corrections typically take longer than impulses, and how combining time analysis with harmonic patterns, Elliott Waves, and support/resistance creates powerful confluence. Practical examples on Bitcoin, Gold, Square, Phantom, and NEO demonstrate how to build structured, probability-based trade cases.

### Highlights

- [05:50] Chart of the Week Ð ENG setup: We review a mid-term long idea requiring confirmation. The approach: wait for price to enter a demand/consolidation zone and reclaim it as support before entering. Avoid catching falling knives; let the market demonstrate demand first.
- [08:30] Order block / consolidation theory: When a prior upward move offers no resistance on the way up, the same zone often acts as support on retest. Use the 0.5 midpoint of that zone as a key pivot.
- [14:30] Introductory Fib Time concept on ENG: Corrections commonly extend to twice the length (2:1) of the preceding impulse Ñ useful for aligning time projections with price confluence zones.
- [17:00] Why time matters: Many traders focus only on price percentage moves and ignore the time axis. Time helps determine whether waves belong to the same move or degree of correction.
- [18:30] Correction vs. impulse rule: If a correction is too quick relative to the preceding impulse, it is likely only the first leg of a larger, more complex correction Ñ probabilities favor longer, deeper corrections.
- [20:00] Timing vs. direction: Being right on direction but wrong on timing often leads to losses (early entries get stopped out). Fibonacci time helps align correct direction with correct timing.
- [22:00] Best use of Fib Time: Most powerful when combined with harmonic patterns or a solid Elliott Wave count, allowing projection of both price and the day/hour of likely reactions.
- [23:30] How to use the Trend-Based Fibonacci Time tool:
  - Click once at the low of the impulse
  - Click again at the high
  - Double-click to generate time extensions
  - Key levels: 0.618, 1.0, 1.618, 2.0, 2.618
- [29:00] Bitcoin example: The 0.618 time extension aligned with a 45% rally and the 1:1 extension marked another major move Ñ demonstrating how time extensions can time impulsive breakouts.
- [30:00] Two templates: Fibonacci-based extensions (0.618, 1, 1.618, 2.618) for Fibonacci-based trades, and 0.5-based extensions for more general or stock-market analysis.
- [33:00] Macro Bitcoin example: From the 2011 low through major cycle pivots, Fib time extensions (0.618, 1.0, 2.0, 2.618) accurately marked bull and bear transitions Ñ reinforcing how Fibonacci time influences major market structure.
- [38:00] Shark harmonic example on Bitcoin: Shows how an early move that reached price targets without satisfying time criteria often requires a deeper retest before true continuation Ñ critical for not entering too early.
- [44:30] Harmonic time relationships (ABCD):
  - Three-click method: start of A ? B ? start of C
  - Look for AB=CD at 1:1 or 1.618
  - Different patterns require different ratios (reference harmonic cheat sheet)
- [47:30] Fibonacci channel confluence: Combining Fibonacci channels (1.618 extension, 0.618 retracement) with Fibonacci time can pinpoint highs and lows to the exact hour.
- [53:00] Gold Gartley analysis: Macro example showing how corrections of large impulses may require years of time, and how the 2.618 extension can align with harmonic D points Ñ but fundamentals must also be considered.
- [60:00] Elliott Wave scenario on Gold: If treating a move as Wave 3, time projections extend significantly further out, highlighting the need to combine structure, time, and probability.
- [62:00] Square (stock) harmonic: Looking at AB:CD 1:1 and 1.618 relationships to project when a reversal zone becomes most probable Ñ always requiring confluence before acting.
- [64:00] Phantom example: Projecting potential C-wave lows using Fibonacci time, then setting conditional expectations for D-wave completion.
- [67:30] Timing entries vs. missing moves: Even correct directional analysis can lead to stop-outs without precise timing. Fibonacci time helps avoid premature entries.
- [70:00] Market psychology confluence: After a swing failure, bearish sentiment plus sidelined bulls creates fuel for upward moves Ñ anticipating what other traders expect is itself an edge.
- [75:30] Confluence rule: Never trade Fibonacci time alone. Combine it with support/resistance, harmonics, divergences, market structure, and sentiment.
- [79:30] Fibonacci in nature and markets: The 0.618 ratio appears across nature, architecture, and markets Ñ reinforcing why it holds statistical significance in price and time analysis.
- [83:00] NEO case study:
  - Impulses on NEO are quick; corrections are long
  - Current correction appears too short Ñ likely more downside/sideways needed
  - Projected potential bottom zone around April using multiple Fib time projections (1.618 crossovers)
- [91:00] Why 1.618 and 2.0 are preferred over 0.618: Since corrections typically take longer than impulses, the 0.618 is usually too short Ñ probabilities favor at least a 1:1 time extension.
- [94:00] Time frames: Fibonacci time works across all time frames (from 1-minute to monthly), but reliability increases on higher time frames (4H+).
- [96:00] Use on both impulses and corrections: Fib time applies equally to impulsive moves and corrective structures (W-X-Y sequences, 1:1 corrective legs).
- [100:30] Confluence between multiple Fibonacci time pulls: Combining multiple pivot-based time pulls to identify where several extensions overlap strengthens the probability of a reaction.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully and practicing on multiple charts and time frames, always combining Fibonacci time with other forms of confluence and applying strict risk management when planning any trade.

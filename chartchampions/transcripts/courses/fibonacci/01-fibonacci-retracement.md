# Fibonacci Retracement

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session introduces the Fibonacci retracement tool as a core component of technical analysis, covering correct application techniques, the importance of confluence, and how to identify high-probability support and resistance zones. We learn how to properly pull fibs from left to right, interpret key levels such as the 0.382, 0.5, 0.618, and 0.66, and combine fibs with other tools like market structure, channels, and higher timeframe levels to validate trade decisions.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the Fibonacci series and the role of the Fibonacci retracement as a primary charting tool for identifying potential reversal zones.
- [01:16] Confluence is essential: Fibonacci should never be used in isolation. Combining it with horizontal levels, pitchforks, Elliott Waves, or moving averages increases reliability. A single tool alone is not sufficient confirmation.
- [02:24] Definition of Fibonacci retracement levels as percentages of a prior impulse move. The three most important levels highlighted are:
  - 0.382 (38.2%)
  - 0.5 (50%)
  - 0.618 (61.8%)
- [03:19] Correct Fibonacci direction: Always pull fibs from left to right.
  - For resistance: pull from the high on the left to the low on the right.
  - For support: pull from the low on the left to the high on the right.
- [05:40] Walkthrough example showing how to pull a fib for identifying resistance on a retracement upward and support on a retracement downward.
- [08:30] Review of a historical BTC fib pull showing resistance confluence between the 0.618 fib and a monthly structural level, demonstrating multi-tool confluence.
- [09:34] Stop loss placement: Avoid placing stops arbitrarily just below fib levels. Use market structure for stop placement instead.
- [10:00] Rules for trending markets:
  - In strong uptrends on macro timeframes, the 0.382 level generally holds as support.
  - If the 0.382 breaks, expect a retracement toward the 0.618.
  - Losing the 0.618 is often an early signal that the uptrend may be weakening or over.
- [13:33] Example of early-warning signal: a close below the 0.618 preceded extended sideways consolidation and eventual trend failure.
- [14:39] Avoid trying to time exact tops and bottoms in extended trends. Instead, wait for the 0.236 to be reclaimed as support (or lost as resistance) before increasing confidence that a reversal is in place.
- [16:50] Case study showing repeated rejections at the 0.236 during a prolonged downtrend, followed by a reclaim that confirmed a shift in trend bias.
- [18:00] Introduction of the 0.66 level ("Champions Channel" between 0.618 and 0.66) as a refined zone for entries, backed by extended data testing.
- [18:40] Entry technique: Scale entries across the Champions Channel:
  - Front-run the 0.618
  - Midpoint between 0.618 and 0.66
  - The 0.66 itself
- [19:12] Multi-market examples (SPX, Tron, Oil, LINK) showing precise reactions at the 0.66 level across different asset classes.
- [21:06] Fibonacci confluence explained: Use multiple fib pulls (overall swing, impulsive move, local swing) and look for fib levels that cluster tightly together. These clustered zones form stronger support/resistance regions.
- [23:25] BTC example demonstrating confluence between the 0.382 and 0.5 from two different fib pulls, aligning with a weekly level and producing a sharp 6% rejection.
- [25:30] Example of fib + channel confluence: a 0.66 level aligning with the top of a parallel channel to create a strong short zone Ñ illustrating how combining fibs with market geometry improves setups.
- [26:55] High-probability long setups when price bounces within the Champions Channel (0.618Ð0.66) and then creates a smaller swing that also retraces into its own Champions Channel.
- [27:18] LINK example: Price bounced within the macro Champions Channel, then formed a secondary Champions Channel bounce, leading to a strong follow-through move.
- [28:14] XRP example: Repeated 0.618 bounces on leading diagonal structures, reinforcing the reliability of fib levels when combined with structural context.
- [29:30] Scaling entries in practice: Place laddered bids across the Champions Channel. If price moves impulsively before all fills, cancel lower bids and reposition higher with proper risk management.

We encourage reviewing these Fibonacci concepts carefully, practicing proper fib placement and confluence identification, and always integrating them with strict risk management before applying them in live markets.

# Fibonacci Extension

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session explains the Fibonacci Extension tool and its application for projecting price targets on external moves. We review the key extension levels, the three-pivot structure required to use the tool, how corrective versus impulsive price action influences target selection, and how confluence with support/resistance strengthens the setup.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the Fibonacci Extension tool and its purpose for projecting targets on external moves, distinguishing it from other Fibonacci tools.
- [00:22] Overview of the most common extension levels used:
  - The 1.0 (1:1) extension Ñ useful for partial profit-taking.
  - The 1.618 extension Ñ a key target in impulsive moves.
  - Failure to reach the 1:1 is identified as a sign of weakness.
- [00:45] Emphasis on using the extension tool with confluence rather than in isolation.
- [01:12] Tool location on TradingView, listed as the Trend-Based Fib Extension, and the specific levels to configure, including the uncommon 1.1313 level used for a single harmonic pattern.
- [02:22] Primary levels to focus on: 0.618, 1.0, 1.618, with higher extensions (2.618, 3.618, 4.618) acting as further targets once price breaks the 1.618.
- [02:54] The three-pivot requirement for the tool:
  - Bullish structure: low ? high ? higher low.
  - Bearish structure: high ? low ? lower high.
- [03:36] Step-by-step clicking sequence to plot the extension and generate projected targets.
- [03:52] Differentiating target selection by market behavior:
  - Corrective / choppy price action ? target the 1:1 extension.
  - Impulsive / trending price action ? target the 1.618 extension.
- [04:45] Rationale for locking in partial profits at the 1:1 even in strong impulsive moves, since price typically shows some reaction at that level.
- [05:29] Progression to higher-order targets such as the 2.618 once price breaks above 1.618, framed within basic wave counting (1-2-3).
- [05:55] Practical example on ENJ showing a pullback and the 1:1 acting as resistance for a reversal.
- [06:21] Combining the extension level with higher timeframe confluence (e.g., weekly resistance) to improve the probability of a reaction.
- [06:49] Comparison between a sideways/choppy market (reacting at 1:1) and an impulsive market like Cardano (wicking into 1.618 before reversal).
- [07:16] Detailed example illustrating a support-to-resistance (SR) flip at the 1:1 extension:
  - Buyers at a prior bounce become trapped once price breaks the level.
  - Trapped longs exit at breakeven on retest, reinforcing resistance.
  - Confluence with a psychological round number adds strength to the level.
- [08:40] Reversal at the 1.618 after the SR flip, demonstrating full target execution to the downside before a strong reversal higher.
- [09:11] Advanced application: extending from a prior low to compare the first impulsive leg to subsequent impulses, a technique rooted in Elliott Wave theory.
- [10:02] Live chart walkthrough identifying an asset in long accumulation and projecting an extension from the breakout. The 1:1 is cleared impulsively, with resistance appearing at the 1.618 Ñ reinforcing its role as a key reaction level even on strong momentum moves.
- [11:30] Closing emphasis: even after large percentage moves, confluence between the daily level and the 1.618 extension often produces meaningful reactions.

We encourage reviewing these extension concepts carefully, practicing their application alongside other confluences, and always applying disciplined risk management when projecting targets.

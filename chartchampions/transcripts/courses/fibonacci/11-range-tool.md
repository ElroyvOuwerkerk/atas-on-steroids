# Range Tool

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session explores the 0.25 range tool, a probability-based method for identifying where price sits within a defined trading range using Fibonacci-style quadrants. We review how to anchor the tool on meaningful swing highs and lows, how to avoid longing breakouts and shorting breakdowns, and how to combine the range with fractals for swing high and swing low confirmation. Practical examples across Bitcoin, SPX, GBP, and altcoin charts demonstrate how this framework helps filter out low-probability trades, define take-profit zones, and reduce FOMO.

### Highlights

- [04:01] Introduction to the 0.25 range concept and its origin as a simple yet powerful probability tool for identifying trade zones.
- [06:56] Why this tool helps traders avoid FOMO, filter noise, and remain prepared for both upside and downside moves.
- [08:03] Core definition: the 0.25 range identifies where price sits inside a selected range in a probability-based manner, applicable to scalps, day trades, and swing trades.
- [08:40] Best anchor points for the tool:
  - Previous day high/low
  - Previous week high/low
  - Previous month high/low
  - Clearly defined swing highs and swing lows
- [09:05] Introduction to the fractal indicator as an aid for identifying valid swing points for anchoring the range.
- [10:30] Core rule set based on quadrants:
  - Between 0.75 and 1.0 ? do not long; look for shorts or reversals
  - Between 0.25 and 0 ? do not short; look for longs or reversals
  - At the 0.5 midpoint ? stay out or take profits (treated as 50/50)
- [13:35] Fib tool setup: divide range into 0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1.0 with extended lines, optionally color-coded (red = no longs, green = no shorts, yellow = midpoint).
- [16:05] Compounding guidance: adding back into a position at the 0.5 only when strong confluence (order flow, open interest) supports continuation.
- [17:50] Bitcoin example illustrating how traders longing the breakout above 0.75 or shorting below 0.25 get caught in liquidity sweeps.
- [20:00] SPX example: anchoring from swing low to swing high and watching price react at the midpoint Ñ demonstrating how the midpoint acts as a natural take-profit zone.
- [22:00] Importance of waiting for lower-time-frame swing low confirmation before committing to trend continuation trades.
- [25:40] Live Bitcoin example showing why entering at the exact midpoint is a coin flip; waiting for 0.75 or 0.25 provides better odds.
- [27:40] Reading candle structure: a candle with no lower wick suggests unfinished business below; a candle with no upper wick suggests unfinished business above.
- [30:00] Using fractals to identify valid swing lows Ñ the three-bar pattern with a fourth-bar confirmation Ñ before entering longs at range support.
- [34:00] Higher-time-frame altcoin example combining:
  - Price at the 0.25 range (no shorts)
  - Monthly level confluence
  - Breaking a fractal high as a continuation signal
  - Entry on retracement to CC/point of control
- [40:00] Replacing RSI and oscillators with the 0.25 range: the range itself reveals overbought and oversold conditions through structure rather than lagging indicators.
- [43:30] Sub-range technique: applying a second 0.25 fib inside the upper or lower quadrant of the main range for refined entries, especially useful on breakouts.
- [46:00] Bitcoin example showing how daily opens near the midpoint often trap longs who buy into the upper quadrant.
- [49:00] Core takeaway rule: no more longs at 0.75, no more shorts at 0.25.
- [49:30] Fractal indicator settings explained: period 2, custom small triangle icons for cleaner visibility.
- [55:00] Fractals across time frames: lower time frames produce more fractals; 15m, 1h, 4h, daily, and weekly are most useful for swing-based analysis.
- [57:40] Clarification on previous day EQ trades: possible for experienced traders reading order flow, but newer traders should wait for 0.25 or 0.75 for stronger probabilities.
- [1:01:30] Weekly chart demonstration showing how the midpoint of a higher-time-frame range becomes a precise take-profit zone.
- [1:03:30] Final framework: before any trade, identify where price is within the range. If at an unfavorable quadrant, skip the trade Ñ no trade is still a trade that protects capital.
- [1:05:00] Emphasis on patience and consistency with rules as the key to avoiding FOMO and maintaining probability-based execution.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, practicing the identification of valid anchor points, and applying the 0.25 range framework alongside sound risk management before incorporating it into live trading decisions.

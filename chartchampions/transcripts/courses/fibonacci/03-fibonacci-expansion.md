# Fibonacci Expansion

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session focuses on the Fibonacci Expansion tool and its practical application for identifying entries, targets, and areas of confluence, particularly when price action reaches all-time highs or all-time lows where traditional horizontal levels are unavailable. We review how the expansion tool differs from the extension tool, how to configure the key levels, and we examine real chart examples on OMG and Apple to illustrate how expansion levels can act as support, resistance, and take-profit zones.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Clarifying the difference between the Fibonacci Expansion and the Fibonacci Extension:
  - The extension is a three-click tool often used for Elliott Wave analysis.
  - The expansion is a separate tool used for projecting moves external to the Fibonacci pool being traded.
- [00:48] Why the Fibonacci Expansion tool is valuable:
  - Useful for stocks, coins, commodities, and forex when price reaches all-time highs or lows.
  - Adds another layer of confluence for entries and targets.
- [01:21] Recommended level configuration:
  - Enable the 2.0 level (two times the measured move).
  - Core expansion values to focus on: 1.272, 1.133, 1.414, 1.618 Ñ these also tie into harmonic trading patterns.
- [02:32] How to apply the tool step by step:
  - Begin with a standard Fibonacci retracement from low to high.
  - If the structure breaks below the swing low, use the expansion levels beyond the pool to project downside targets.
  - These levels can serve as long entries or take-profit zones for existing shorts.
- [04:34] Importance of confluence: expansion levels should not be used in isolation. Combine with oversold readings, bullish divergences, higher timeframe fib pools, and trendlines/channels.
- [05:06] Flipping the pool: after a reaction at an expansion target, redraw the fib (prior point 2 becomes new point 1) to project the next set of expansion targets in the opposite direction.
- [05:35] Example of multiple expansion reactions in sequence Ñ 1.272 and 1.414 producing clean resistance reactions, with a channel top aligning at the 1.414 for added confluence.
- [06:11] All-time low application (OMG example):
  - At all-time lows there are no horizontal levels, so fib expansion becomes a primary tool.
  - Pull from the last pivot low up to the last pivot high and project downward.
  - Price reacted strongly off the 1.313 level, producing a 36%+ bounce.
- [07:30] Observing symmetry: price bounced from the 1.313 support and then rejected at the 1.313 level flipped to the upside, demonstrating how expansion levels function in both directions.
- [09:15] Secondary reaction at the 1.212 level providing another bounce back to the 1.133 for a 35% move Ñ reinforcing the usefulness of expansions at extreme lows.
- [09:55] All-time high application (Apple example):
  - Draw from the last major swing high down to the last major swing low to project upside expansion targets.
  - Key projected levels discussed: 1.313, 1.212, 1.414, and 1.618 near 290.
- [11:19] Trading approach and risk considerations:
  - Avoid shorting an all-time high without strong multi-layer confluence and a tight, well-defined invalidation.
  - Buying all-time lows can be relatively less risky when combined with signals such as falling wedges, oversold conditions, and bullish divergences.
  - Expansion levels serve primarily as take-profit targets when trading in the direction of a strong trend.
- [12:24] Final takeaway: look for stacked confluence across multiple fib pools, timeframes, and indicators before committing to trades at extremes, and always define invalidation clearly.

We encourage viewers to carefully review these Fibonacci Expansion concepts, practice applying them across multiple assets and timeframes, and always combine them with sound risk management and clearly defined invalidation levels before taking any trade.

# Candlesticks

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session introduces Japanese candlesticks as the most widely used chart type for reading market behavior. We review how candlesticks display open, high, low, and close values, how they relate to timeframes, and how they integrate with core trading concepts such as market structure, trend lines, support and resistance, and order flow analysis.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to candlesticks and the structure of the session, covering utility, implementation, and tips.
- [00:20] Overview of different chart types (bars, line, Renko, trend reversals) and why Japanese candlesticks remain the most popular, showing the open, high, low, and close of each period.
- [00:47] Using candlesticks to understand market movement during specific time periods and their role in strategies such as swing failure patterns and failed auction setups.
- [01:16] Explanation of timeframes using a 4-hour chart: how each candle represents a defined period, tracking price from open through highs, lows, and close before transitioning to the next candle.
- [01:55] Applying the same theory to a 30-minute chart, illustrating how a candle with a sharp sell-off and small wicks reveals strength and weakness within that period.
- [02:22] How candlesticks are used for drawing trend lines and support/resistance levels:
  - Use the wick of the candle for trend lines.
  - Use the close of the candle on daily, weekly, and monthly horizontal levels.
  - The choice between wick and close depends on the strategy.
- [03:01] Candlesticks as the clearest way to interpret market structure, a fundamental element of trading analysis.
- [03:19] Role of candlesticks in frequent setups like the swing failure pattern and failed auction.
- [03:32] Combining Japanese candlesticks with order flow:
  - Use one screen for market structure via candlesticks.
  - Use another for order flow software.
  - Watch for divergences between order flow and price structure to identify strength and weakness.
- [04:05] The value of regularly switching timeframes to maintain perspective, avoiding over-focus on short intervals such as the 1-minute chart and remembering to zoom out to the 4-hour or weekly context.

We encourage reviewing these candlestick fundamentals carefully and applying them alongside sound market structure analysis and disciplined risk management.

# Risk Management Introduction

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session introduces the fundamentals of risk management in trading, covering why it is the most important component of a sustainable trading approach. We learn how to calculate position size based on stop loss distance, how to apply the 1% risk rule, and how to combine entries, invalidations, and take-profits into a structured trading plan.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to risk management as a core pillar of trading education.
- [00:20] Utility of risk management:
  - Protects capital from catastrophic losses.
  - Trading is a game of probabilities Ñ every trader experiences losing trades.
  - Excessive leverage without stop losses leads to account blow-ups.
- [01:05] Importance of a trading plan Ñ entries should never be random; planned risk is part of the plan.
- [01:20] Implementation steps:
  - Define entry, invalidation, and stop loss before taking the trade.
  - Use a consistent position sizing strategy.
  - Risk no more than 1% of capital per trade.
- [01:55] Example of a failed auction short setup: entry trigger below range, invalidation above the high, target on a break of new lows.
- [02:29] Position sizing calculation example:
  - Account size: $10,000.
  - Max risk per trade (1%): $100.
  - Stop loss distance: 2%.
  - Required position size: $5,000, so that a 2% stop equals the $100 risk cap.
- [03:46] Scaling risk with experience:
  - Risk percentage may be adjusted based on trade confidence and experience.
  - Starting at 1% and increasing gradually is advised.
  - Never exceed 5% risk per trade Ñ this is already an aggressive upper limit.
- [04:38] Tip 1 Ñ Always use a hard stop loss:
  - Avoid soft or mental stops when starting out.
  - Place the stop in the order book to prevent small manageable losses from turning into major drawdowns.
- [05:27] Tip 2 Ñ Take profits as planned:
  - Lock in TP1 as defined in the trading plan.
  - Avoid letting greed override pre-planned exits.
- [05:59] Tip 3 Ñ Plan the trade, trade the plan:
  - Every trade must have a plan built from technical analysis.
  - Avoid emotion-driven or random entries.
- [06:04] Tip 4 Ñ Journal every trade:
  - Record what went right and what went wrong.
  - Both winners and losers contain lessons that drive improvement.

We encourage reviewing these risk management principles carefully and applying them consistently, ensuring that every trade is executed with a defined plan and disciplined capital protection.

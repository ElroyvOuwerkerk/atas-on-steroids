# Market Replay: Reclaims and Rejections

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session focuses on identifying whether a key level has been reclaimed or rejected, exploring four distinct confirmation methods ranging from the most aggressive to the most conservative. We review examples across Ethereum, Bitcoin, and ES/NQ to understand how candle closes, retests, market structure, and value-based confirmations can be applied to swing trading, day trading, and scalp trading contexts.

### Highlights

- [05:08] Introduction to the lesson topic: how to identify whether a level has been reclaimed or rejected, applied across multiple assets and timeframes.
- [07:47] Overview of the four types of reclaims/rejections, ordered from most aggressive to most conservative:
  - Candle close
  - Retest
  - Market structure-based reclaim
  - Value-based reclaim
- [09:20] Candle Close Reclaim/Rejection explained on Ethereum (4H timeframe): the first candle close back inside a reference level after an attempted breakout provides the most aggressive entry trigger. Example shown with local invalidation and lower targets.
- [13:19] Candle close example on a range low scenario: when the SFP fails, the first 4H close back inside the range can serve as a reclaim entry, though it carries higher risk due to limited confirmation.
- [15:28] Risk management during the trade: once a market structure change confirms strength (higher high, higher low), moving stop-loss to break-even after take-profit one secures a risk-free trade.
- [17:36] Market Structure-Based Reclaim introduction with the three-pivot concept:
  - Pivot 1 Ð higher high
  - Pivot 2 Ð higher low
  - Pivot 3 Ð break of previous high (confirmed structure change)
- [19:20] Bitcoin example showing a failed market structure reclaim at the range high Ñ without pivot 3 confirmation, the retest becomes an invalidation and signals a potential short setup instead.
- [22:39] Importance of pairing candle closes with market structure confirmation: a single SFP without a candle close is insufficient confirmation for a confirmed breakout or reclaim.
- [26:00] Market structure-based reclaims applied to investing: accumulating during pullbacks at high-confluence areas with confirmed structure changes supports long-term entries.
- [27:19] Second Bitcoin example at a range low (59k zone): the market structure-based reclaim produces follow-through where candle-close entries alone would be too risky.
- [30:00] Daily timeframe example at 56k range low: SFP paired with a daily close inside the level, followed by market structure confirmation, provides a conservative swing entry with strong follow-through.
- [33:19] Observation that conviction and follow-through typically emerge only after confirmed market structure changes, not during initial consolidation at the low.
- [35:19] Value-Based Reclaim introduction on ES: the most conservative method combines market structure with value area placement from the volume profile.
- [36:39] Reminder on value area placement types: inside day, overlapping to upside/downside, or entirely above/below the previous day's value Ñ essential context for interpreting value-based reclaims.
- [41:00] Example on ES at the naked weekly value area low (5,200): confirmation sequence requires reclaim + market structure + a new day placing value entirely above the previous day's value area.
- [43:00] Note on invalidation: value-based reclaims require wider stops, making them unsuitable for scalping but appropriate for longer-term positions with proper risk sizing.
- [44:00] Intraday application on NQ (1-minute): limiting analysis to previous day high, open, EQ, low, and naked value levels to study reclaims in a scalping context.
- [46:30] Explanation of why daily open outside previous day's value area strengthens the reclaim case Ñ opening outside signals strength, making a retest a cleaner entry.
- [49:00] VWAP retest trade logic: after losing VWAP, a retest aligned with local market structure (downtrend) offers a high-quality short entry; tight invalidation suits scalping.
- [55:19] Previous day value area high test: failure to reclaim leads to an opposite-direction short opportunity. Lower lows and lower highs confirm the loss of the level, with the retest offering an additional entry.
- [58:40] Integrating order flow and delta with reclaims: looking for positive delta expansion on breaks above reclaimed levels to confirm genuine strength.
- [01:01:00] Q&A Ñ timeframe selection for confirming reclaims: match the confirmation timeframe to the trading style (1m for scalping, 5Ð15m for day trading, 4H/Daily for swing). The 15-minute is a strong sweet spot for daily value area trades.
- [01:03:19] Guidance on fixed range volume profile usage: only extend the pull up to the point where the level was lost, then evaluate the retest of the resulting value area low without over-extending the range.
- [01:05:39] Discussion on using two 30-minute candle closes for confirmation: valid only within specific documented playbooks (like the CCV setup); not a universal rule for all reclaims.
- [01:09:30] Final note: reclaims are highly useful for context building. Confirmed reclaim of previous day value area high (with supporting market structure) biases the day toward long setups.

We encourage reviewing these reclaim and rejection frameworks through backtesting, applying them with proper risk management, and selecting the confirmation style best aligned with your trading timeframe.

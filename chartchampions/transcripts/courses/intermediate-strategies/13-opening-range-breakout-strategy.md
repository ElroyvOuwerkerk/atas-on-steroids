# Opening Range Breakout Strategy

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This lesson presents the Opening Range Breakout strategy combined with VWAP as an institutional filter. We learn how to define the first 15 minutes of the RTH session as a key reference range, validate breakouts using VWAP alignment, and structure trades with predefined invalidation and profit targets. The lesson also covers practical adjustments for wide ranges, position sizing, and trade management using market structure.

### Timestamps

- [00:00] Introduction to how institutional intent is often revealed within the first 15 minutes of the trading session, and why combining the opening range with VWAP provides a rules-based edge.
- [00:55] Definition of the Opening Range:
  - The high and low formed during the first 15 minutes of the RTH session (9:30 to 9:45 a.m. ET).
  - This window often captures aggressive positioning, volume spikes, and early directional bias.
- [01:26] Visual breakdown on TradingView showing how to mark the opening range high and opening range low using 1-minute candles across the first 15 minutes.
- [02:08] Core Strategy Rules:
  - Wait for the 15-minute opening range to form.
  - Go long if price breaks above the opening range high and price is above VWAP.
  - Go short if price breaks below the opening range low and price is below VWAP, with VWAP sloping downward.
  - VWAP alignment filters out low-quality breakouts.
- [02:46] Demonstration of VWAP alignment in TradingView, including the use of an anchored VWAP to confirm institutional direction.
- [03:21] Trade Parameters:
  - Target: 1.5x the size of the opening range.
  - Stop loss: opposite side of the opening range.
  - Entry confirmation: 1-minute candle close beyond the range.
- [04:13] Managing Wide Opening Ranges:
  - Option 1: Reduce position size or use micro contracts to keep risk controlled.
  - Option 2: Use additional context (levels, structure) and tighten the stop and target.
  - Preferred adjustment: target 1x range, monitor the 50% midpoint, and use a tighter stop.
- [05:20] Why VWAP Matters:
  - VWAP represents session fair value and is widely used by institutions for large-order execution.
  - Price above VWAP indicates buyer control; below VWAP indicates selling pressure.
  - Trading in the direction of VWAP aligns with institutional order flow.
- [05:59] Real Trade Example on the NASDAQ:
  - Identified opening range high at 23668 and low at 23587.
  - Strong breakdown below the opening range low with downward VWAP alignment.
  - Used candle structure to adjust entry instead of chasing or waiting for a retest that never came.
- [07:01] Stop Management Considerations:
  - Using the full opposite side of the range is the textbook approach.
  - On instruments like NQ, this can create excessive risk, so a tighter stop based on structure may be more practical.
  - Trail stop using recent 1-minute swing highs/lows to follow momentum.
- [08:17] Scaling and Re-Entry:
  - Take partial profits when reached, then look for a second entry at the 50% retracement.
  - Re-engage only when VWAP and market structure (lower highs / lower lows) remain aligned.
- [09:07] Active Trade Management:
  - Adjust stop to the previous 1-minute candle high after each new candle close.
  - "Box in" the final portion of the trade as price approaches target.
- [09:47] Why This Strategy Works:
  - The opening range provides clean, predefined timing and levels.
  - VWAP confirms sentiment and validates the breakout.
  - Waiting for both reduces premature entries common in early-session trading.
- [10:33] Final Best Practices:
  - Mark your levels.
  - Follow your rules.
  - Track your trades to build consistency through structure.

We encourage traders to review these concepts carefully, practice identifying the opening range and VWAP alignment in a structured way, and always apply disciplined risk management when testing or implementing this approach.

# Divergences Tutorial

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This video provides an in-depth tutorial on divergences in trading, emphasizing that they should never be used as a primary entry/exit signal but rather as a confluence tool alongside other technical factors. The instructor breaks down the four main types of divergences (bullish, bearish, hidden bullish, hidden bearish), explains divergence classes (A, B, and C), and shares real chart examples from Bitcoin and Binance to illustrate proper application within established trends and at key support/resistance levels.

### Timestamps:

- [00:00] Introduction to divergences and the philosophy of naked chart trading. - Divergences should be the last thing referenced, not the first. - Beginners often overload charts with 10+ indicators (RSI, MACD, Ichimoku, etc.) believing more = better edge. - Rhetorical reminder: 95% of retail traders lose money while obsessing over indicators.
- [01:32] Why bearish divergences are misleading after sideways breakouts. - Example: Bitcoin's 2019 breakout from $3K-$4K range up to mid-$5Ks. - After a massive momentum push, the secondary leg up is always on lower momentum—naturally producing bearish divergence. - Many retail traders get trapped shorting healthy bullish trends because of this misread signal.
- [03:54] Standard Bullish Divergence (Reversal Signal). - Price makes a lower low, oscillator makes a higher low. - Only valid after an extended downtrend approaching an important support level. - Signifies selling pressure is drying up—possible trend reversal (always probabilities, never guaranteed).
- [05:50] Live Bitcoin example showing bullish divergence into a Fibonacci 0.618 extension and hourly support. - Stresses the importance of confluence: monthly levels + Fibs + divergence together.
- [06:34] Standard Bearish Divergence (Reversal Signal). - Price makes a higher high, oscillator makes a lower high. - Always calculated from candle closes, not wicks (official method). - Strongest when occurring at high-timeframe resistance after an extended uptrend.
- [08:27] Hidden Bullish Divergence (Continuation Signal). - Price makes a higher low, oscillator makes a lower low. - Used to buy the dip in an established uptrend—NOT for reversals. - Common mistake: traders misuse hidden divergences expecting reversals.
- [10:18] Binance chart example of hidden bullish divergence during its uptrend to all-time highs. - Look for confluence: Fibs from low to high, 0.618 retracements, daily/hourly support levels.
- [11:18] Hidden Bearish Divergence (Continuation Signal). - Price makes a lower high, oscillator makes a higher high. - Used to short rallies within an established downtrend. - Best at old support flipping to resistance (SR flip) and prior monthly levels.
- [12:11] Real BNB example of a "sucker's rally": - 4-5 green candles after a downtrend trap FOMO buyers. - Combined with SR flip + monthly resistance + trendline third touch = high-probability short setup.
- [14:14] Divergence Classes (A, B, C) explained. - Class A: Strongest—standard higher high in price with lower high on oscillator. The only class worth trading. - Class B: Double top in price with lower high on oscillator—weaker. - Class C: New high in price but oscillator at same level—weakest, generally avoid.
- [16:50] Final takeaways and warnings. - Divergences are a confluence tool, never a standalone trade signal. - Always combine with support/resistance, Fibonacci levels, trend structure, and volume. - Trading is about probabilities—use proper risk management.

Apply these concepts wisely: Stop treating divergences as standalone entries. Instead, layer them with high-timeframe levels, trend context, and proper risk management to dramatically improve your probability of success. Practice spotting Class A divergences at key zones, and start filtering out the noise from less reliable signals!

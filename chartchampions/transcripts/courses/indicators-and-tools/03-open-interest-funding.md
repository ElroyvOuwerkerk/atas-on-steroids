# Open Interest & Funding

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session explores how open interest and funding rates can be used alongside volume and price action to assess the health of market trends in cryptocurrency. We learn how to distinguish between trend moves fueled by new contracts opening versus position covering, and how to interpret funding extremes within the proper market context to avoid misreading signals.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to open interest and funding as essential tools for trading decisions, with open interest highlighted as a must-know concept.
- [01:14] Open interest defined: a statistic used to assess the health of macro trends Ñ uptrends, downtrends, and sideways ranges Ñ and to anticipate potential breakouts.
- [01:54] Example of a historical open interest pattern: Bitcoin repeatedly rejecting at 1B open interest during a descending triangle, illustrating that patterns evolve and must be reassessed over time.
- [02:45] Context is critical: no pattern or statistic should be traded in isolation. Each setup is unique and must be evaluated with respect to overall market conditions, volume, and structure.
- [04:46] Healthy uptrend criteria: When all three align, the trend is backed by new money entering through new long contracts.
  - Price rising
  - Volume increasing
  - Open interest increasing
- [05:30] Signs that an uptrend is weakening: price making new highs on declining volume and declining open interest, which signals exhaustion rather than fresh buying interest.
- [06:49] Practical example: a new high in price made on decreasing volume and declining open interest produced a bearish signal that preceded a pullback.
- [07:37] Healthy downtrend criteria: price falling with increasing bearish volume and rising open interest, indicating new shorts opening and new money entering on the sell side.
- [08:12] Interpretation of open interest changes:
  - Rising OI + rising price = new longs opening (bullish)
  - Falling OI + rising price = short covering / position closing (bearish)
  - Rising OI + falling price = new shorts opening (bearish)
  - Falling OI + falling price = long liquidation / exhaustion (potentially bullish reversal)
- [09:15] Market logic explained: price must be supported by genuine buying interest at new levels. If an advance is driven only by short closures, demand at that price is weak and pullbacks become likely.
- [10:45] Confluence example: rounded bottom structure combined with bullish CVD divergence showing heavy selling absorbed by limit buyers Ñ a sign of strong underlying demand.
- [11:40] Comparing two similar price moves with different contexts: one driven by a short squeeze after heavy selling at the lows (valid continuation), versus one occurring after upward-sloping consolidation (weaker setup).
- [13:01] Weakening downtrend signals: declining volume and declining open interest on continued price drops indicate fading seller interest, often preceding rounded bottoms and reversals.
- [14:08] Summary of core principles:
  - Rising volume + rising OI = trend likely continues
  - Falling volume + falling OI = trend exhaustion possible
  - Rising price + falling OI = short covering, not new demand
  - Falling price + rising OI = aggressive new short selling
- [15:16] Introduction to funding rates on perpetual swaps: because perpetuals have no expiry, funding is used to anchor contract price to spot. Positive funding = longs pay shorts; negative funding = shorts pay longs.
- [17:28] Using funding to identify crowded trades: if price is falling while funding remains positive, longs are likely opening into weakness Ñ a sign of a crowded long position.
- [18:48] Context matters for funding interpretation: traders tolerate high funding during strong uptrends because positions are profitable, but high funding during downtrends pressures losing positions and often leads to flushes.
- [20:45] Funding rate reference levels:
  - 0.01% Ñ normal
  - 0.02%+ Ñ slightly skewed, not alarming
  - 0.04%+ Ñ moderate pressure to close
  - 0.07%+ Ñ exceeds market fees, significant
  - 0.075%+ Ñ extreme, often precedes volatility
- [21:50] Warning against trading funding in isolation: attempting to position purely to receive funding often results in being caught offside by sudden price moves. Funding should only support a decision when combined with other confluence factors.

We are encouraged to apply these open interest and funding concepts carefully, always within the full market context, and to use them as part of a broader analytical framework alongside sound risk management.

# VWAP Tutorial

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session introduces the Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP) as a dynamic support and resistance tool that combines price and volume, making it highly valuable for both scalpers and swing traders. We explore how VWAP behaves across multiple timeframes Ñ session, daily, weekly, yearly, and anchored Ñ and how it can be used effectively when combined with other confluences such as point of control, Fibonacci levels, value area high/low, and market structure. The core takeaway is that VWAP should never be traded in isolation, but as part of a layered confluence approach aligned with market context.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to VWAP as a dynamic support and resistance level Ñ below the line, expect resistance; above the line, expect support.
- [00:45] Why VWAP matters: it combines price action with volume, two of the most important market inputs, into a single reference level.
- [01:14] VWAP benefits larger-size traders because high transacted volume at this level enables easier fills with reduced slippage.
  - Low-volume sideways markets create slippage issues for scalpers.
  - Institutional and large-capital traders often target VWAP for executions.
- [03:33] VWAP works across multiple timeframes:
  - Session VWAP Ð used for scalp trading.
  - Daily, Weekly, Monthly, Quarterly, Yearly, Decade VWAPs Ð higher timeframe reference levels.
  - Anchored VWAP Ð pinned to a specific significant event or price level.
- [03:51] Yearly VWAP example: price above the line represents value above fair price; price below represents below value. First retests in a trend typically produce strong reactions.
- [05:28] Importance of context Ñ strong trends with higher highs and higher lows can push through VWAP levels due to momentum (e.g., short squeezes).
- [06:08] The weekly VWAP is an actively interacted level and should be monitored regularly; the yearly VWAP is touched less frequently but reacts strongly.
- [07:05] Session VWAP in action: strong confluence setups when VWAP aligns with Fibonacci levels or Point of Control in trending markets.
- [09:17] VWAP + Point of Control confluence creates high-probability scalp entries, especially during trend continuations.
- [10:30] VWAP aligning with the Weekly Value Area High provides additional confluence, strengthening the trade setup.
- [11:15] Example of a scalp trade combining VWAP, TPO, volume, and a liquidity grab above a prior high before reversing to the VWAP.
- [12:52] Scalp trade mechanics:
  - Define entry, exit, and invalidation before entering.
  - Use laddered entries to manage size and risk.
  - Trades may last only minutes with small point moves Ñ consistency compounds results.
  - Limit orders help minimize slippage on entry and exit.
- [14:42] Daily VWAP for swing traders: retracements to the VWAP represent returns to fair value, providing lower-risk entries in trending markets.
- [15:46] Market structure protects swing positions Ñ as long as higher highs and higher lows remain intact, VWAP retests offer trend-continuation opportunities.
- [16:23] Summary of VWAP functions:
  - Support in uptrends.
  - Resistance in downtrends.
  - Support/resistance in sideways markets.
  - Confluence is essential Ñ never trade VWAP alone.
- [19:40] Weekly VWAP acts as consistent support throughout active uptrends, offering repeated entry opportunities on retests.
- [20:35] Anchored VWAP explained: pinning VWAP to significant events (e.g., major highs, lows, or market shocks) to measure the volume-weighted price from that point forward.
- [23:30] Practical application tips:
  - On time-based charts, VWAP works well on 3, 5, and 15-minute timeframes.
  - VWAP is timeframe-agnostic Ñ applicable to all charts.
  - Always monitor the weekly VWAP as a major reference.
- [24:40] Final rule: Always combine VWAP with at least two confluences and apply context Ñ understanding why price is moving significantly improves setup quality.

We encourage reviewing these VWAP concepts carefully, applying them with proper confluence and market context, and always maintaining disciplined risk management when putting them into practice.

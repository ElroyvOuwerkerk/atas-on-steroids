# Fractals

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session explores the concept of fractals in trading, explaining how similar historical price action patterns can be used as a forecasting tool to anticipate future market movements. We learn how to identify fractals, apply them with the correct chart settings, combine them with other forms of technical analysis for confluence, and understand their limitations. A detailed case study on Bitcoin illustrates how a fractal from June 2019 helped forecast multiple pivots during January 2021.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Definition of a fractal: price action that mirrors previous market movement. The principle "history doesn't repeat, but it rhymes" explains why similar patterns re-emerge over time, forming the basis for recognizing structures like ascending triangles or bump-and-run formations.
- [01:00] Why experience is essential for trading fractals:
  - Recognizing a fractal requires having traded similar price action before.
  - Fractals are typically recalled from memory rather than searched for.
  - Pattern recognition develops through repeated exposure to live price action.
- [01:45] Chart settings for drawing fractals in TradingView:
  - Use the Bars Pattern tool with HL Bars setting.
  - Do not use mirrored or flipped variations by default.
  - Start at the origin of the prior price action and extend to its end point.
- [02:30] Example of overlaying a fractal: consolidation, downside move, bull flag, continuation, and retracement. Demonstrates how fractal projection can provide confidence in anticipated moves.
- [03:00] Importance of confluence:
  - A fractal should never be traded in isolation.
  - Combine it with tools such as harmonic patterns and daily support/resistance.
  - Multiple confirming factors increase the reliability of a trade idea.
- [04:00] Timeframe considerations:
  - Fractals can appear on any timeframe, but higher timeframes (4H and above) tend to be more reliable.
  - Lower timeframes produce more noise and less consistent fractal structures.
- [04:50] Do not expect perfection with fractals:
  - Expect similarity, not exact replication.
  - Slight deviations in the middle or end of the pattern are normal.
  - Fractals act as a forecast, often giving a directional roadmap days or weeks in advance.
- [05:50] Case study: Using a June 2019 descending triangle fractal to project price action in January 2021:
  - Both periods started with a low–high–low structure after strong rallies.
  - The fractal projected: break of $30k ? rally ? retrace ? double bottom ? rally ? decline.
  - Early recognition of the fractal was key to building a plan with high confidence.
- [08:00] How the fractal played out:
  - $30k break with a long wick.
  - Rally followed by a double bottom.
  - Subsequent rally to ~$38,500, then retracement to ~$33,000.
  - Fractal invalidated once price broke above the prior high — signaling to discard the forecast.
- [09:00] Why fractals work: markets are driven by participants applying similar technical analysis, producing recurring structural behavior.
- [10:00] Time alignment matters:
  - A 4H fractal should be compared against 4H price action — not applied to a 1-minute chart.
  - The duration of the current pattern should resemble that of the original fractal.
- [11:00] Summary of key points:
  - Fractals provide trade ideas and directional bias.
  - They originate from recognized past price action.
  - Entries should be taken from lower timeframes using additional technical tools.
  - Fractals are best used as a forecast and guideline, never as a standalone entry trigger.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, practicing fractal recognition over time, and always applying strong risk management when combining fractals with other technical confluences.

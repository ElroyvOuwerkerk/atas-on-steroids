# Log Scale Charts

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session explains the practical differences between logarithmic and linear (normal) chart scales, focusing on when each scale is most appropriate for analyzing price action. We learn how log scale becomes essential when dealing with highly volatile assets, long-term historical data, or when seeking a higher timeframe perspective, and how it directly affects tools such as channels and Fibonacci retracements/extensions on platforms like TradingView.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the three primary scenarios where log scale is preferred over normal scale:
  - When the asset is extremely volatile with very large percentage moves (100%+).
  - When analyzing older historical data spanning several years.
  - When seeking a higher timeframe or alternative perspective on price action.
- [01:08] Example of a chart that has moved hundreds of percent, demonstrating why log scale is the appropriate view for such large-magnitude moves.
- [01:38] Application to a Bitcoin higher timeframe chart showing a price channel that only respected price action when plotted on log scale, producing a 15% rejection at the channel top Ñ a setup that would have appeared invalid on normal scale.
- [02:52] Reinforcement of the criteria: this Bitcoin example meets both the old data and high volatility conditions, making log scale the correct choice for higher timeframe analysis (weekly/monthly).
- [03:36] Case study of a log scale Fibonacci extension projecting the $42,000 target on Bitcoin, which was reached and produced a 30% pullback from the 1.618 extension level. The same Fib drawn on normal scale would have produced incorrect levels.
- [04:46] Important TradingView technical note: when switching to log scale, you must enable the "Fib levels based on log scale" option on Fibonacci tools, otherwise the levels will be plotted incorrectly.
- [05:18] Demonstration of how an Elliott Wave count (waves 1-2-3-4) becomes virtually impossible to read on normal scale when early-cycle dollar moves are small relative to later moves, while log scale visualizes percentage moves proportionally.
- [06:25] Walkthrough of plotting a Fibonacci retracement from a December 2018 low to a June 2019 high and down to a March 2020 low, illustrating how toggling the log scale checkbox dramatically changes the projected values.
- [08:11] Discussion of using both scales for confluence Ñ particularly when data is highly volatile but not very old Ñ to identify potential retracement levels (examples given around $16,000 and $9,000 as hypothetical Fib-based reversal zones).
- [09:53] Key takeaway on perspective: levels that appear weak or broken on normal scale may hold cleanly on log scale, providing stronger trade setups, especially near all-time highs.
- [10:25] Summary of best practices:
  - Use log scale for historic data.
  - Use log scale for extreme volatility and large percentage moves.
  - Check both scales to find confluence and strengthen level identification.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully and testing both chart scales on your own analysis, while always applying proper risk management when acting on Fibonacci, channel, or Elliott Wave based levels.

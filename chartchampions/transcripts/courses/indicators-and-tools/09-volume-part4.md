# Volume Part 4

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This video provides an in-depth tutorial on using the Fixed Range Volume Profile tool in trading, demonstrating how to identify high-probability support and resistance levels by analyzing untested ranges and their points of control. Viewers will learn practical techniques for selecting fixed range boundaries, understanding why price reacts at point of control levels, and applying this strategy across different timeframes for both swing and scalp trading.

### Detailed Timestamps:

- [00:00] Introduction to the Fixed Range tool as one of the most powerful trading tools, particularly useful for analyzing untested ranges to determine likely rejection points where price will reverse.
- [00:27] Key insight: Price doesn't simply reject from the wick of an untested range Ñ instead, price often enters the range and rejects from the point of control. Understanding the untested range is critical.
- [00:43] Explanation of why price moves the way it does Ñ markets are structured, not random. Price is drawn toward liquidity regions and rejected from crowded trades, creating clear patterns.
- [01:25] Why an untested point of control is such a strong level:
  - Identify the initial range with Value Area Low, Value Area High, and Point of Control
  - The point of control represents where the most volume was transacted
  - Trading rules: Short the value area high, long the value area low, and never enter new positions at the point of control
- [02:23] Support/resistance flips explained Ñ when price breaks above value area high, it acts as support on retest; when it loses value area low, it acts as resistance on back-test.
- [02:45] Why the point of control becomes a magnet Ñ many inexperienced traders enter positions at the middle of the range, creating predictable reaction zones for experienced traders to exploit.
- [03:22] Footprint charts mentioned as the next evolution beyond fixed ranges. Standard volume tells you how much volume traded on a candle, but fixed ranges show where horizontally the volume occurred Ñ critical for identifying true support/resistance.
- [04:00] Detailed example of why volume location within a candle matters more than just the wick low Ñ high volume areas within candles are stronger S/R levels than simply the candle extremes.
- [05:03] How to select fixed range boundaries:
  - Start at the first wick that begins the range
  - End at the candle just before the breakdown/breakout
  - Capture the consolidation period where most volume was transacted
- [06:39] Second example pulling a fixed range from a daily resistance area Ñ start where the range begins, end after the back-test that confirms the breakout.
- [08:08] Real example of how the strategy played out: predicted a drop to 6,688 using fixed range point of control, which hit to the exact dollar. Demonstrates the precision of the tool.
- [09:25] Interactive challenge: Where to pull fixed ranges when looking for support? Two valid approaches discussed:
  - Aggressive entry: From where rejection began at the highs
  - Relaxed entry: Extended to capture the last candle before breakout
- [11:51] Level-to-level trading approach Ñ focus on key levels like 6,600 (double bottom support) before considering lower targets like 6,200. Don't skip ahead until current support fails.
- [13:18] Important caution about volume distribution: large volume across multiple price areas creates support, but it's not as strong as concentrated volume at a single key level.
- [13:53] Demonstrating fixed range selection for resistance levels (example: 7,317) Ñ start at the first rejection, end where the breakdown begins.
- [15:00] Timeframe considerations:
  - Higher timeframes make range identification easier Ñ patterns "stare you in the face"
  - 5-minute charts require much more reactive trading
  - Scalp trading requires intense focus; positions exited within minutes if reaction isn't right
  - Swing trading is more relaxed Ñ set and forget approach
  - Same technical analysis applies across all timeframes

Apply the fixed range tool to your own chart analysis Ñ start by identifying clear consolidation ranges on higher timeframes, mark the point of control, value area high, and value area low, and watch for price reactions at these key volume-based levels. Mastering this single tool can dramatically improve your entry and exit precision!

# Indicators Introduction

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This video provides an in-depth review of popular trading indicators from a profitable trader's perspective, emphasizing a minimalist approach to indicator usage. The discussion covers the limitations of lagging indicators like MACD crossovers, while exploring practical applications of tools such as Average True Range (ATR), Ultimate RSI, Stochastic RSI, Pivot Points, and Volume Profile. Viewers will gain insights into which indicators are useful for newer traders, how to apply them as confluence tools, and why ultimately mastering price action without indicator dependency is the goal.

### Detailed Timestamps

- [00:00] Introduction to Indicators A profitable trader's minimalist approach to indicators. Most indicators discussed are lagging indicators, meaning they rely on historical data calculations (such as moving averages) and trail behind current price action.
- [00:30] MACD Crossover Strategy Critique Explanation of why MACD crossovers are considered an awful trading strategy:
  - By the time the bullish/bearish cross occurs, the bulk of the move has already happened
  - Entering on a bearish cross often means holding through significant drawdown
  - Better to short at the highs of moves rather than after the move has played out
  - Can be useful for trend confirmation (e.g., death cross), but not for entries
- [02:00] Moving Averages as Leading Indicators Demonstrates how the 200 Daily MA can act as a leading indicator by anticipating price rejections at the moving average before they occur, allowing traders to plan trades in advance.
- [02:35] Average True Range (ATR) Strong recommendation for stock and forex traders:
  - Uses a 14-period lookback to calculate average candle movement
  - Higher volatility = larger ATR number
  - Stop loss placement: Place stops outside the ATR range to avoid premature stop-outs
  - Price targets: Integrate ATR into target setting (e.g., $100 ATR could inform a $100 profit target)
- [04:18] Ultimate RSI Multi-Timeframe An upgraded version of the standard RSI, ideal for newer traders:
  - Allows simultaneous display of two RSIs (e.g., 1H and Daily)
  - Helpful for traders who forget to zoom out to higher timeframes
  - Visually highlights overbought/oversold conditions with line markers
  - Free indicator on TradingView (search "CM_Ultimate_RSI_Multi_Time_Frame" by Chris Moody)
- [06:24] ZigZag Indicator Useful for identifying swing highs and swing lows:
  - Helps traders struggling to identify trend momentum
  - Excellent for determining where to pull Fibonacci retracements from
  - Simplifies swing point identification for less experienced traders
- [07:02] Stochastic RSI Comparison with standard RSI:
  - Features K and D line crossovers within the RSI
  - Bearish signal: Blue crosses below red in overbought territory
  - Bullish signal: Blue crosses above red in oversold territory
  - Stochastics moves faster between extremes than standard RSI
  - Better suited for scalping with adjusted settings
  - No definitive answer on whether RSI or Stochastics is superior—personal trading style determines preference
- [09:39] Pivot Points (Fibonacci) Setup tip: double-click and change to Fibonacci, hide historical:
  - Standard pivot in middle, with R1 (resistance) and S1 (support)
  - Some traders wait for R1 break + retest before going long to R2
  - Same logic applies for S1 break for shorts to S2
  - Best used as confluence with other technical factors (e.g., trendlines)
  - Warning: Don't use pivot points in isolation—combine with other analysis
- [11:30] Volume Profile - High and Low Volume Nodes Application using ZRX as an example:
  - High volume nodes at key levels = stronger confidence in resistance/support holding
  - Low/zero volume nodes at levels = weaker confluence, less reliable holds
  - Combine with weekly/daily horizontal levels for stronger confluence
  - Look for the Point of Control (POC) to identify significant rejection zones
  - Requires TradingView Pro version
  - Experienced traders can often identify these zones visually without the indicator
- [13:33] Closing Thoughts The ultimate goal is to develop the skill to read price action without relying on indicators, but these tools can serve as helpful aids for newer traders during the learning process.

Take action: Experiment with these free TradingView indicators to find what fits your personal trading style, but always strive toward developing pure price action reading skills. Remember—indicators should support your analysis as confluence tools, not replace sound technical decision-making!

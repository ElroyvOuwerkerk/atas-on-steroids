# Volume Part 1

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session introduces the fundamentals of volume analysis in trading, explaining how volume represents the total contracts bought and sold within a given timeframe and why liquidity, spread, and volume confirmation are essential for making informed trading decisions. We review how volume behaves during trends, reversals, consolidations, and breakouts, with practical examples from Bitcoin price action, and learn how to screen assets for sufficient volume and volatility before entering trades.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to volume: Volume is defined as the total number of contracts bought and sold within a specific timeframe. Each candle on a volume histogram aggregates both buys and sells into a single total figure.
- [01:29] Liquidity and spread: We examine why liquidity matters. A wide bid-ask spread typically signals low liquidity, which increases execution risk. Traders should prefer assets with tight spreads and high volume. Example given: a 10-cent spread on Bitcoin Cash USD market.
- [03:16] Volume as trend confirmation: For every buyer there is a seller Ñ each volume bar reflects combined activity. Volume helps confirm trends, reversals, and breakouts.
- [05:06] Recommended volume settings: Switching the volume moving average period to 30 and using the histogram display style for clearer visualization.
- [05:30] Healthy bullish trend characteristics:
  - Rising price accompanied by rising volume = strong, healthy uptrend
  - Rising price with declining volume and large sell spikes = weak uptrend, higher reversal risk
- [06:40] Healthy bearish trend characteristics:
  - Falling price with growing red (sell) volume = strong downtrend
  - Falling price with shrinking volume = selling interest fading, potential exhaustion
- [07:22] Case study Ñ Bitcoin breakdown from 6K to 3K: The initial breakdown occurred on very high volume, confirming the move. Subsequent legs down showed progressively lower volume, signaling weakening bearish momentum and eventual bullish exhaustion of sellers.
- [08:40] Breakout confirmation rule: A valid breakout from a range requires an increase in volume. A breakout on low or average volume is likely a fakeout and often returns to the range.
- [09:19] Rounded bottom example: After months of sideways consolidation on low volume, the eventual breakout occurred on a large volume surge Ñ confirming a legitimate buy opportunity.
- [10:24] Bull flag pattern with volume:
  - Initial breakout on high volume
  - Flag/consolidation on decreasing volume
  - Continuation breakout again on increasing volume = valid bullish continuation
  - Without the secondary volume surge, the breakout is suspect
- [12:09] Identifying a safe short setup: A high-volume initial breakdown followed by downward continuation where sell volume remains dominant and buy volume stays weak indicates sellers remain in control with minimal counter-pressure.
- [14:43] Bitcoin uptrend walkthrough: Step-by-step review of a bullish sequence:
  - Breakout from range on elevated volume
  - Pullback/retest on decreasing volume
  - Successful retest followed by renewed breakout on higher volume
  - Repeat pattern of expansion and contraction confirming trend health
- [17:46] Early warning signs: When new highs are made on lesser volume than prior swings, caution is warranted Ñ momentum may be weakening even if price continues upward.
- [18:01] Elliott Waves and volume: Elliott Wave analysis relies on crowd psychology and therefore requires high-volume markets dominated by human participants. Low-volume altcoins are not suitable for Elliott Wave analysis; Fibonacci-based approaches may work better in those cases.
- [19:00] Screening for tradable assets: A practical workflow for finding volatile, liquid instruments:
  - Sort by 24-hour volume on the exchange
  - Minimum threshold suggested: 200+ BTC volume
  - Combine volume filter with strong directional movement for scalping opportunities
  - Avoid coins with extreme price moves on very low volume Ñ likely pump-and-dumps or anomalies

We encourage traders to review these volume principles carefully and integrate them with sound risk management when evaluating trends, breakouts, and potential trade setups.

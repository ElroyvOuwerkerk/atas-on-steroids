# Severin's 3 Candle Entry Strategy Refinement Part 2

## Portal Summary & Highlights

In this video, we dive deep into the momentum entry strategy, the most advanced method in the refined three candle entry system. We explore how to capitalize on fast momentum breaks when traditional COT (Change of Trend) or COC (Change of Context) setups aren't available, using the powerful 88% extreme quartile statistic combined with trapped trader dynamics to achieve potentially massive risk-to-reward ratios of 10:1 or higher.

### Timestamps

- [00:00] Introduction & Legal Disclaimer Overview of the three candle entry strategy refinement part two, covering utility, implementation, and tips. Important: Pause and accept the legal disclaimer before continuing.
- [00:49] Understanding Momentum in Trading
  - Momentum drives quick follow-through in price movements
  - Shows conviction and participation behind moves
  - Key insight: Momentum is often fueled by trapped participants
  - Most traders see momentum after it forms and miss the move
- [01:41] When to Use the Momentum Entry
  - Use when no clear three candle setup (COT or COC) is available
  - Priority order: COT first ? COC second ? Momentum entry third
  - Ideal for situations with too many conflicting signs
  - Example: Fading a massive downtrend where the first confirmation isn't trustworthy
- [02:39] Red Flag Example: Trading Into Previous Demand Zones Detailed NASDAQ example showing why every three candle setup isn't worth taking when longing into a previous demand zone - one of the key red flags to avoid.
- [03:27] Identifying Momentum Early - Three Key Factors
  - Understanding naked price action
  - Utilizing the extreme quartile statistic: Opens in upper/lower 25% of previous candle = 88% probability of follow-through
  - Capitalizing on trapped traders closing positions
- [04:08] Demand Break Analysis - Seller Control Dynamics Visual breakdown of downtrend structure: lower highs, lower lows, demand flipping to supply. Understanding when sellers maintain control vs. when the market shifts.
- [05:02] The Trap Mechanism Explained How short sellers become trapped when:
  - Breakout candle closes in upper quartile
  - Winning positions turn to losing positions
  - Forced buying fuels momentum to the upside
- [05:46] Multiple Confirmations Stack Simultaneous signals: break of structure + break of valid supply zone (3+ tests) + upper quartile close = 88% probability setup. Entry taken on the retest candle.
- [06:27] Short Trade Momentum Entry Example Identifying previous demand zones (4 touches minimum), waiting for breakout candle closing in lower quartile, entering on retest with local stop loss.
- [07:17] Extreme Quartile Statistic Deep Dive
  - 88% probability of next candle follow-through when closing in extreme quartile
  - Combined with supply/demand zone breaks = trapped traders
  - Visual example of supply zone, breakout candle, and retest entry
- [09:05] Criteria for Momentum Entry - Complete Checklist
  - Long trade: Break of supply zone + close in upper quartile
  - Short trade: Break of demand zone + close in lower quartile
  - Entry: Retest of the next candle
  - Stop loss: Just below/above the retest candle (local stop)
- [09:49] Drawing Supply/Demand Boxes Correctly Include the most amount of wicks while keeping boxes as small as possible but as big as needed. Example showing 8 important wicks included in one small box.
- [11:24] Live Example: No COT or COC Given Beautiful example hitting predefined level with no reversal candles - momentum entry becomes the solution with 5 demand touches, lower quartile close, and retest entry.
- [12:12] Stop Loss Strategy for Momentum Entries
  - Local stop loss only - not overall highs/lows
  - Quick follow-through assumption allows tight stops
  - Trailing stop loss strategy to box in price
  - Don't limit to small R&Rs - aim for big moves
- [13:42] Achieving 10:1 Risk-to-Reward Ratios The momentum entry's tight stop loss enables very high R&R ratios, potentially 10:1 or higher. This allows for larger position sizes due to reduced risk.
- [14:06] Compounding Trades with Momentum Entry Strategy for adding to winning trades:
  - Take initial COT or COC entry
  - Add compound position on momentum break
  - Creates average entry with improved risk profile
- [14:59] NQ Compound Trade Example Key level hit ? COC entry taken ? Confident in bigger pullback ? Identify new demand zone (5 touches) ? Wait for lower quartile close ? Compound on momentum entry ? Trail stop loss or take partial profits.
- [15:46] Alternative Example: Standard Three Candle Not Ideal When setup doesn't qualify as COT (no upper quartile close) or COC - wait for momentum entry instead. Supply zone identification (5 touches), upper quartile breakout, retest entry, quick upside move.
- [16:47] Tips and Tricks Summary
  - Most advanced entry method - requires deep understanding of COT and COC first
  - Favorite entry for choppy price action around key levels
  - Use when needing more data to pull an entry
- [17:04] Next Steps & Q&A Resources Submit questions via Discord in sessions questions. Additional help available in the Trading Help Forum.

Ready to master momentum entries? Start by perfecting your COT and COC identification, then practice spotting supply/demand zones with 3+ touches and extreme quartile closes. Remember: preparation before momentum strikes is what separates profitable traders from those who chase moves!

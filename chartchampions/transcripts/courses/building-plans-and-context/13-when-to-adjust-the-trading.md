# How and when to modify your trading plan

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This lesson discusses the importance of modifying your daily trading plan when necessary, based on new market information and data. It covers the utility of a trading plan, reasons for modifying it, psychological traps to avoid, and steps to take when adjusting your plan. The key is to make data-driven decisions, not emotional ones, and to protect your capital for the next opportunity.

### Timestamps

- [00:29] The utility of a trading plan:
  - Gives structure to your trading day
  - Sets your bias, levels, risk, and triggers
  - Makes your process measurable and reviewable
  - Combines market context, key levels, entry triggers, and risk per trade
- [01:25] Why modification of the daily plan is necessary at times:
  - Markets are dynamic and evolve quickly, especially in crypto
  - New data can make the morning plan outdated by the New York Open
  - Modifying the plan is applying discipline to new information, not abandoning it
- [01:53] When to step away from the trading plan:
  - Unexpected market behavior, such as sudden impulses or news-driven moves
  - Invalidations have changed and become too extreme
  - Trading plan and correlations point in different directions
- [04:17] Major data releases (CPI, FOMC, NFP) can change everything in the market
  - Step away from entering new positions close to these events
  - Wait for structure to form after the event
- [04:40] Missing a trade is normal and not a bad thing
  - Another trade will come
  - Avoid taking bad trades out of FOMO
- [04:55] Psychological traps when adjusting daily plans:
  - Not adjusting because of ego and thinking you are right
  - Confirming bias or revenge trading under the guise of following the plan
- [05:20] What to do when adjusting the trading plan:
  - Update key levels and test their validity
  - Re-evaluate invalidation points
  - Reduce position size and wait for confirmation if invalidation is within risk management boundaries
- [06:07] Key points to remember:
  - Modify the plan based on new information, not emotion
  - Don't chase volatility; respond to structure and high-probability setups
  - There are three types of trades: long, short, and no trade (protecting capital)
  - The next setup will always come
- [07:53] Checklist for adjusting daily plans: If any of these are "yes," it's valid to step away from the plan and wait for more clarity and data.
  - Is the change data-driven or emotional?
  - Has volatility increased significantly?
  - Are key levels broken with conviction (e.g., high volume)?
  - Is there a major event incoming (CPI, FOMC, NFP)?

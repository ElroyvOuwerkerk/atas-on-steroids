# Daytrading Strategy

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session outlines a day trading strategy built around volume, momentum, and reactionary entries, combining TPO charts, open interest, Fibonacci confluence, and VWAP/Point of Control levels. We learn how to gauge market context, identify high-probability setups through confluence, and apply disciplined profit-taking using a real trade example.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the day trading strategy, which is reaction-based rather than using pre-set limit orders. Key requirement: patience, as trades are only taken when setups appear.
- [01:03] Core components of the strategy: Average expectation: 1Ð4 trades per day.
  - TPO charts
  - Open interest
  - Volume momentum
  - Fibonacci for confluence
- [01:57] Emphasis on not forcing trades. If no setup exists, remain patient Ñ some days may offer zero valid trades.
- [02:43] How volume measures the strength of a move: observing how quickly volume enters the market and the size of the candles is the primary way to gauge momentum.
- [03:42] Open interest reveals aggressiveness Ñ whether moves are driven by new longs, new shorts, or position closures. Critical for distinguishing genuine moves from unwinding.
- [04:10] Fibonacci confluence levels used:
  - 0.66 (CC) Ð primary level, especially in ranging conditions
  - 0.796 Ð effective in low-volume/ranging environments
  - 0.28 Ð useful for entries during high-momentum, trending days
- [05:19] Volume tools for entries: value areas, points of control, and VWAPs. TPO components: single prints and high time nodes as favorable zones.
- [05:40] The importance of reading context: avoid buying pullbacks during strong bearish momentum, and vice versa. There is no black-and-white rule Ñ context defines bias.
- [06:25] Identifying aggression using exocharts' net longs/net shorts highlighting. Upward moves should ideally be supported by aggressive long pushing; otherwise the move lacks conviction.
- [08:29] Trending day example: Pullback into VWAP + Daily Point of Control (also weekly POC) produced a strong long setup Ñ one of the most common high-probability confluences in this strategy.
- [09:23] Short setup example: Daily POC + previous day's value area low + session VWAP confluence in a downtrending market. Key observations:
  - Large candles on the counter-trend move up
  - Liquidations visible on the push
  - Open interest declining = short squeeze characteristics
  - Multiple rejections at confluence zone
- [11:07] Live trade walkthrough: Short taken at VWAP/POC/previous day value area low confluence, targeting the local CC (0.66 Fib). Full position closed at target Ñ no greed, no partial holds.
- [13:08] Trade management review: Fibs drawn from low to high, target hit precisely at the 0.66 CC level, demonstrating disciplined execution.
- [14:25] Exit signal confirmation: Bullish CVD divergence (higher low in price, lower low in CVD) at the CC level warned against re-shorting the next attempt Ñ illustrating how CVD divergences support exit decisions.
- [15:08] Overview of the integrated template: TPO, volume profile, developing profiles, delta profile (2-week lookback), CVD divergences, open interest, net longs/shorts, and high/low metrics for measuring average daily activity.
- [16:33] No trading strategy is universally perfect. Strategies must be adapted to individual experience, availability, and style.
- [17:22] Summary of the strategy:
  - Momentum gives trade direction
  - Volume provides entries
  - Fibonacci gives confluence
  - Context ties it all together Ñ e.g., recognizing when declining OI during an upward move reflects a short squeeze rather than weakness

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, practicing them in context, and applying strict risk management before implementing any strategy in live markets.

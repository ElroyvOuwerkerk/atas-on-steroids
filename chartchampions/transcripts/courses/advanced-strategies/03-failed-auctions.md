# Failed Auctions

## Portal Summary & Highlights

In this comprehensive session, we dive deep into the concept of failed auctions Ð a powerful trading setup that helps identify major signs of market weakness or strength. We'll explore how failed auctions provide high risk-to-reward ratio trades with clear entry triggers, stop-loss placements, and profit targets. Unlike swing failure patterns, failed auctions involve spending more time beyond a key level before reclaiming it, which traps more traders and typically results in greater follow-through on trades.

### Timestamps:

- [00:00:18] Session Overview Introduction to the three main components covered: utility, implementation, and tips and tricks for trading failed auctions.
- [00:00:30] The Power of Failed Auctions - Key Utility Three major advantages of failed auctions:
  - Ability to recognize major signs of weakness or strength
  - Provides high risk-to-reward ratio trade setups
  - Offers easy entry triggers and clear invalidation points
- [01:20] Failed Auctions vs. Swing Failure Patterns Key distinction explained: Swing failure patterns involve quick moves up/down and immediate reversal, while failed auctions involve spending more time beyond the level before reversing back into the range.
- [02:42] Implementation: Bearish Failed Auction Setup Step-by-step breakdown:
  - Identify sideways price action within a well-defined range
  - Wait for price to break above the range high
  - Look for increased open interest and volume during the breakout (ideal scenario)
  - Confirm failed auction when price comes back down into the range
  - Execute trade with stop-loss above the new high made
- [04:48] Take Profit Targets Strategy Multiple take profit options discussed:
  - TP1: Last low made before the high, or the CC (Current Candle) Fibonacci level
  - TP2: Range point of control, overall range low, or next major monthly level
  - Consider using fixed range volume profile for additional target identification
- [06:33] Bullish Failed Auction Example (Non-Range Scenario) Demonstrates that failed auctions don't require range-bound conditions:
  - Identify key levels with SR levels and liquidity
  - Look for stop-loss clusters below visible lows
  - Wait for price to spend time below the low, then reclaim
- [08:06] Order Flow Analysis for Confirmation Using ATus Exo Charts to identify:
  - Bullish CVD divergences Ð price making higher lows while CVD makes lower lows
  - Open interest increases at the lows
  - These signals provide additional confirmation for stronger follow-through
- [09:54] Trade Management Strategies After hitting TP1, three options: Important consideration: If stopped out after moving to profits, opportunity exists to re-enter at better prices.
  - Move stop-loss to entry (breakeven)
  - Move stop-loss into profits
  - Keep original stop-loss for larger targets
- [11:25] Tips and Tricks for Success Essential guidelines:
  - Take profit is always context-dependent Ð VWAP, CC, or range POC can all be valid targets
  - Preferred timeframes: 30-minute and 4-hour charts (30-min aligns with TPO charts)
  - Higher timeframes = more follow-through expected
  - Failed auctions trap more traders than swing failure patterns, resulting in better moves
- [12:56] Entry Execution Best Practice Market orders preferred over limit orders after failed auction confirmation:
  - Moves following failed auctions are typically quick
  - Limit orders carry high risk of not getting filled
  - "Pay the price of doing business" to secure the trade

Start applying these failed auction concepts by practicing identification on your charts, and consider paper trading the setups before committing real capital. The key is recognizing the difference between quick swing failure patterns and the more powerful failed auctions that trap additional traders for enhanced follow-through!

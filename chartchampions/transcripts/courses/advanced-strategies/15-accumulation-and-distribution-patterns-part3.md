# Accumulation and Distribution Patterns: Part 3

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session reviews the reaccumulation and redistribution schematics as part of a broader market cycle analysis, with Bitcoin used as the primary case study. We examine how to identify these ranges, confirm directional bias through order flow and market structure, and avoid common traps where typical schematics mislead traders. The discussion emphasizes integrating schematics with harmonics, delta, CVD, and liquidity concepts to achieve consistent, well-defined trade entries.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the full market cycle: accumulation ? markup (price explosion) ? reaccumulation ? distribution ? markdown (price implosion) ? redistribution, using Bitcoin's move from under $20,000 to $69,000 as a clear example.
- [01:22] Multiple reaccumulations can occur during a bull trend, not just one. Each range represents a pause before continuation to the upside.
- [02:02] Identifying distribution: price reaches major resistance and begins showing signs of weakness and market structure changes, signaling the transition to markdown.
- [02:47] Several redistribution ranges typically form on the way down, each followed by another leg lower, until signs of strength appear near a major low.
- [03:20] Definitions:
  - Reaccumulation Ð sideways range after markup, resolving upward on continued demand.
  - Redistribution Ð sideways range after markdown, resolving downward.
- [04:16] How to trade a reaccumulation range: mark the range high and range low, maintain a bullish bias, but still trade both directions within the range (longs at range low, shorts at range high) until breakout.
- [05:40] Confirming reaccumulation requires monitoring market structure, CVD, delta, open interest, and breakout volume. Bias alone is not enough Ñ acknowledge fakeout possibilities.
- [06:30] Simplified approach: during reaccumulation and redistribution, individual Wyckoff labels (spring, test, UTAD, etc.) are not marked out Ñ the focus is on the range and overall bias rather than micro-labeling.
- [07:05] Redistribution example after Bitcoin's $69,000 rejection: identifying both the overall range low and a sub-range low when extreme wicks distort the structure.
- [09:14] Bearish bias during redistribution does not exclude long trades inside the range Ñ bias guides setup selection but ranges still get traded both ways.
- [10:05] Key invalidation rules:
  - Reaccumulation is invalidated if the range low is lost with high negative delta and market structure turns bearish.
  - Redistribution is invalidated if the range high is reclaimed with high positive delta.
- [11:42] The intricacies of trading: nothing in trading is black and white. Even perfect-looking schematics can fail Ñ which is why risk management is essential.
- [13:35] Schematics give high-probability directional bias, never certainty. Always combine them with order flow, market structure, and awareness of market maker behavior.
- [14:55] Example of a market trap: a redistribution range on Bitcoin coincided with a bearish Gartley harmonic. Traders fixated only on schematics could mistake the pattern for accumulation (low ? higher low ? higher high) and get trapped at resistance.
- [17:50] Lesson: layering tools (harmonics + schematics + order flow + market structure) gives a far more robust edge than relying on any single tool.
- [19:04] Trade execution logic: wait for a swing failure pattern / failed auction at the range high to confirm the short. If the range breaks out cleanly on strong volume and delta, the bias was wrong Ñ no trade is taken. Knowing when not to trade is a core driver of high win rates.
- [20:19] Concept of late shorts: avoid shorting into support after a sharp move down. Instead, short the rallies and lower highs within the range where invalidation is tight and defined.
- [21:40] Characteristics of a high-quality short in redistribution:
  - Bearish higher-timeframe bias
  - Bearish divergences on rallies
  - Consolidation above support (bearish)
  - Fakeout above local highs
  - Bearish CVD confirmation
- [22:42] Market structure trumps everything in range trading: a low-volume break of range low that continues to produce lower lows and lower highs should not be longed just because the breakout volume was weak.
- [23:48] Higher-timeframe distribution example on Bitcoin: AR ? ST ? UTAD at $69,000 ? Last Point of Supply ? Sign of Weakness breakdown. Invalidation defined as a reclaim back into the prior range (above ~$30,000).

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, practicing them on charts, and always applying strict risk management when integrating schematics with order flow and market structure.

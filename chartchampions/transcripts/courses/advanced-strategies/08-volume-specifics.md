# Volume Specifics

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session explores how to identify high and low liquidity environments using volume candles, and how to adapt technical analysis accordingly. We learn how candle size, trade count, CVD divergences, and open interest can be used to decide whether to fade a move or follow it, with emphasis on aligning entries with the behavior of larger market participants and key levels of confluence.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the core concept: identifying whether the market is in a high liquidity or low liquidity environment, and why the chosen strategy or technical setup should change based on this distinction.
- [00:29] Framework for using volume candles to assess liquidity by analyzing candle highs, lows, and the time printed, especially during breakouts or fade trades (counter-breakout setups).
- [00:49] Identifying fade opportunities when price breaks through key levels such as: Large candle wicks beyond these levels often indicate a fake out.
  - Point of control
  - Value area highs and lows
  - Monthly and weekly levels
  - Fibonacci 0.618 confluences
- [01:35] Example of a low liquidity environment: on a 5M volume chart, large candles indicate thin order books. A swing failure pattern or liquidation run occurs, with price rapidly moving on minimal transactions.
- [02:36] Discussion of order block retests: price returning to the last area of movement before a strong impulse, showing how liquidity conditions differ on the retest versus the initial move.
- [03:38] Inverse scenario: many small volume candles signal a high liquidity environment. When price approaches a key level with compact candles and the CVD diverges, it indicates larger traders absorbing orders.
- [04:46] Key principle for CVD traders: CVD divergences work best in high liquidity environments. Divergences in low liquidity conditions tend to be unreliable, which explains why many traders struggle with inconsistent CVD signals.
- [05:29] Combining open interest buildup with bearish CVD divergence at resistance to identify high-probability sell-offs, confirmed by sustained acceptance down through levels on small candles.
- [06:16] Importance of trade count analysis:
  - Low trade count with large volume candle = large single player executing size
  - Following the footprints of larger participants rather than retail flow
  - Rapid moves on low trade counts signal institutional positioning
- [08:17] Example showing price rising on large candles, consolidating sideways, forming bearish CVD divergence, then selling off on a low trade count Ñ a signal to shift from buying dips to shorting rallies.
- [09:10] Practical takeaway: mastering liquidity recognition allows us to decide whether to fade or join a move, providing a structural edge over participants unaware of these dynamics.
- [10:07] Summary of key points:
  - Candle size on volume charts reflects liquidity
  - CVD is most reliable in high liquidity conditions
  - Low liquidity moves are generally fade candidates
  - Follow the larger market participants, not retail flow
  - Context is essential Ñ signals must occur at meaningful levels with proper confluence

We encourage reviewing these liquidity-based concepts carefully and integrating them with disciplined risk management and proper contextual analysis before applying them in live markets.

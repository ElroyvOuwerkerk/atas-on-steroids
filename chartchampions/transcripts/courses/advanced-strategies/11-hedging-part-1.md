# Hedging: Part 1

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session focuses on hedging strategies in trading, primarily applied to Bitcoin and cryptocurrency markets. We explore how hedging reduces downside risk on spot holdings by taking reverse positions, how leverage can be used efficiently to fully hedge a portfolio with minimal exchange exposure, and how hedge mode on exchanges allows simultaneous long and short positions. The discussion also includes practical trade examples involving range trading, swing failure patterns, market structure changes, and trailing stop management.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to hedging: a method used to reduce losses on an asset during a downtrend, primarily by taking a reverse position against an existing open trade.
- [00:53] Focus on cryptocurrency markets, while noting that hedging principles apply across all tradable markets.
- [01:10] Key benefits of hedging:
  - Reducing or eliminating dollar losses during downtrends.
  - Providing peace of mind during volatility or when away from the trading screen.
  - Allowing positions to remain open longer (e.g., long from a range low while shorting at resistance).
  - Helping secure profit on existing trades.
  - Potential consideration for taxable events when avoiding closing long-term positions.
- [02:22] Trade-off discussion: hedging reduces risk but can also reduce potential gains. Each trader must define their goals Ñ day trading, swing trading, or position trading Ñ and whether they aim to accumulate more Bitcoin or stablecoins.
- [03:31] Example 1 Ñ Fully hedged position: Owning 1 spot Bitcoin at $10,000 and holding 1 short Bitcoin on an exchange. If price rises 10%, portfolio value remains unchanged Ñ no gain, no loss.
- [04:43] Example 2 Ñ Unhedged position: Owning 1 spot Bitcoin with no hedge. A 10% drop results in a 10% portfolio value loss.
- [05:39] Requirements for hedging: holding spot Bitcoin plus stablecoins or Bitcoin on the exchange. Stablecoin options such as USDT and USDC can be used to open hedge shorts.
- [06:05] Using leverage advantageously for hedging: Holding 1 Bitcoin offline and only 0.1 Bitcoin on the exchange with 10x leverage can replicate the equivalent short value of 1 Bitcoin, achieving a 1:1 hedge while minimizing exchange exposure.
- [07:55] Hedge mode on Bybit: Enables holding both long and short positions simultaneously on the same contract, reducing the need for multiple accounts. Multiple accounts may still be useful for separating strategies by timeframe (e.g., scalp vs swing).
- [09:08] Practical example Ñ Range trading with hedge:
  - Long taken at the bottom of the range from weekly support after a retest and market structure change.
  - Targets: range point of control as TP1, top of range as next area.
  - At resistance, monitor for a swing failure pattern on the daily as a potential short entry.
- [09:57] Reasoning for keeping the long open while taking the hedge short: price may reject, retrace to point of control, and continue upward. Take partial profit at resistance but keep a portion of the long running.
- [11:03] Position sizing for hedges: A 1:1 hedge must include both on-exchange and offline holdings. Higher leverage (e.g., 5x) can be used if the trader wants to profit from the drop, not just neutralize losses.
- [11:42] Confluence example for shorts:
  - CME gap filled at daily resistance.
  - Trapped longs into the high.
  - Market structure change to the downside.
  - Failed auction above the high (sweep and reclaim below).
- [12:37] Trade management: Trailing the stop loss on the long into profit, or waiting for a clearer sign of weakness in order flow before closing.
- [13:33] Psychology of preferring leveraged shorts: with a large spot portfolio, leveraged shorts protect against dollar losses during drops while allowing additional Bitcoin accumulation on the downside.
- [14:38] Why hedge shorts can be taken at "riskier" levels: a stopped-out hedge short results in no net loss when spot positions continue to appreciate.
- [15:25] Holding winning short trades longer than longs and waiting for significant signs of strength before closing Ñ applying the same logic to large hedge positions (e.g., the 69,000 short).
- [16:25] Summary of key points:
  - Hedging reduces risk during downward moves.
  - Primary form: shorting at 1:1 spot portfolio value.
  - Account for both exchange and offline holdings when sizing.
  - Other hedging methods include options trading (puts) and diversification into other assets and safe havens.
  - Leveraged shorts can serve dual purposes Ñ hedging and gaining additional value on downside moves.

We encourage viewers to carefully review these hedging concepts, define their personal trading goals, and apply position sizing and leverage with disciplined risk management.

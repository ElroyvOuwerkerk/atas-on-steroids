# Support and Resistance

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session introduces support and resistance as foundational tools in technical analysis, explaining how to correctly mark horizontal levels across multiple timeframes and how trend lines differ in validity. We review the distinction between marking levels on cryptocurrency charts versus stock market charts due to price gaps, along with practical charting tips to maintain clarity and reduce bias.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to support and resistance as the fundamentals of trading and essential daily tools used in technical analysis.
- [00:47] Why support and resistance, although considered basic, are among the strongest and most reliable tools available.
- [01:01] Implementation approach: Always begin marking levels on the higher timeframes (e.g., monthly) and work down toward the daily timeframe.
- [01:18] Trend line rules:
  - Two touches are required to draw a trend line.
  - The third touch is a speculative entry.
  - A confirmed trend line requires validation after the third touch.
- [02:04] Horizontal levels are more objective than trend lines, which can be subjective depending on whether wicks or candle closes are used.
- [02:41] Reference to Elliott Wave theory as the preferred framework for drawing accurate trend lines.
- [02:45] Level weakness principle: The more times a level is tested, the weaker it becomes, as liquidity (e.g., resting sell orders) gradually depletes with each test, often leading to an eventual breakout.
- [03:21] Color-coded level system explained: monthly in purple, weekly in yellow, daily in blue, with higher timeframe levels considered stronger.
- [03:37] Rules for marking horizontal levels:
  - For resistance: look for a green close followed by a red close.
  - For support: look for a red close followed by a green close.
- [03:59] Differences between cryptocurrency and stock market levels: Crypto trades 24/7, so closes and opens align, while stock markets often leave gaps between sessions.
- [05:00] Handling stock market gaps:
  - For resistance, select the higher of the close/open prices.
  - For support, select the lower of the close/open prices.
- [06:03] Chart cleanliness tip: Hide distant levels and keep only relevant, nearby levels visible to avoid clutter and improve decision-making clarity.
- [06:55] Bias management technique: Use Alt + I on TradingView to invert the chart, which can help remove a fixed directional bias by offering a fresh perspective.
- [07:29] Mastering TradingView is highlighted as a fundamental skill since it is the primary platform for marking out all technical analysis levels.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully and applying them with disciplined practice and proper risk management to build a strong technical foundation.

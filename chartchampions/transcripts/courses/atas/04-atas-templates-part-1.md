# ATAS Templates Part 1

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session introduces the first part of a templates series designed to build an efficient trading workspace. We review six core templates condensed from years of refinement, covering order flow, sessions, classic TPO, liquidity pivots, VWAPs, CCTR ranges, and cluster stacking. The discussion explains how to combine time-based and volume-based profiles, naked points of control, single prints, VWAP closes, CVD, open interest, and delta to make informed, multi-confluence decisions across scalp, day, and swing trading.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the templates series and the rationale for using prepared templates to handle any market volatility with confluences such as naked session POC, VWAP closes, single prints, and naked weekly POC.
- [02:30] Overview of the six condensed templates:
  - Order Flow
  - Sessions
  - Classic TPO
  - Liquidity Pivots and VWAPs
  - CCTR (Trend Reversal and Clusters)
  - Stacking Balances with Daily/Weekly/Monthly levels
- [03:55] Order Flow Template: combines the daily TPO and volume profile, showing value area highs/lows, time POC (orange) and volume POC (gray), VPOCs, and naked POCs.
- [05:07] VWAP closes explained as soft flip levels Ñ daily VWAP (white) and weekly VWAP (yellow). Single prints marked in blue act as inefficiency levels that price often revisits.
- [05:52] Daily and weekly OHLC levels, plus the daily open (green line) as equilibrium, and the previous day 50% (equilibrium) as a reference for intraday context.
- [06:27] Depth of market, CVD (cumulative volume delta), open interest, and footprint statistics overview. Session delta values help confirm absorption and divergence beyond visual CVD.
- [07:33] Cluster view with delta profile, bid/ask imbalances (purple = bid, blue = ask) set at 350% threshold, and red-bordered volume POCs to quickly identify trapped traders.
- [09:47] Recommended chart settings: use volume-based candles (500k, 1M, 5M, 10M) depending on volatility; adjust tick size proportionally Ñ smaller for low volatility, larger for high volatility.
- [10:59] Critical setting: use at least 15 days of look-back data for accurate weekly levels, and 30/60/90 days for monthly levels. Insufficient look-back produces incorrect levels.
- [13:00] Combining TPO letters with volume profile in a single view to validate point of control confluence; example of VWAP touch combined with a lower-low CVD divergence as a confluence entry.
- [14:07] Previous daily VWAP close as a reliable bounce level Ñ historically produces 1-2% reactive moves; serves as a star level (short if above, long if below).
- [15:08] Footprint statistics: session delta, open interest, and delta bar tops/closes for spotting divergences at highs and lows.
- [16:16] Current price and time display utility for tracking countdowns into session opens or news events without losing focus.
- [17:38] Sessions Template: yellow markers indicate session opens and closes. Used for trend-following, intraday levels, naked session VPOCs, and single prints.
- [18:48] Sessions delta visualization with hidden candles mode shows aggregate delta per session to identify directional control by bulls or bears.
- [19:54] Distinction between dynamic current session POCs (still forming, will shift) and previous session POCs (true support/resistance).
- [21:46] Classic Template: higher-timeframe levels including daily, weekly, monthly TPO and volume profiles Ñ color-coded as daily (orange/gray), weekly (yellow/gray), monthly (purple/gray).
- [23:11] Single prints on weekly/monthly profiles are shown as small dots on the profile rather than lines to reduce chart clutter.
- [24:03] Tick size guidance for the classic template: use tick 1 for accurate level discovery, then increase to 10-100 for cleaner visual price levels during volatile periods.
- [25:46] Volume profile vs. TPO confluence: when both align at the same level, the value area high/low becomes a stronger zone of interest.
- [27:00] Volume traded readouts per profile (e.g., 21M total session volume) provide context for the strength of a level.
- [28:48] Selecting POCs by trade type: swing trades require higher-volume POCs; scalp trades can use smaller-volume POCs. Always pair with another confluence.
- [30:25] Using naked POCs as bias indicators: when all naked POCs are stacked to one side, this can signal a directional liquidity target; price tends to reach areas of unfinished business and resting orders.

We encourage reviewing each template carefully, applying these confluences in context, and combining them with disciplined risk management before integrating them into a live trading plan.

# CVD Tutorial

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This video provides an in-depth tutorial on Cumulative Volume Delta (CVD) divergences, a powerful tool for identifying where larger traders are positioning with limit orders to absorb market order flow. Viewers will learn how to spot bullish and bearish CVD divergences, distinguish high-quality setups from weak ones, and use confirmation techniques like market structure breaks and swing failure patterns to time entries effectively.

### Detailed Timestamps:

- [00:00] Introduction to CVD divergences Ð CVD stands for Cumulative Volume Delta, the running total of delta. The core thesis: bigger money enters with limit orders, and divergences reveal when these traders are absorbing opposing market orders.
- [00:30] How CVD is calculated Ð Each candle's delta is added/subtracted to create a running total. Divergences should build over multiple candles, not from a single wick or stop run, since CVD uses the whole candle.
- [01:12] CVD settings explained Ð Full data doesn't reset and is preferred for divergence analysis. Session resets daily (rarely used). Visible range calculates only what's on screen and can also be useful.
- [03:00] Bullish CVD divergence breakdown Ð Price forms a higher low while CVD forms a lower low, indicating aggressive market selling that isn't pushing price down. This means a larger trader is absorbing sells with limit buy orders.
- [04:14] Real chart example on Sierra Charts Ð Demonstrates higher lows on price with continuously lower lows on CVD across a higher timeframe, signaling strong absorption by bigger capital.
- [05:00] Why trade with the bigger hand Ð Comparing to stock market dynamics (e.g., not fighting the Fed). Aggressive shorts that fail to push price down often get squeezed, leading to upside moves.
- [05:30] Bearish CVD divergence explained Ð Price makes a lower high while CVD makes a higher high, indicating aggressive market buying being absorbed by limit sell orders from larger traders.
- [06:55] Multi-step divergences Ð Identifying both high-timeframe and local bearish divergences stacking together, creating high-probability short setups.
- [08:00] Avoid quick wick divergences Ð Single-candle wick divergences (V-shaped recoveries) are weak and shouldn't be traded. Look for absorption that develops over multiple candles.
- [09:30] Possible vs. confirmed divergences Ð A "possible" divergence becomes "confirmed" when price actually moves away from the level after divergences form, signaling trapped traders.
- [10:30] Identifying trapped traders with delta Ð Use delta on candlesticks to gauge how much aggressive volume bought or sold into a level. High delta into highs that fail = many trapped breakout traders.
- [12:20] Watch out for delta leading price Ð Sometimes apparent divergences resolve with price following delta upward instead. Context is critical Ð not every divergence will play out.
- [13:30] Building a complete trading case Ð Combine CVD with rotation timing, delta volume, market structure, and other technical confluences. Volume tools should complement, not replace, your full toolkit.
- [15:00] Confirmation via market structure break Ð Once the low that led up to the high breaks, that's confirmation to short the next retrace. Larger confirmation = breaking the entire range low.
- [17:00] Multi-timeframe applicability Ð CVD divergences work on any timeframe Ð higher timeframes generally produce stronger signals, but the same tools apply for scalping, swing, and position trading.
- [17:50] Swing failure pattern example Ð High taken on weak volume and weak delta, followed by a candle with high volume and strong negative delta = textbook swing failure. Short with stop above the high.
- [19:30] Open interest as added confluence Ð Check if highs were taken on a decrease in net longs and increase in net shorts to further validate a swing failure setup.
- [20:00] Why divergences initially form Ð Limit orders sit at range highs expecting reversals. When buying interest exceeds what those limits can absorb (with proper context), price squeezes upward.
- [20:40] Faster confirmation technique Ð Don't always wait for full breakdown. If the candle attempting the break shows large negative delta while failing, that itself is strong confirmation to enter early.

Take action: Start applying CVD divergence analysis to your charts by combining it with market structure, delta readings, and contextual confluences. Practice spotting the difference between weak wick-based divergences and multi-candle absorption setups to dramatically improve your entry quality and trade conviction.

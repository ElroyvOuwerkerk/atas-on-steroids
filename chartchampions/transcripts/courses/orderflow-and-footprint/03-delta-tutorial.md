# Delta Tutorial

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session provides a detailed exploration of Delta as a trading tool, explaining the distinction between passive and aggressive order flow, how Delta is calculated from the footprint chart, and how it can be applied to interpret breakouts, ranges, and Delta clusters. We learn how to assess whether the market is doing a good job of moving in its intended direction by combining Delta with volume, market structure, and additional confluences such as prior day value areas and open interest.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to Delta and why individual interpretation matters more than generic definitions.
- [00:42] Passive vs. Aggressive Order Flow:
  - Passive orders = limit orders placed in the book (may or may not fill).
  - Aggressive/active orders = market orders that guarantee a fill and actually move price.
  - Markets only move because active orders consume passive liquidity.
- [02:29] Definition of Delta: the sum of aggressive trades (market buys minus market sells), used to identify who is in control at a candle or price level.
- [03:16] How to read Delta on a footprint chart:
  - Example calculation: 667k buys ? 1M sells = ?351k Delta.
  - Positive Delta example: 2.8M buys ? 229k sells = +2.6M.
- [05:30] Reading daily candle Delta: a green candle can still carry negative Delta, revealing inverse signals that may be useful for strategy development.
- [07:00] Two key questions before entering any trade:
  - What direction is the higher time frame market trying to go? (Assessed via market structure Ñ higher highs/lows or lower highs/lows.)
  - Is it doing a good job of going that way? (Assessed via Delta and volume.)
- [08:36] Bullish vs. bearish confirmation:
  - Price up + rising positive Delta/volume = healthy trend.
  - Price up + falling Delta/volume = bearish divergence, likely reversal.
- [10:00] Delta Clusters Ñ chart zones with concentrated aggressive activity:
  - Heavy green at highs = trapped buyers; expect rejection on revisit.
  - Heavy selling at lows = trapped sellers; expect bounce on revisit.
  - Only valid on untested clusters Ñ once price passes through multiple times, participants have likely exited.
- [14:00] Case study: Confluence of a heavy Delta cluster with the previous day value area high produced a precise short entry within a few dollars of the high.
- [18:00] Delta on breakouts:
  - Healthy breakout: increasing positive Delta + rising volume shows new active buyers supporting price.
  - Breakout on low Delta/volume = bearish, often a fake-out or swing failure.
  - Same logic applies inversely to breakdowns.
- [20:54] Concept of acceptance: price only establishes a new range if the breakout is validated by volume and Delta.
- [22:30] Example of a failed breakout: multiple attempts with progressively lower volume and Delta positioned at candle midpoints rather than at the breakout zone Ñ signaling exhaustion.
- [26:00] Visual recognition on footprint charts: thick zones indicate heavy transaction, while thin zones indicate little participation and higher reversal probability.
- [27:20] P-shaped and b-shaped bid-ask profiles as visual confirmations of aggressive buying or selling into highs/lows.
- [29:40] Why Delta beats arbitrary candle closes:
  - Waiting for a 15-minute or 4-hour close can cause missed trades.
  - Delta and open interest give real-time confirmation of acceptance or rejection.
- [33:40] Delta inside ranges:
  - Weak negative Delta at highs = sellers uninterested; range likely to break up.
  - Weak positive Delta at lows = buyers uninterested; range likely to break down.
  - Strong buying at lows + weak selling at highs = bullish range bias.
- [35:40] Range example on Bitcoin:
  - Initial sell-off on heavy volume/negative Delta established the range.
  - Failed break of lows closed with +27M Delta ? signal to look for a long back toward the point of control or range high.
  - Failed break of highs closed with ?29M Delta ? signal to short back toward range low.
- [41:30] Introduction to CVD divergence (Cumulative Volume Delta) Ñ e.g., high Delta at the lows without a new low signals buyer control and potential bullish rotation.
- [43:40] Range breakout checklist: compare the Delta of the breakout candle to the largest candles inside the range Ñ the breakout should exceed them, with Delta concentrated at the breakout zone, not the midpoint.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, backtesting them on your own charts, and applying them only with proper confluence and sound risk management.

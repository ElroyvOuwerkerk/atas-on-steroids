# Imbalances Part 1

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session explores how to read and trade market imbalances using order flow and footprint data. We cover the mechanics of buying and selling imbalances, stacked imbalances, absorption at range highs and lows, swing failure patterns, and how to use these tools to time entries at key reference levels rather than relying on preset bids.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the concept that price moves in one direction until one side is overwhelmed by the other. Price rallies until new longs stop opening or shorts overwhelm longs, and vice versa on the sell side.
- [00:47] Definition of imbalances: when volume traded at a price level shows proportionally more buyers or sellers. Using the Fibonacci 618 ratio (or roughly 400%+ difference) to highlight imbalances on the footprint chart.
- [01:22] Imbalances appear in a diagonal/stepped pattern, not horizontally. Practical examples of reading buy vs sell volume ratios on the footprint (e.g. 1.7M bought vs 1,000 sold).
- [02:41] Stacked imbalances explained: three or more imbalances on top of one another, signaling strong aggression. The more stacked and clustered in an area, the stronger the level.
- [03:37] Interpreting market reactions after stacked imbalances:
  - Aggressive buyers typically produce a rally
  - Aggressive sellers typically produce a sell-off
  - If the market fails to move as expected, the contrary move is likely magnified due to absorption by limit orders
- [05:40] Stacked imbalances can act as future support and resistance levels when revisited, as aggressive participants may return to defend those zones.
- [06:38] Two key contexts to focus on imbalances: grinding markets and breaks of important levels. When breaking down, look for heavy selling imbalances to confirm continuation; without them, fading the move may be preferable.
- [08:48] Trading breakouts vs fades: if a level breaks with heavy selling imbalances but price reclaims the level with buying imbalances, this signals trapped shorts and often leads to strong reversals Ñ the foundation of the swing failure pattern.
- [11:09] Absorption at range lows: heavy selling imbalances with price barely moving down indicates a limit buyer absorbing sell orders. A subsequent market structure break to the upside confirms a bullish reversal.
- [13:23] Absorption at range highs: heavy buying at market with price failing to move up indicates limit sellers absorbing. Wait for a local market structure break before entering shorts to avoid premature entries.
- [15:39] Bearish setup with wick volume: high volume and stacked buying imbalances contained within the upper wick followed by a close lower, then confirmed by selling imbalances, signals trapped longs and downside continuation.
- [18:00] Swing failure pattern criteria:
  - Reference level taken on high volume
  - Aggressive selling into the wick
  - Price retraces back above with aggressive opposite-side buying imbalances
  - Monitor open interest and volume as confirmation
- [19:53] Trading imbalances at major reference levels (weekly, monthly, key confluences). Rather than pre-setting bids, use alerts and wait for evidence Ñ absorption, trapped traders, or aggressive buying Ñ before entering.
- [22:20] Four practical trigger scenarios for long entries:
  - Slow grind down with aggressive shorts at each low, then buying imbalances appear
  - Price reaches a key liquidity level and prints buying imbalances
  - Price wicks through a level and is immediately bought up with stacked buying imbalances
  - Local market structure break triggering a short squeeze on a bleed day
- [24:39] On slow bleed days, avoid buying premature bounces on low volume with no buying imbalances. Wait for heavy volume and stacked buying imbalances at a reference point to confirm a change in direction.
- [25:53] Live example: heavy selling imbalances into a low, price reclaims the level, buying imbalances confirm the reversal Ñ leading to a significant rally.
- [26:38] Second example: trading below a reference level with selling imbalances followed by buying imbalances, higher lows, and eventual breakout with stacked buying imbalances producing sustained upside.

We encourage viewers to study these imbalance concepts carefully and combine them with market structure and reference levels, always applying disciplined risk management before acting on any setup.

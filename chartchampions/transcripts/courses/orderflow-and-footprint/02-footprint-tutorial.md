# Footprint Tutorial

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session provides an introduction to footprint charts and how they can be used to analyze market activity in greater depth than standard candlestick charts. We explore how footprint charts reveal where volume occurs within a candle, how to interpret buying versus selling interest at key levels, how to identify high-probability breakouts and swing failure patterns, and how to use volume clusters and imbalances for support, resistance, and trade planning.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to footprint charts: understanding that they allow us to look inside a candle to see volume transacted at each price, offering significantly more depth than standard OHLC candlesticks.
- [01:05] Example of a bullish hammer candle: why it can be misleading. The close of a candle does not necessarily indicate where the largest volume occurred, and footprint data helps avoid misinterpreting candlestick patterns.
- [02:11] Walkthrough of how a large limit sell wall and market orders can create a wick that appears bullish on a standard chart, but reveals different intent when analyzed with footprint volume data.
- [04:11] Using footprint charts to identify acceptance above or below key levels Ñ a capability that candlestick charts alone cannot provide. This is essential for interpreting support, resistance, and range behavior.
- [05:50] Introduction to the bid/ask profile: observing that repeated failed breakdowns on low sell volume indicate weak selling interest and lean toward a bullish bias.
- [07:00] Explanation of how to read sell volume (left) vs. buy volume (right) inside each price row within a candle, and how to compare aggregated participation.
- [08:30] Interpreting a wick low: when buying volume dominates sell volume at the lows, this suggests demand exceeds supply and price is likely to auction back up.
- [09:20] Setup tips for footprint chart tick sizes:
  - Use smaller tick sizes (e.g., 2 ticks) for day trading
  - Use larger tick sizes for higher timeframes to cluster levels into meaningful zones
  - Experiment to find what suits your trading style
- [11:00] Switching between volume view and bid/ask view Ñ the bid/ask view provides more actionable data by splitting buyers and sellers at each price.
- [12:40] Importance of the point of control (POC) within a candle: a POC at the low with heavy buying after a support break suggests likely reversal to the upside.
- [14:35] Practical application: use footprint analysis at key levels (range highs/lows, Fibonacci levels, support/resistance), not continuously during low-activity sideways markets.
- [15:40] Analyzing a failed breakout at range highs:
  - Buying volume decreases as price rises
  - Selling volume climaxes at the high
  - This imbalance signals lack of acceptance and favors rejection
- [18:00] Defining acceptance above a level: continued willingness to buy at higher prices. Lack of acceptance is one of the strongest signals for anticipating failed breakouts.
- [18:40] Characteristics of a bullish breakout:
  - Rising volume on the breakout candle
  - Increasing positive delta
  - Point of control climbing on successive candles
  - Bid/ask profile shaped like a "P" (buyers increasing with price)
  - Increasing net longs in open interest
- [21:40] Typical range structure in crypto: mid-point retests, swing failure patterns, and liquidity sweeps used to trap breakout traders before reversal.
- [22:20] Identifying a potential swing failure pattern (SFP):
  - Price breaks out on strong volume but reverses
  - Pullback occurs on declining volume (longs taking profits)
  - Buyers step in aggressively at support, confirming demand
  - Net longs then rebuild on the next leg higher
- [25:40] Characteristics of a failed breakout / bearish setup: breakout on low volume, pullback on heavier sell volume, lower highs forming with increasing negative delta and net shorts.
- [26:20] Introduction to volume clusters: darker areas indicate high-volume nodes (likely support/resistance), while lighter areas indicate low-volume zones where price can travel quickly.
- [27:30] Practical example: identifying a low-volume gap above a monthly level, suggesting price could move through it quickly to the next high-volume node where resistance is more likely.
- [29:20] Comparing breakout attempts by volume: a prior high formed on ~1 billion contracts versus a retest on ~350 million signals weak buying interest and reduced probability of continuation.
- [30:40] Trade decision logic:
  - Breakout on high volume ? consider trading with the breakout
  - Breakout on low volume ? consider fading the move
  - Always combine footprint data with broader technical context
- [31:40] Overview of imbalances: calculated diagonally between adjacent price levels (not horizontally). A buying imbalance occurs when aggressive buyers significantly outweigh sellers at one tick versus the adjacent tick Ñ a powerful tool for identifying strong order flow zones.

We encourage traders to study these footprint concepts carefully, combine them with broader technical analysis, and always apply disciplined risk management when putting these ideas into practice.

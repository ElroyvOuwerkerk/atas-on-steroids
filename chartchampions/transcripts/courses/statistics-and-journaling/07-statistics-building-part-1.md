# Statistics Building: Part 1

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This lesson introduces the foundations of statistical thinking for traders, explaining why an edge is defined as a repeatable market pattern with a measurable statistical advantage rather than a streak of wins. We learn how statistics provide clarity, support position sizing decisions, reduce emotional bias, and shift the trader's focus from single trade outcomes to long-term probabilities. The lesson also outlines the difference between static levels and playbooks, and explains the role of backtesting in building a reliable statistical framework.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the statistics building series and its educational purpose.
- [00:32] Why a small number of winning trades does not statistically prove an edge, and how professionals rely on data rather than feelings.
- [01:11] Definition of an edge: a repeatable market pattern with a measurable statistical advantage. A losing trade does not invalidate an edge if the setup has positive expectancy over a large sample size.
- [01:54] Reviewing the win rate vs. risk-to-reward table:
  - A 20% win rate can be profitable with a 5:1 risk-to-reward ratio.
  - The preference for including only setups with a 60Ð65% win rate or higher in a playbook.
  - Reasoning: higher-probability setups allow flexibility in trade management, since even a 1:1 ratio can remain profitable.
- [03:08] Benefits of statistical trading:
  - Clarity on what works and what doesn't.
  - Ability to scale position size with confidence based on measured probabilities.
  - Reduced emotional decision-making.
  - More consistent, process-driven execution.
- [03:49] Mindset shift from asking "Will this trade win?" to "Does this trade fit my system?". Individual trades become less emotional because outcomes are viewed within the broader statistical distribution.
- [04:42] Understanding that winning streaks do not create false confidence, since backtesting has revealed the natural inclusion of losses. Confidence comes from data, not hope.
- [05:13] Difference between a static level and a playbook:
  - Static level: a fixed reference point such as the daily open, previous day high, or previous day low.
  - Playbook: a structured framework built around specific market behaviors and conditions.
  - Static levels define where, playbooks define when, why, and how.
- [06:04] Introduction to backtesting as the process of analyzing historical market data to evaluate how a setup or playbook would have performed. Backtesting builds statistical proof and increases market understanding, even from price action not traded live.
- [06:42] Emphasis that backtesting seeks repeatable probabilities, not certainty.
- [07:16] Practical approach to collecting playbook ideas: maintaining a running list in a notebook or document and systematically backtesting each idea.
- [07:52] Key data points to record when backtesting:
  - Level or playbook being tested.
  - Market context (e.g., uptrend, downtrend, combined).
  - Win or loss outcome.
  - Deviation above and below the level to calculate an average risk-to-reward.
  - Notes for context, chart references, or attached screenshots.
- [08:28] Reminder to remain realistic and objective during backtesting and avoid manipulating results to fit a desired outcome.
- [09:07] Sample size guidance: a minimum of 200 observations before considering a statistic meaningful.
- [09:21] Reasoning for performing backtesting personally instead of outsourcing: the process itself develops market understanding and pattern recognition.
- [09:57] Statistics building also strengthens recognition of reoccurring patterns and behavioral tendencies in the market.
- [10:16] Best practices for building a statistical framework:
  - Take time; do not rush the process.
  - Avoid overcomplication.
  - The most effective statistics are often simple and repeatable.

We are encouraged to apply these foundational concepts carefully, build statistics through disciplined backtesting, and always combine data-driven analysis with sound risk management.

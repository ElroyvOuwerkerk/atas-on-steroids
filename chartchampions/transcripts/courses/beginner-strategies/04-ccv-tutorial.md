# CCV Tutorial

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session explores the 80% probability trade setup, referred to as the CCV (Chart Champions Value) setup, which is based on trading the acceptance of price back into the previous day's value area. We learn how to identify the conditions for the setup, why context and confluence are essential before execution, and how range-bound behavior on Bitcoin offers higher-probability opportunities than chasing breakouts.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Range-bound market principle: Price spends more time ranging than trending across all timeframes from 5-minute to daily. This favors buying support and selling resistance over chasing breakouts.
- [00:51] Defining the 80% setup:
  - Market opens outside the previous day's value area high (VAH) or value area low (VAL).
  - Price becomes accepted back inside via two consecutive 30-minute candle closes within the value area.
  - Volume confirmation is helpful but not strictly required.
- [01:19] Theory behind the setup: A broken VAH or VAL flips into support or resistance, and price tends to traverse back to the opposite side of the prior day's value area.
- [01:54] Importance of context: The setup should never be taken blindly Ñ without confluence and proper market context, the edge is lost.
- [03:03] Example of a setup skipped: Even though the official criteria were met (acceptance below VAH across multiple 30-min closes targeting VAL), the trade was avoided due to broader context.
- [04:34] Alternative trade selection: A long back toward the daily open was preferred over shorting, because the market was more likely to range than break down.
- [05:38] Why the short was avoided Ñ context analysis:
  - Price had just broken out of a 10-day range with healthy volume.
  - V-shaped reversals from fresh breakouts are uncommon on Bitcoin.
  - Markets typically build a new range to accumulate open interest before reversing.
- [06:42] Absorption and CVD divergences: Bullish CVD divergences at lows indicated limit buyers absorbing market sell pressure, signaling larger participants expected higher prices.
- [08:00] Importance of entry timing: Even when directional bias is correct, premature entries often get stopped before the move plays out. Patience develops with experience.
- [09:02] Pre-trade checks:
  - Distance from daily open to prior day's value area.
  - Width of the value area.
  - Whether the prior day was range bound (ideal) or trending strongly (less ideal).
- [10:02] Avoiding the setup after a strong move: When price has just made a large directional break on heavy volume and rising open interest, a same-direction continuation or new range is more likely than a full reversal.
- [10:43] Why ranges form: Bitcoin tends to build ranges to accumulate open interest and trap participants offside before continuation.
- [11:07] Successful trade example:
  - Daily open at 9,358; prior VAH at 9,348; prior VAL near 9,260.
  - Multiple 30-minute closes below VAH confirmed acceptance.
  - Volume supported the move; context was clearly range-bound.
  - A swing failure at prior day's high added further confluence.
- [13:38] Execution details: Short entered slightly below VAH at 9,327 and closed at 9,261, front-running the previous day's VAL by a couple of dollars to ensure fill.
- [14:06] Pre-trade checklist recap:
  - Was the prior day range bound? Ñ increases probability.
  - Was there a major breakout? Ñ decreases probability.
  - Are there signs of absorption against the expected direction?
  - Is there potential for a retest of the daily open before continuation?
- [15:10] Market behavior insight: When price moves back inside the prior day's value area, the market is in a price discovery balance, with buyers and sellers agreeing on fair value until a larger participant forces direction.
- [16:00] Key takeaway: In a range, expect range-bound behavior. Avoid trading breakouts before they confirm Ñ instead, watch for signs that confirm or invalidate continuation.

We are encouraged to study this setup carefully, combine it with context, volume, open interest, and absorption analysis, and apply it only when confluences align Ñ always with disciplined risk management.

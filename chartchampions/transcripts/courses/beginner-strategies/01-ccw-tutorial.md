# CCW Tutorial

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session introduces a mechanical trading strategy built around Fibonacci retracement levels, laddered entries, predetermined stop losses, and multiple take-profit targets. We learn how to structure a trade with four scaled entries at specific retracement levels, how to size positions correctly, and how to manage stop losses as take-profit levels are hit. The strategy aims to reduce emotional decision-making, though it is intended to be combined with additional confluence rather than used in isolation.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to a mechanical trading strategy that predefines both stop losses and take profits, allowing for emotion-free execution. The system can be applied across any timeframe but is best used alongside other confluences rather than in isolation.
- [01:06] Fibonacci setup rules:
  - Draw the Fibonacci retracement from low to high for long setups.
  - Use laddered entries at four "golden" retracement levels.
  - Default allocation is 25% per entry, but weighting can be adjusted based on confluence at specific levels.
- [02:07] Position sizing clarification: If a trade is allocated $4,000 on a $40,000 account, this does not mean entering $4,000 per level. Instead, each ladder entry represents 25% of the total allocation ($1,000 per entry). Position sizes should always be calculated with a proper formula, not arbitrary amounts.
- [03:25] Stop loss placement: Set a few dollars below the swing low used for the Fibonacci anchor. This provides protection against double-bottom swing failure patterns common in altcoins, where lows are often swept by a small margin.
- [03:42] Take profit management:
  - Multiple scaled take profits are placed at successive extension levels.
  - At TP1, moving stop to break-even is optional.
  - At TP2, move stop to break-even or into profit below the last consolidation (if there are two higher lows forming).
- [04:45] Example of a full ladder trade: Price fills all four entries, bounces, and reaches TP1, TP2, and TP3 but not TP4. Context Ñ such as higher-timeframe resistance Ñ is essential when deciding how far to project take profits.
- [05:37] Stop loss width rationale: The invalidation sits slightly below the swing low to accommodate swing failure patterns while remaining tight enough to preserve a reasonable risk-reward.
- [07:05] Why 0.28 is used for TP1: Testing showed that the 0.28 negative Fibonacci level frequently aligns with the 0.618 retracement from the subsequent high-to-low leg, making it a historically strong reaction zone.
- [08:19] Partial fill scenario: If only Entry 1 is filled and TP1 is reached, we must decide: This management decision is personal and depends on trade context.
  - Cancel the lower unfilled orders and move stop to entry, or
  - Keep lower orders active while anticipating further upside.
- [10:36] Short setup example: On a high-to-low Fibonacci, two of four short entries filled. The recommended approach is to wait until TP2 before cancelling remaining unfilled orders and moving the stop into profit.
- [12:00] Identifying weakness to exit early: If price fills an entry, bounces, but fails to reach TP1 and begins forming bearish structure (e.g., head and shoulders, lower highs), closing early for a small loss can be preferable to holding to full invalidation.
- [14:05] Limitations of a purely mechanical stop: Arbitrary stop placement below the low can produce wide invalidations (e.g., 6%). Incorporating structural reference points such as a point of control can reduce stop width significantly (e.g., to 3.5%), improving risk-reward.
- [16:17] Multi-timeframe application: Demonstration on a 1-minute chart shows the same system applied to a short setup Ñ three entries filled followed by three take profits reached, confirming the setup's flexibility across timeframes.

We should review these concepts carefully, test the system in a controlled environment, and always combine it with proper position sizing and risk management before applying it to live markets.

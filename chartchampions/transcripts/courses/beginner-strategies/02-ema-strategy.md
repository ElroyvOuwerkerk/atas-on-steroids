# EMA Strategy

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session presents an exponential moving average (EMA) based trading strategy designed for the 4-hour and higher timeframes, with a preference for the daily chart. We review how to combine the 26, 55, 100, and 200 EMAs with Fibonacci retracements to identify trend direction, confluence zones for dip buying, and signals for closing positions or reversing bias.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the EMA 2.0 strategy, best applied on the 4-hour or higher timeframes, with the daily chart being preferred for cleaner signals.
- [00:19] Core indicators required:
  - 26 EMA (orange)
  - 55 EMA (yellow)
  - 100 EMA (white/blue)
  - 200 EMA (green)
- [01:25] Why EMA compression is not tradable Ñ signals become valid only when EMAs fan out and create clear separation.
- [01:41] The buy signal: when the 55 EMA crosses below the 26 EMA with clear spacing, indicating a confirmed uptrend.
- [02:19] Defining a daily uptrend through structure Ñ successive higher highs and higher lows Ñ and the principle of buying the dips within this structure.
- [02:49] Aggressive dip-buying approach: scaling into positions at the 55, 100, and 200 EMAs, using the 200 EMA area as guidance for stop placement.
- [03:18] Importance of confluence: aligning EMA touches with horizontal support/resistance levels to improve entry confidence.
- [03:47] Adding Fibonacci retracements from swing low to swing high; the 0.382Ð0.618 zone is the typical retracement area in healthy uptrends.
- [05:05] Example walkthrough of dip-buying as price retraces to the 55 and 100 EMAs with Fibonacci confluence at the 0.5Ð0.618 levels.
- [06:11] Scenario where the 0.618 is lost but the 200 EMA holds Ñ treated as an aggressive dip-buying opportunity with EMA confluence.
- [08:00] Resistance-turned-support flips confirmed at the 0.5 Fibonacci level, reinforcing dip-buy entries during the trend continuation.
- [08:14] SR flip at the 0.618 golden pocket as a high-probability continuation entry in a daily uptrend.
- [09:30] Recognizing when fewer dip opportunities appear near the top Ñ price fails to retrace to key EMAs before pushing higher.
- [10:04] Exit signal: when the 55 EMA crosses back above the 26 EMA, close long positions and prepare to reverse bias.
- [10:30] Caution on over-complicating a simple strategy Ñ additional tools should enhance, not replace, the core system.
- [11:04] Using RSI bearish divergence (higher highs in price, lower highs in RSI) as an early warning sign rather than a direct sell signal.
- [12:05] Note that bearish divergences can persist in strong uptrends; treat them as warnings, especially near major psychological levels.
- [13:08] Transition to shorting: open short positions on the bearish 55/26 EMA cross, applying the same framework in reverse.
- [13:37] Short-side management: use Fibonacci retracements from swing high to swing low, targeting the 0.382Ð0.618 zone (especially the golden pocket) for short entries.
- [15:00] Risk acknowledgment: strict EMA-based reversal signals can produce losing trades (e.g., fake-out crosses), reinforcing the need for additional confluence like the golden pocket.
- [15:21] Re-entering shorts on subsequent bearish crosses, with price repeatedly rejecting from the golden pocket on lower timeframes.

We encourage reviewing these EMA and Fibonacci confluence concepts carefully, backtesting them across multiple assets and timeframes, and applying strict risk management before incorporating them into any live trading plan.

# Profile Trading: Part 1

## Portal Summary & Highlights

In this comprehensive session, we dive deep into the world of profile trading, exploring both volume profiles and market profiles (TPO). We cover the historical origins of profile trading dating back to the 1860s Chicago trading pits, explain the critical differences between volume and time-based analysis, and demonstrate practical implementation using real S&P 500 E-mini futures examples. Key concepts like balance vs. imbalance, value areas, and point of control are thoroughly explained with actionable trading insights.

### Timestamps

- [00:00] Introduction to Personal Profile Strategy - Overview of the trading strategy used daily across multiple assets - Session will cover: utility, implementation, and tips & tricks
- [01:01] History & Utility of Profile Trading - Profile trading originated in the late 1800s on trading floors (trading pits) - Original traders at Chicago Mercantile Exchange and NYSE used floor-based methods - Market Profile (TPO) = Time, Price, Opportunity - how floor traders tracked trades
- [02:04] Volume Profile vs. Market Profile Origins - Digital trading enabled mining of transactional data ? Volume Profile was born - Important insight: Trading volume without time provides an incomplete picture - Market profile more effective on traditional markets due to New York session volume surge
- [03:09] Origins of Futures Trading (1860s) - Chicago trading groups created methods to combat fluctuating prices - Examples: Drought ? corn price increases; Too many cows ? price decreases - Futures contracts = agreements between buyers and sellers on future prices
- [04:17] Three Critical Areas of Interest - Value Area High (VAH) - Value Area Low (VAL) - Point of Control (POC) - POC = highest accumulated amount of completed executed contracts - VAH/VAL = 70% of executed orders in relation to POC
- [05:47] Volume Profile vs. Market Profile Differences - Volume Profile POC: Highest accumulated volume at a price level - Market Profile POC: Highest accumulated time spent at a price level - Both use 70% value areas but can show very different levels
- [06:31] Implementation Concepts Overview - Balance vs. imbalance - Building value vs. searching for untested value - Identifying breaks of balance vs. remaining in balance - Understanding why transactions happen is crucial
- [07:00] Market Depth, Time & Sales, Order Book Explained - Market Depth (DOM): Shows only limit orders (no market orders) - Market orders fill limit orders - Time & Sales (Tape): Executed market orders that filled limit orders - Explains slippage and fill mechanics with practical examples
- [09:17] Liquidity & Institutional Role - Key insight: Market movement requires liquidity - Institutions provide liquidity - they move markets because they need to - Trading is a "game of absolutes" - if you win, someone loses - True "order book" refers to institutional liquidity
- [10:35] Order Flow Explained - Order flow ­ market depth ­ time & sales - Order flow = analysis of completed executed price action - Helps determine who controls the market in that move - Nearly live but technically a lagging indicator (completed, not pending)
- [12:27] Volume vs. Time Profiles - Critical Distinction - Volume profiles are NOT the same as market profiles - Volume profile = based on volume - Market profile = based on time (TPO charts)
- [13:17] Live Chart Analysis: S&P 500 E-mini Futures - Daily session separation with fixed volume profiles - Identifying cluster nodes (completed executed orders) - Single prints explained (not FVG, not imbalance - covered in TPO lessons)
- [15:04] Pre-Market Analysis & Previous Day Relevance - Before New York opens: previous day's volume profile determines direction - After-hours volume is minimal compared to NY session - NY volume quadruples ? current day profile becomes more relevant
- [16:31] Break of Balance Analysis - Live Example - New York open creates major moves - Breaking through previous day's VAH - Failed imbalance = no new buyers above = rotate back into balance - Support found at current day's VAL
- [18:05] Initial Balance Concept - Initial Balance (IB) = first hour of NY market open - Only track NY initial balance (not London or Asia) due to volume differences - Critical for determining day type
- [18:26] Detailed Price Action: Futures Open to NY - Futures open pump ? fails to break balance (lack of volume) - Retrace to node support (cluster formation) - Previous day's POC acts as resistance until NY volume arrives
- [19:27] The Rule: To Break Balance, You Need Imbalance - Initial imbalance that fails to continue = rotate back into balance - Balance is key - the market seeks equilibrium
- [20:14] Value Area Shifts & Rotation Mechanics - Value areas shift continuously as data accumulates - Failed downside imbalance ? rotate back to VAH - Failed upside imbalance ? cycle back into rotation
- [21:47] Untested Value Concept - After-hours moves don't "test" levels - only NY truly tests - Previous day's VAL = untested until NY opens - London/Asia touches don't count as true tests - "True imbalance comes with true volume"
- [23:11] Profile Shapes & Day Types - Symmetrical/D-shaped profile = POC at 50% of range - Indicates likely inside day (ping-pong action) - Gaining acceptance back into value area = rotation continues
- [24:03] Multi-Day Analysis Examples - London fails to break balance ? NY breaks through - Unt

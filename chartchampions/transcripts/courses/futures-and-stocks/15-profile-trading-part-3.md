# Profile Trading: Part 3

## Portal Summary & Highlights

In this comprehensive third installment of the Profile Strategy series, we dive deep into the practical application of combining volume and time analysis for more informed trading decisions. We explore the critical concepts of single prints, tails, and day types (elongated, symmetrical, and squat profiles), demonstrating how these elements work together to identify market control and potential trade opportunities. This session provides the culminating framework for understanding how price advertises opportunities, time regulates them, and volume measures their success or failure.

- [07:09] Identifying Profile Shapes - Symmetrical D-shape: POC at approximately 50% of value area - Capital P: POC near value area high with stretched lower tail - Lowercase b: Value area low just under POC - Critical distinction: Don't confuse slightly uneven profiles with truly elongated ones
- [08:14] Squat Profiles Explained - Indicate a counter-trend rally is ending - Characterized by tight TPO range and "shelf-like" value area boundaries - Key insight: Squat profiles almost always follow massive trend days - they signal potential direction change

- [18:47] Single Prints as Efficient Moves - Important reframe: Single prints are NOT inefficiencies to be filled - They represent forceful, directional moves showing opportunity - Can be used for back-testing demand/supply days later
- [21:40] Combining Volume Profile with Market Profile - Volume clusters should form and stair-step during one-time framing - Pullbacks holding on clusters confirm uptrend strength - Dual confirmation: Time shows single prints, volume shows conviction
- [23:04] Symmetrical Day Analysis with Volume - After-hours distribution followed by New York breakout pattern - Lost strong volume node = opportunity for short on lower high - Key: Always correlate both volume and time for complete picture
- [26:06] When to Use Split vs. Non-Split Sessions - Elongated/trending: Use split sessions to see one-time framing - Symmetrical/squat: Keep sessions together to see the shape - Practical application: Switch views based on developing price action
- [26:33] Reading Multi-Day Profiles - Elongated profile followed by squat/symmetrical suggests potential reversal - Poor highs/lows: Double TPO indicates insufficient pressure to break - Markets search for liquidity when poor highs/lows form
- [29:07] Trading Poor Highs and Lows - Poor high + bearish New York session = target single prints below - Goal is to remain in balance - this is where most money is made - Strategy: Trade value area high to low in balanced conditions
- [30:24] Established vs. Developing Profiles - Established: Completed daily profiles for analysis - Developing: Ongoing intraday profiles for real-time decisions - Pre-market distribution + cash session shape determines next targets
- [31:17] Tips and Tricks for Implementation - Analyze live price action combining both volume and time - Distribution breaks create single prints on TPO market profile - Use volume to determine sustainability and conviction of moves

# Compounding

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This video dives deep into three advanced trade management strategies: compounding trades, averaging down, and laddering into and out of positions. These techniques go beyond the basic buy/stop-loss/target approach, helping traders maximize profits on winning positions, recover from losing setups, and achieve better average entry prices. Real-world examples on Litecoin, Bitcoin, and EOS demonstrate how to apply these strategies effectively while managing the increased risk that comes with added complexity.

### Detailed Timestamps

- [00:00] Introduction to the three advanced strategies: compounding, averaging down, and laddering. Disclaimer: these are advanced techniques that add complexity and risk—not recommended for beginners without practice.
- [01:42] Reminder that more risk equals more potential profit. Emphasis on practicing these techniques before risking real capital.
- [02:11] What is compounding a trade? Using REN as an example—standard traders enter with one entry, stop-loss, and target. Compounding involves managing the trade by adding additional positions when secondary setups appear during the trade's progression.
- [03:00] Key rule for compounding: only add to a position if the secondary setup would qualify as a standalone trade on its own merits (e.g., SR flips, support/resistance breaks).
- [04:30] How to manage stop-losses when compounding:
  - Move the original stop-loss up to the new compounded entry's stop level
  - This guarantees profits on the original position while maximizing gains
  - Risk: if stopped out, the added position becomes a loser
- [06:30] Averaging down explained: Using REN as an example of buying an order block, getting stopped through entry, and adding to the losing position at a stronger support level (like the 0.382 Fib).
- [07:30] Critical warning: Averaging down is highly advanced. Beginners should simply take the stop-loss instead. Only average down when there's a strong secondary support level that justifies it.
- [09:30] Strategy after averaging down: Exit at break-even on the bounce, review what went wrong with the original setup, then re-enter fresh if conditions still look strong.
- [11:48] Strict rules for averaging down:
  - Maximum of 1-2 averages, then enforce a stop-loss
  - Never keep adding repeatedly hoping for a bounce
  - Consider sizing the averaged-down buy larger (e.g., 2x) than the original to require a smaller bounce for break-even
- [13:35] Laddering into a trade: Recognition that timing exact bottoms or tops is nearly impossible, so spreading entries across a range provides a better average price.
- [14:23] Practical laddering example with Fibonacci levels: placing buys at 0.5, 0.618, 0.65, and 0.786 retracement levels for altcoin wave 2 retracements. Position sizing can be weighted to favor higher or lower levels depending on conviction.
- [16:40] Litecoin case study: Detailed walkthrough of a real laddered entry with four levels: swing failure, 0.5 Fib, 0.618 Fib, and below 0.65. Lower orders never filled because the trade went bullish.
- [18:50] When to cancel lower ladder orders: After a clear SR flip with confirmed market structure change (higher highs and higher lows). At that point, market buy the remaining position size.
- [20:20] Important insight: Not filling all ladder orders is actually good—it means your bullish bias was correct and price didn't need to retrace deeper.
- [22:00] Compounding walkthrough on Litecoin:
  - Entry 1: Initial laddered buy
  - Entry 2: Ascending triangle breakout & retest (SR flip)
  - Entry 3: Higher-low SR flip on lower timeframe
  - Entry 4: Bull flag continuation pattern
  - All stop-losses progressively moved up to lock in profits
- [25:00] Bitcoin order block laddering example: Three buys placed at 0.1, 0.75, and 0.5 of the order block. The 0.5 level held the heaviest position because it represents critical support—anything beyond is an order block red flag.
- [27:30] Quick recap of all three strategies:
  - Compounding – Add to winners only when new valid setups present themselves
  - Averaging down – Backup plan when a secondary strong support appears in a losing trade; aim for break-even exit
  - Laddering – Spread entries to get better average pricing while accounting for timing uncertainty
- [28:30] How to ladder OUT of a trade: Take profits in stages at significant levels (hourly, then daily). Look for opportunities to re-enter via compounding after partial profit-taking.
- [30:00] Final lesson on what does NOT qualify as a compounding opportunity: if the required stop-loss placement gives a poor risk-to-reward ratio (e.g., 1:1), it's not a valid setup—even if you're already in the trade.
- [31:20] Closing advice: Re-watch this video multiple times until these concepts are fully internalized before attempting them with real capital.

Take Action: Start by paper-trading these techniques to build confidence before risking capital. Master one strategy at a time—begin with laddering for better entries, then progress to compounding winners, and only attempt averaging down once you've developed disciplined trade management. With practice, these advanced methods can significantly enhance your profitability while teaching you to truly let your winners run.

# SFP Liquidity

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This lesson breaks down the Swing Failure Pattern (SFP) and its relationship with market liquidity, explaining how large traders use predictable stop-loss zones to fill their positions. Viewers will learn the mechanics behind why SFPs occur, how to identify and trade them step-by-step, and gain practical tips for maximizing win rates while managing risk effectively.

### Detailed Timestamps

- [00:00] Introduction Ñ Overview of the lesson covering swing failure patterns and liquidity, including utility, implementation, and tips & tricks.
- [00:17] Why SFPs Exist - The Liquidity Problem - Large traders (e.g., $15M positions) face challenges entering thin volume markets - Limit orders may never fill before price moves away - Market orders with size move price against the trader, causing poor entries
- [01:01] Where Big Traders Find Liquidity - Stop losses cluster predictably below significant lows (uptrends) or above significant highs (downtrends) - Most retail traders place stops as market orders at technical levels - These predictable zones become liquidity pools for larger participants
- [01:58] Defining the Swing Failure Pattern - An SFP breaches a key high/low, triggers stops, grabs liquidity, and closes back on the other side - Uptrend SFP: Price dips below key low, closes back above - Downtrend SFP: Price pokes above key high, closes back below - Also known as "stop runs" or "liquidity grabs"
- [02:26] Step 1 - Identify the Key Level - Look for clearly visible swing highs/lows - Consolidation areas are particularly powerful reference points - The more obvious the level, the more stops are sitting beyond it
- [02:51] Step 2 - Wait for the Breach and Reclaim - A break alone is NOT an SFP - Price must close back on the correct side - Patience for the candle close is essential to avoid false signals
- [03:06] Step 3 - Entry and Stop Placement - Enter on the reclaim candle close - Place stop just beyond the wick of the SFP candle - Keep stops tight and clean
- [03:24] Take Profit Strategy - Take partial profits early since you can't predict reversal vs. retracement - First targets: local support/resistance, session VWAP, or value area of the range - Let runners go if structure shifts in your favor
- [03:54] Time Frame Considerations - SFPs work on every time frame - Higher time frames = bigger expected rotations - Daily SFPs carry far more weight than 5-minute SFPs
- [04:22] The Power of Consolidation Zones - Range boundaries after consolidation become strong reference points - A sweep of the boundary followed by a reclaim is one of the cleanest SFP setups
- [04:40] Tips & Tricks - Critical Rules 1. Not every breach is an SFP Ñ must close on the other side 2. Obvious levels are best Ñ more visibility means more stops and stronger reactions 3. Volume matters Ñ high volume at the breach strengthens the signal; be cautious of thin volume
- [05:26] Advanced Confirmation & Risk Management 4. Order flow confirmation significantly increases win rate Ñ look for trapped traders 5. Take profit early Ñ protect capital, you can always re-enter 6. Align with bigger players Ñ understand they're using these levels to enter positions and trade with them, not against them

Start applying these concepts by scanning your charts for clean swing highs/lows near consolidation zones, and practice patience by waiting for the reclaim before entering. Mastering SFPs can transform how you read market movements and align you with smart money flows!

# Trade Entry Strategy

## Portal Summary & Highlights

We also have cheat sheets available for this topic which can be downloaded as a high-res PDF via our vault section here : https://portal.chartchampions.com/vault - download is titled ‘Trading Setup’

In this beginner's trading video, we explore the different types of trade entries, their implementation, and tips for effective use. The video covers limit orders, market orders, and alerts, discussing their advantages, disadvantages, and when to use each type. It also provides a live demonstration of placing orders in the order book and shares valuable insights on fees and slippage.

### Timestamps:

- [00:00] Introduction and legal trade disclaimer
- [00:20] Overview of the three types of orders in trading:
  - Limit orders: Preset orders on a chart
  - Market orders: Executed in real-time at the current price
  - Alerts: Used to indicate an area to place an order and enter a trade
- [00:58] Implementation of limit orders:
  - Placed at strong levels of confluence after technical analysis
  - Useful for swing traders who cannot be at their computer for entry
- [01:30] Market orders and their primary use case:
  - Used after checking the reaction from a level
  - Combined with alerts placed at areas of interest on the chart
  - Entering with a market order if the reaction is good; remaining patient if not
- [02:56] Advantages of using alerts with market orders:
  - Avoids instantly losing trades due to lack of good reaction at a level
  - Scalp traders primarily use market orders for quick moves
- [03:46] Comparing limit and market orders:
  - Limit orders: Cheaper and guarantee entry price but do not guarantee order fulfillment
  - Market orders: More expensive and can have slippage but guarantee order fulfillment
- [04:52] Strategy for large orders: Using a mixture of limit and market orders
- [05:16] Live demonstration of placing limit and market orders in the order book
- [06:57] Setting alerts for price levels
- [07:13] Tips and tricks:
  - Market orders are more expensive (0.1% fee) but guarantee entry
  - Slippage is rare on market orders, and large slippage is uncommon
  - Waiting for limit orders can lead to missing trades entirely
  - Fees should be considered a cost of doing business and not a deterrent to trading

By understanding the different order types, their strengths, and their weaknesses, traders can make informed decisions on when to use each one. Implementing a combination of alerts, market orders, and limit orders can help optimize trade entries while minimizing the risk of missed opportunities. Remember, fees are a part of trading, and focusing too much on saving on fees can lead to missed trades and potential profits.

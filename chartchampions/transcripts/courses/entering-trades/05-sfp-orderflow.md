# SFP Orderflow

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This lesson dives into combining Swing Failure Patterns (SFPs) with order flow analysis to gain extra confirmation and confidence in trade setups. While SFPs work effectively on their own as a high win-rate setup, understanding order flow allows traders to identify trapped participants, gauge demand or supply response, and validate reversals through market structure shifts.

### Timestamps

- [00:00] Introduction to the lesson on Swing Failure Patterns and order flow, including a legal trading disclaimer and overview of utility, implementation, and tips.
- [00:17] Recap of the previous lesson on SFPs from a price action perspective. Emphasis that order flow is not required to trade SFPs - they remain valid setups on any timeframe (30-min, 5-min, etc.) with high win rates on their own.
- [00:58] How order flow adds value: it doesn't change the setup but provides additional insight and confirmation, helping gauge the strength of likely reactions and revealing trapped participants.
- [01:23] Step 1 - Look for Trap Traders on the footprint chart:
  - High activity at the lows (volume picking up in the liquidity zone)
  - Longs getting stopped out as price sweeps the level
  - New shorts opening, shown as negative delta at the candle low with rising open interest
- [02:10] The perfect entry trigger on the footprint chart: increase in open interest, correct delta confirming the trap, and candle closing back on the other side of the swept level. For SFPs at highs, flip the logic - look for trapped longs and positive delta.
- [02:46] Step 2 - Look for Demand (or Supply) to Show Up:
  - Buying imbalances on the footprint chart where aggressive buying overpowers selling
  - Increase in open interest combined with positive delta, indicating new longs opening
  - For SFPs at highs: selling imbalances and new shorts opening
- [03:25] Step 3 - Change in Market Structure: After the SFP, price should begin making higher lows and higher highs on the lower timeframe, confirming a bullish shift. Structure flip allows holding positions with more confidence or compounding.
- [04:03] Summary of the framework: Steps 1 and 2 = order flow confirmation, Step 3 = price action confirmation. Together they provide the full picture.
- [04:10] Tips and Tricks begin with practical rules for application.
- [04:14] Tip 1: Trap traders are the foundation - always ask: "Can I see trapped participants at the key level?" If yes, the SFP carries extra weight.
- [04:32] Tip 2: Demand or supply must respond - traps alone aren't enough; look for the opposing side stepping in with imbalances and new positions.
- [04:45] Tip 3: Market structure confirms the move - don't assume a full reversal from the start. Take profit one early, then adjust based on structure shifts.
- [04:57] Tip 4: Order flow is confluence, not a requirement. If an SFP appears, take the trade. Order flow is a bonus layer of confluence, not a gatekeeper.
- [05:22] Tip 5: Skill develops with screen time. Start with visual SFPs first, build confidence, then layer in order flow gradually. Don't pressure yourself to master every reading from day one.
- [05:40] Conclusion: Combine the visual method (Part 1) with order flow confirmation (Part 2) based on personal style and experience - both approaches work effectively.

Start by mastering visual SFPs and gradually integrate order flow analysis as your screen time grows. Apply these three steps - trap traders, demand/supply response, and market structure shift - to elevate your SFP trading and build genuine confidence in your setups!

# Wedge Patterns

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session reviews a losing XRP trade and the lessons derived from it, then provides a detailed educational breakdown of rising and falling wedge patterns, including volume confirmation, entry methods, and target placement using Fibonacci retracements. The session also introduces a structured trading journal and position-sizing tool, and closes with a review of successful Bitcoin and Litecoin trades based on order blocks and support/resistance flips.

### Highlights

- [06:00] XRP trade review and lessons on aggressive entries:
  - Original trade was overly aggressive, laddering longs from the 0.236 Fibonacci level rather than waiting for a reclaim of the 0.382.
  - Conservative setup would have been waiting for a break and retest of the 0.382 resistance before entering long.
  - Stop-loss placement was too tight and got wicked out, highlighting the importance of placing stops at structurally meaningful levels.
  - Key takeaway: mental state strongly influences trade quality; avoid trading when unfocused.
- [13:30] Retrade of XRP using the original plan: waiting for the 0.382 reclaim and retest resulted in a successful 7% gain, recovering the earlier loss. Demonstrates the value of patience and sticking to the original setup.
- [16:30] Rising wedge pattern Ñ core concepts:
  - Price converges upward with at least five touches (typically 3 on one trend line, 2 on the other).
  - Impulse moves become progressively smaller as price nears the apex.
  - Volume must decline as price rises Ñ without this, the short setup is weaker.
  - Bearish divergence on RSI strengthens the setup.
- [19:20] Three ways to trade a rising wedge:
  - Aggressive: short the third touch of the upper trend line with stop above the trend line.
  - Standard: market short on the break of the lower support trend line.
  - Conservative: wait for a breakdown and retest before shorting (not always available).
- [24:30] Target placement for wedges: apply a Fibonacci retracement from the start of the wedge to its peak Ñ Target 1: 0.382, Target 2: 0.618 (golden pocket), Target 3: origin of the wedge.
- [26:00] Elliott Wave perspective Ñ Ending Diagonal: rising wedges often appear as 1-2-3-4-5 ending diagonals where wave 3 is shorter than wave 1, and wave 5 is shorter than wave 3. A stop can be placed above a 1:1 projection of wave 3 to invalidate the count.
- [28:00] Falling wedge pattern:
  - Inverse of rising wedge Ñ bearish volume should decline as price makes lower lows.
  - Entries: aggressive at third touch of lower trend line, or on confirmed breakout above resistance trend line.
  - Bullish divergence on RSI adds confluence.
- [33:30] Example of a falling wedge trade on Bitcoin: break of upper trend line with aggressive entry provided a ~26:1 risk-to-reward opportunity when stops were placed below the low that led to the break.
- [37:00] Consolidation quality matters: downward-sloping bull flags after a wedge target hit indicate healthy continuation; horizontal or bearish consolidation often precedes larger reversals.
- [40:30] Volume filter rule: if volume is increasing on a rising wedge, do not short; if volume is increasing on a falling wedge, do not long Ñ volume must contract into the apex.
- [42:30] How to draw wedge trend lines: visually identify converging price action, connect at least two highs and two lows, and allow for minor wicks through the line. Combine with horizontal levels and Fibonacci confluence for stronger setups.
- [47:00] Trading journal introduction:
  - Logging every trade enforces accountability and allows review of mistakes and winning behaviors.
  - Separate sheets for USD-denominated and BTC-denominated pairs to avoid calculation errors.
  - Record entry, stop-loss, target, position size, timeframe, trade screenshot, exit, and notes on psychology.
- [48:30] Position sizing methodology: account size and stop-loss distance automatically determine position size while keeping risk at a fixed percentage (e.g., 2%). Tighter stops allow larger positions at the same risk.
- [55:30] Daily order block trade on Bitcoin:
  - Order block = the last red candle before a strong bullish breakout.
  - Divide the candle into quarters (0%, 25%, 50%, 75%, 100%).
  - Heavy buys placed at the 0.5 of the order block provided a high-probability entry.
- [57:30] Litecoin trade Ñ weekly S/R flip: patient entry after the weekly resistance broke and retested as support, combined with an ascending-triangle structure, yielded a strong day-trade return targeting the monthly level.
- [1:04:00] Trade confirmation principle: avoid blindly placing bids at key levels Ñ wait for the touch, observe consolidation, and enter on confirmation of breakdown or breakout.
- [1:06:30] Timeframe reliability of wedges: higher timeframes (daily, weekly) carry significantly higher probability than lower timeframes (5-min). Combine wedge analysis with Fibonacci and horizontal confluence for best results.

We encourage traders to carefully study the wedge criteria Ñ volume contraction, structural touches, and target placement Ñ and to adopt consistent trade journaling with proper position sizing and risk management when applying these concepts.

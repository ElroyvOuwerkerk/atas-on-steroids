# Triangle Patterns

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session reviews a completed ENG/BTC chart-of-the-week setup, introduces a four-point trade entry checklist, and provides a detailed breakdown of how to trade ascending, descending, and symmetrical triangles using volume, Fibonacci levels, and measured moves. The session closes with a short-term Bitcoin technical analysis focused on key Fibonacci and horizontal support/resistance levels.

### Highlights

- [06:30] ENG/BTC Chart of the Week review: Entry placed below the wick at horizontal support, combined with the 0.5 Fibonacci retracement and the 55/200 EMA about to cross bullish Ñ illustrating a confluence-based long setup.
- [09:30] Volume as a key confirmation tool: A rally on decreasing volume is a bearish signal, while a breakout or support bounce on higher-than-average volume (above the 20MA volume) confirms strength.
- [11:00] Liquidity and stop placement: Placing buy orders below wicks captures liquidity from traders whose stop losses are clustered there. Stop losses should be set at the point where the trade idea is invalidated, not at an arbitrary level.
- [12:30] Swing failure pattern (SFP): A wick that breaks support and closes back above it, especially combined with Fib and EMA confluence, is a strong bullish reversal signal.
- [13:00] Support/Resistance flips: Broken support becomes resistance; broken resistance becomes support Ñ a core principle for identifying take-profit zones.
- [18:00] Exiting at weekly resistance: After hitting a major resistance, measuring the Fib from the recent high to low and exiting on the 0.618 retracement of the pullback bounce is a disciplined profit-taking technique.
- [22:00] Range trading approach: Trading between daily support and weekly resistance using bounces, and preparing laddered buy zones between the 0.618 and 0.786 if support fails.
- [27:00] Trade Entry Checklist (four required criteria):
  - 1. Entry Ñ placed where probabilities favor the trade.
  - 2. Stop loss Ñ set at the invalidation point of the setup.
  - 3. Take profit Ñ located at a credible resistance; multiple targets allowed.
  - 4. Risk-to-reward Ñ minimum 2:1 for experienced traders, preferably 3:1 or higher for newer traders.
- [35:00] Win rate vs. risk-reward: Higher risk-reward ratios allow a trader to be profitable even with a lower win rate, highlighting the importance of consistency over single trades.
- [38:00] Triangle patterns overview: Introduction to ascending, descending, and symmetrical triangles as continuation patterns; the direction of entry into the triangle often determines the breakout direction.
- [40:00] White space rule: A valid triangle must have multiple clean touches on both sides with minimal white space; excessive white space invalidates the pattern.
- [41:30] Volume behavior inside triangles: Volume should decrease as price moves toward the apex, becoming very low near the breakout, then expand sharply on the breakout for confirmation.
- [44:00] Measured move target technique: Measure the price range from the triangle's base to its peak, then project that distance from the breakout point to establish a target.
- [46:00] Four types of triangle traders:
  - Aggressive: buys at the bottom of the triangle for maximum reward.
  - Market breakout: enters immediately on the break with volume.
  - Retest trader: waits for the breakout to retest the trendline.
  - Conservative: waits for retest and a higher high confirmation before entering.
- [49:00] Flip-based re-entry: After a breakout, a resistance that flips to support provides another high-probability long entry with a tight stop below the flip zone.
- [53:00] Descending triangle example: Using Fibonacci retracements from each lower high to the base, shorts can be taken repeatedly at the 0.618 level of each internal wave.
- [57:00] Symmetrical triangles: True continuation patterns Ñ direction of entry generally dictates direction of exit. Confirmation requires a volume expansion on the breakout; a weak breakout often signals a false move.
- [1:03:00] Bitcoin technical outlook: Key short-term support at the 0.382 Fib; holding this level maintains bullish structure. Next resistance zones identified at the 0.618 Fib (~8,400) and monthly resistance near 9,250.
- [1:08:00] Trade with the chart, not opinions: When a prior bias (e.g., a bearish target) is invalidated by price action breaking resistance, the opinion must be abandoned to follow the chart's probabilities.
- [1:12:00] Consolidation bias: Consolidation under resistance often resolves bullishly; consolidation above support that repeatedly fails to bounce strongly, especially with bearish divergences, often resolves bearishly.

We encourage all viewers to review these concepts carefully, practice applying the trade entry checklist to every setup, and prioritize disciplined risk management when working with triangle patterns and support/resistance frameworks.

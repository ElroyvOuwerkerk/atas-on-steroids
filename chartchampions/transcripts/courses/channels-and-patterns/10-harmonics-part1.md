# Harmonics Part 1

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session provides a structured walkthrough of harmonic patterns and how Fibonacci tools can be applied to identify, validate, and trade them. We cover the importance of wave symmetry, the need for confluence beyond Fibonacci alone, and the specific Fibonacci retracement and expansion levels used to define the AB=CD, Bat, Gartley, Butterfly, and Crab patterns, along with entry, take-profit, and volume considerations.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to harmonic patterns:
  - Patterns rely on Fibonacci retracement and expansion but also require a correct visual structure.
  - Symmetry between waves is essential Ñ disproportionate time or movement between legs invalidates the pattern.
  - Time symmetry connects directly to Fibonacci time analysis.
- [01:16] Confluence rule:
  - Never trade a harmonic pattern based on Fibonacci alone.
  - Seek at least one additional confluence factor at the trade level to improve probabilities.
  - Single-tool entries (e.g., trading a 0.618 alone) are considered low probability.
- [02:18] Trading the C wave vs. waiting for D:
  - Officially, harmonic patterns are entered at point D once the pattern completes.
  - With strong confluence, earlier participation in the C wave is possible, treated as anticipating pattern completion.
  - Harmonic patterns are best traded on higher time frames Ñ minimum 1H, preferably 4H+.
- [03:10] AB=CD pattern:
  - From impulse A to B, apply Fibonacci retracement to find C between the 0.618 and 0.786.
  - If C reaches 0.618 ? target a 1.618 expansion for D.
  - If C reaches 0.786 ? target a 1.272 expansion for D.
  - Symmetry in time between AB and CD legs is required.
  - Use market structure (e.g., old support flipping to resistance) for additional confirmation.
- [05:45] Bat pattern:
  - Impulse from X to A on a higher timeframe.
  - B must retrace to between 0.382 and 0.5 of XA (perfect Bat = exact 0.5).
  - C retraces between 0.382 and 0.886 of AB, ideally 0.5 or 0.618.
  - Point D = 0.886 retracement of XA, with confluence from 1.618Ð2.618 expansion of BC.
  - Take Profit 1 = 0.618 of CD; Take Profit 2 = high of A.
- [09:39] Gartley pattern:
  - B retraces to 0.618 of XA (preferred).
  - C between 0.382 and 0.886 of AB, ideally 0.618.
  - D = 0.786 retracement of XA, with confluence from 1.272Ð1.618 expansion of BC.
  - Take Profit 1 = 0.618 of CD; alternative target = 1:1 extension of XA from D, with similar time symmetry between legs.
- [12:43] Butterfly pattern:
  - B must retrace to the 0.786 of XA (deeper retrace than Bat/Gartley).
  - C retraces 0.382Ð0.886 of AB, often 0.618.
  - D = 1.272Ð1.618 extension of XA, with confluence from 1.618Ð2.618 expansion of BC.
  - Volume behavior to confirm bullish butterfly:
    - XA: price up on decreasing volume.
    - AB: price down on increasing bear volume.
    - BC: price up on low volume (smart money taking profit).
    - CD: bear volume should decrease as price approaches D Ñ increasing bear volume is a red flag against the long.
  - Take Profit 1 = point B; Take Profit 2 = 0.618 retracement of AD.
- [17:05] Crab pattern:
  - B retraces between 0.382 and 0.618 of XA, ideally 0.618.
  - C between 0.382 and 0.886 of AB, ideally 0.5 or 0.618.
  - D = 2.24Ð3.618 expansion of BC, with confluence from a 1.618 expansion of XA or a 1.618 expansion of AB.
  - Take Profit 1 = 0.618 of CD; Take Profit 2 = high of A or trail stops.
- [19:01] General trade management principles:
  - Use market structure as a primary tool alongside Fibonacci.
  - Place stops just beyond pattern invalidation points.
  - Seek non-Fibonacci confluences (e.g., moving averages, structure) to strengthen setups.

We encourage reviewing each harmonic pattern carefully, practicing identification on higher timeframes, and applying strict confluence and risk management before considering any live application.

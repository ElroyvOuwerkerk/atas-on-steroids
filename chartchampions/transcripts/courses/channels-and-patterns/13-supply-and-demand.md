# Supply and Demand

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This lesson explores the concept of supply and demand in trading, explaining how these fundamental market forces drive price movements across all financial assets. We learn how to identify supply and demand zones, recognize valid retests, understand supply and demand flips, and apply these concepts in the context of market structure, liquidity, and accumulation/distribution phases.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to the lesson on supply and demand, with emphasis on reviewing the trade disclaimer and understanding that the material is educational.
- [00:42] Overview of the utility of supply and demand: when demand exceeds supply, prices tend to rise; when supply exceeds demand, prices tend to fall. Real-world example given with the chip shortage during the 2020-2022 pandemic.
- [01:48] General definition of supply: the total amount of goods or services available at various price levels. The law of supply states that higher prices lead to greater quantity supplied.
- [02:35] General definition of demand: the total quantity consumers are willing to purchase at various price levels. The law of demand states that higher prices lead to lower quantity demanded.
- [03:09] Trading-specific definition of supply: the quantity sellers are willing to sell at different price levels. Supply zones act as resistance areas where selling pressure prevents further price rises.
- [03:48] Trading-specific definition of demand: the quantity buyers are willing to purchase. Demand zones act as support areas where buying pressure prevents further price drops.
- [04:36] Explanation of how supply zones form before downtrends and demand zones form before uptrends. Applicable across all timeframes.
- [05:16] Practical example on the ES chart: identifying valid supply and demand zones through multiple tests of a level (minimum three tests).
- [06:27] Characteristics of supply zones: cluster of price action followed by sharp decline. Example of a retest leading to continuation to the downside.
- [07:28] Characteristics of demand zones: cluster of price action followed by a sharp increase Ñ the opposite dynamic to supply zones.
- [07:51] How traders use these zones for execution:
  - Sell/short at supply zones, anticipating price drops.
  - Buy/long at demand zones, anticipating price rises.
  - Target the opposite zone as a logical take-profit area.
- [08:51] Relationship between supply/demand zones and accumulation/distribution: large participants cannot execute size at once, so they gradually accumulate (demand) or distribute (supply) within price ranges.
- [09:54] Supply and demand zones for trend continuation: retests of these zones can provide entry points aligned with the prevailing trend.
- [10:22] Nasdaq 30-minute example of using a retested supply zone for continuation shorts in a downtrend, with price making successive lower lows.
- [11:55] Using supply/demand to confirm trends:
  - In uptrends: demand zones hold, supply zones break (bullish momentum).
  - In downtrends: supply zones hold, demand zones break (bearish momentum).
- [13:03] Importance of combining supply/demand analysis with market structure to determine whether price is in accumulation or distribution.
- [13:35] Liquidity in supply and demand zones: these are high-activity areas where institutional traders operate, offering optimal execution points for aligning with larger players.
- [14:37] Introduction to supply and demand flips: when a supply zone becomes a demand zone (or vice versa), signaling potential shifts in sentiment and trend reversals.
- [15:34] Supply-to-demand flip: a former resistance zone becomes support after being breached, indicating buying interest has overwhelmed selling pressure.
- [16:17] Demand-to-supply flip: a former support zone becomes resistance after being broken, indicating selling interest has surpassed buying pressure.
- [16:55] Flips viewed through the lens of accumulation and distribution: institutions accumulate after a supply-to-demand flip and distribute after a demand-to-supply flip.
- [17:47] How to identify valid flips:
  - Price breaks a zone and retests it as the opposite role.
  - The previous zone should have offered at least three tests or attempts.
  - High volume on the retest confirms strength.
  - Confirmation via candle closes beyond the zone and value being built on the other side.
- [18:52] Bitcoin 1-hour example: a demand zone flipped into supply after being broken, then that supply zone was later flipped back into demand Ñ illustrating multiple role reversals on the same price level.
- [21:07] Tips and tricks:
  - Conduct extensive backtesting across different assets and timeframes.
  - Analyze multiple timeframes (daily, 4H, 1H, 30M, 5M, 1M) for a complete picture.
  - Train your eyes to spot zones quickly for a more holistic market view.

We encourage reviewing these supply and demand concepts carefully through dedicated backtesting and applying them with sound risk management when integrating them into a trading approach.

# Channels Part 1

## Portal Summary & Highlights

This session provides a detailed walkthrough of trading parallel channels, covering why they help remove directional bias, how to construct them, how to identify valid breakouts versus deviations, and how to manage trades across both day trading and swing trading timeframes. We learn how to use volume as a key confirmation tool, how to position take-profits at the midpoint, and how to apply measured-move targets after a channel breakout, with practical examples including a live Bitcoin case study.

### Highlights

- [00:00] Introduction to parallel channels and why they are valuable for removing directional bias — we trade the chart in front of us rather than a predetermined view.
- [00:38] Explanation of deviations at the range high and range low — price rarely touches the exact top or bottom perfectly, and these overshoots or undershoots (partial rise / partial decline) are important signals.
- [01:17] Rule for a confirmed channel: at least four touches (two on top, two on bottom). Once formed, the channel is traded until it breaks — accepting that one trade will inevitably lose when the structure finally fails.
- [02:01] Pattern bias discussion: although technically neutral, a channel forming after a downward move can be viewed as a potential bear flag with a slight edge toward continuation. The reverse applies after an uptrend.
- [02:52] Volume as a breakout filter:
  - Breakouts on low volume often fail and return into the channel — creating deviation/fakeout entries.
  - Genuine breakouts should occur on increased volume.
  - Volume typically declines inside a channel and expands on the valid break.
- [04:00] Measured move targets: a basic target is a 1:1 projection of the channel's height applied from the breakout point. However, context matters — always cross-reference horizontal levels, Fibonacci extensions, and prior support/resistance before relying on a mechanical 1:1.
- [05:06] Introduction to partial rise (failure to reach the top = weakness) and partial decline (failure to reach the bottom = strength) as early directional clues for the eventual break.
- [06:20] Day trading channels strategy:
  - Enter at the extreme (top or bottom).
  - Take 50% profits at the midpoint.
  - Move stop to break-even after midpoint is reached.
  - If price reclaims the midpoint with a higher low, consider compounding ~30% of the booked profits (not 100%) to maintain locked gains.
- [08:30] Why the midpoint matters: price often rejects there, so booking profits early allows re-entry at the opposite extreme if the move reverses.
- [09:57] Swing trading / long-term channels require a different approach:
  - In extended ranges (days to months), the midpoint becomes a full exit zone, not just a partial take-profit.
  - Use aggressive trailing stops near the midpoint of large channels.
  - Wait for a midpoint reclaim before compounding back in toward the top.
- [12:40] Drawing channel boundaries: use candle closes rather than wick extremes when the closes have produced multiple rejections, giving a more reliable structural line.
- [14:50] Signs that a range is transitioning into a contracting pattern — fewer full touches of the extremes can indicate compression rather than clean range behavior.
- [15:49] Practical application: in a very large range, buy the bottom and close at the midpoint rather than targeting the opposite extreme — this adapts expectations to the structure's true behavior.
- [16:07] Channels are not only sideways — upward-sloping and downward-sloping parallel channels (bull flags and bear flags) are equally valid, with continuation typically favored in the direction of the prior trend.
- [17:00] Early channel identification using only three points (high–low–lower high or low–high–higher low) — trading begins once the third touch shows a reaction, allowing earlier entries than waiting for the full four-touch confirmation.
- [19:40] Stop-loss placement principles:
  - No fixed percentage — each channel is different.
  - Use market structure: prior highs, prior lows, and relevant swing points.
  - Risk-to-reward inside a confirmed range may be acceptable at 1.4–1.5:1, lower than the typical 2:1 standard, due to higher setup probability.
- [23:30] Managing a deviation vs. breakout:
  - Low-volume break outside the channel followed by a reclaim = deviation (trade back toward the opposite side).
  - High-volume break with a close outside = valid breakout — look for a retest of support turned resistance (or vice versa) for entry, with stops back inside the channel.
- [27:00] Example walkthrough combining multiple confluences at a channel top: monthly resistance, 0.786 Fibonacci retracement, and horizontal resistance all aligned, strengthening the short setup.
- [29:40] How to recognize a partial rise during a long trade: if price fails to reach the channel top and loses the midpoint on a lower timeframe, close the long and expect a move back to the bottom.
- [31:00] Breakout projection example: after a confirmed close above the channel, cloning the channel's height and projecting it upward gave a 1:1 measured-move target aligning with a monthly level — a textbook double top projection scenario.

We encourage reviewing these concepts carefully, applying them with disciplined risk management, and testing them in a structured way before committing to live execution.
